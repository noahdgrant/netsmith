# PCB view

## How to get to it

The section "PCB" (key 6). The board is placed as soon as the tab opens (the physical file `<stem>.physical.nsm` beside the source, if there is one, is read with the board: ADR 0048; the layout file `<stem>.pcb.toml`, if there is one, is applied: ADR 0032); "Place and route" runs the router and draws the tracks. "Layers" toggles groups of the drawing.

## Driving it

`scripts/editor-verify drive pcb`; `drive four-layer` after `launch --fixture four_layer`.

1. The tab shows the outline, the footprints with pads in the copper's colour (a muted copper on the front, teal-blue for a part on the back, a tone and a ring for a hole that goes through), and no key (the side panel lists the nets). The drawing's marks are thin and soft (`src/pcbdraw.css`; hairlines are a count of screen pixels, so they stay hairlines at any zoom), a keepout is hatched, a pick glows in the selection's blue. Needs KiCad's footprint libraries (`NETSMITH_KICAD_FOOTPRINTS` or an install netsmith finds, ADR 0021); without them the view says why.
2. Click "Place and route": a chip says `routed: N tracks, M vias` and the button becomes "Route again". An unrouted connection or a placement problem is listed in red.
3. Layers: untick "Silkscreen": the silk groups (`.l-silk`) are hidden.
4. A four-layer board draws each plane as a translucent fill with a dashed border in the tone of its layer (`--pcb-plane-1` to `--pcb-plane-4`: the server's rectangle carries `data-layer="In1.Cu"`, `pcb/planes.ts` turns `In1.Cu` into tone 1, `In2.Cu` into 2, `In3.Cu` into 3 and the layers from the fourth in into 4, and `pcb/drawing.ts` writes `GND · In1.Cu` on the border as `text.plane-label[data-layer]`; a server with no layer attribute leaves the tone to the plane's place among the planes, as round 5 did) and lists it in the Nets tab (`plane In1.Cu`).
5. The "DRC" chip shows the last `fab` result for the board: "run Fab to check" until the build panel has run it.

### A big board: `drive big-pcb` after `launch --fixture big`

The toolbar is in groups and folds by the width of its own view, not the window's (`responsive.css`, a container query): below 1180 px the tool choice and Layers lose their words (the names stay for a screen reader and the tooltips), `took` and the cursor readout go; below 900 px the DRC chip goes; below 640 px only the findings pill is left of the chips; below 560 px it wraps. It is one row at 1280 and at 1000 px. The toolbar is in groups: the primary action and the status pills (the findings pill is `[data-testid=findings-chip]`), the tool choice as a segmented control (Select, Keepout) with undo and redo as icon buttons, then the cursor, the grid, `Layers` (a panel that is also the legend: each layer has a swatch of the board's own colour; `Show all`) and the side panel's toggle. The part's panel is the shared inspector (an eyebrow, the title with the value, a definition list, Rotate, Flip, Lock, Release, and the pads with their nets as chips).

The drawing has no key: the board fills the canvas, and the nets are in the Nets tab of the panel at the side ("Panel" in the toolbar shows or hides it), each with its colour and pad count. The filter narrows the list; hovering a net lights it on the board and dims the rest, a click picks it (a click on a track or via picks its net too; a click on a pad selects its part, which lights its nets). "Place and route" shows the job under the toolbar and in the status bar: the stage (`BigBoard: placed 89 parts on a 70 by 76 mm board`, `routing, try 1 of 3: 12 of 40 connections done`), a clock, and a Cancel button that stops the router and keeps what it routed (the chip then says `stopped`). The editor, and the other tabs, stay usable while it runs; when it ends the toolbar says how long it took. A route started here is kept when another tab is opened meanwhile.

## The canvas: select, move, rotate, flip, lock, undo (ADR 0030 E0 and E1)

`launch --fixture outline_and_pours` (twelve parts, a cutout, a keepout, a physical file that sizes them, and a layout file that places the keepout: the launcher takes the keepout's `.x` and `.y` out of the example's physical file so the recipes can move it); `drive pcb-select` and `drive pcb-edit`. Both start by putting `main.pcb.toml` back as the fixture has it, so they can run in any order.

- **Select**: click a part (its courtyard is the target; the smallest under the pointer wins), Shift or Ctrl+click adds or removes, a drag on empty board draws a box that takes every part it touches, a click on empty board clears, Ctrl+A takes all. The list on the right ("Parts") selects too and zooms to the part. The inspector over the list shows the selected part's place, turn, face, who placed it, its pads with their nets (click a net to light it) and a link to its source line; a double click on a part opens its declaration. The nets of the selection are lit, everything else dims, and the ratsnest lines that end on the selection are drawn thicker.
- **Hover**: a tooltip names the part, its value, kind and package, and for a pad its number and net; over a track or via, the net; over a keepout's edge, what it forbids.
- **Pan and zoom**: the wheel zooms about the pointer, the right or middle button drags the board (the main button belongs to selecting and moving), "Fit" or a double click on the background fits. The grid dots appear when they are far enough apart to read; the cursor's place (mm from the outline's corner) is in the toolbar.
- **Move**: drag a part (or the selection: all the unlocked parts move by the same amount). It snaps to the grid in the toolbar (0.05 mm to 2.54 mm, or none; kept in the browser). The ratsnest follows while dragging. The part's entry is written to `<stem>.pcb.toml` when the button goes up.
- **Keys** (not while typing in a field or the source editor): `R` turns the selection 90 degrees counter-clockwise (`Shift+R` the other way; a selection turns about its middle), `F` puts it on the other face (the footprint is mirrored left to right; a selection mirrors about its middle), `L` locks or unlocks (a locked part has a padlock, does not move on drag, `R` or `F`, and the placer leaves it where it is), `Delete` hands the part back to the placer (its entry is removed), arrows nudge by the grid (Shift: ten times), `Esc` cancels a drag, then the tool, then the selection, `K` is the keepout tool (below). The buttons in the inspector do the same.
- **Undo and redo**: `Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+Shift+Z` or `Ctrl+Y`, and the toolbar buttons, over every edit of the canvas, including keepouts. The history is the page's; it is dropped if the layout file changes under it (the server answers 409).
- **What is written**: only the parts touched get an entry (`at`, `rotation`, `side`, `lock`, `source = "hand"`), the other tables and the other parts' entries are kept, and a part with no entry is placed by the placer as before. Every edit throws away the routed copper: it no longer fits (the chip says so by going back to "placed"; route again).

## Height limits (ADR 0045 section 11): `launch --fixture height_limits`, `drive pcb-height`

The fixture is `scripts/editor-verify-js/fixtures/height_limits.nsm` with its physical file (a lid keepout over the left half that allows 3 mm on the top face; `.tall.height` 9 mm, `.flat.height` 1.5 mm, `.bare` none) and a layout file that puts the three headers side by side under the lid. A keepout that limits height is amber with `max 3 mm` in its corner (`data-height-limit`); the scene has each part's `body` and `height` and each keepout's `maxHeight`.

- **Hover**: a part says `stands 9 mm tall` (or `height not stated`) and, for each limit it stands under, `lid allows 3 mm here: 6 mm too tall` / `fits` / `unchecked`; a keepout's edge says `parts at most 3 mm tall`. The part inspector has a `Height` row, the keepout inspector `Max height`, the keepouts list `max 3 mm tall`.
- **Findings**: picking a finding about a keepout (`too_tall`, `height_unchecked`, `in_keepout`, `pad_in_keepout`) draws that zone's outline in solid amber with `lid: at most 3 mm` (`[data-testid=keepout-lit]`); picking it again puts it out.
- **Drag**: a part with a stated height stops before a step that would put its body over a limit it is too tall for, and a note beside the pointer (`[data-testid=tip]`) says `limited by lid`, the part's height and the limit; the note goes when the drag ends. A part that starts under a limit it is too tall for can be dragged out; a part that fits, or states no height, goes under the lid freely. The board's own zones hold a drag, not one a part carries. Arrow keys, rotate and flip are not held (the finding names what they leave).
- A recipe that drags starts with `Fit`: a drag that starts or ends off screen lands on the side panel.

## Keepouts (ADR 0029, ADR 0030)

`drive pcb-keepout` (fixture `outline_and_pours`). The outline, the cutouts and the keepouts the source declares are drawn (`data-keepout`, `data-cutout`); the outline and the cutouts are not edited. The Keepout button, or `K`, is the rectangle tool: its options (layers, and what it forbids: tracks, vias, copper, parts) are in the toolbar while it is on; drag a rectangle on the board (it snaps to the grid) and it is drawn and written as `[Board.keepouts.keepout1]` with a `size`. A picked keepout has eight handles to resize it by and can be dragged by its border (a drag inside it moves it once it is picked); the inspector changes its layers and what it forbids; `Delete` removes it; undo and redo cover all of it. A keepout the source sizes and does not place (the example's `antenna`) is moved the same way and only its `at` is written; one whose place the source states, or that a part carries, is shown and not moved. The router, the placement check (a part in a keepout that forbids parts is a finding), the placer and KiCad's rule areas obey a drawn keepout like any other.

## Findings (ADR 0030)

`drive pcb-findings`. The chip in the toolbar says how many findings there are (red if any is an error); it opens the Findings tab. Each finding has a rule (`overlap`, `outside_outline`, `in_keepout`... netsmith's; `clearance`, `courtyards_overlap`... KiCad's), a message with the numbers, and its place in mm from the outline's corner. A click zooms to it, marks it on the board and selects the parts it names. netsmith's checks are recomputed after every edit; the connections still to route and netsmith's design rules check of the copper (tag `netsmith`, source `drc`; the rules are KiCad's names: `clearance`, `hole_to_hole`, `copper_edge_clearance`, `silk_over_copper`...) are findings only once the board is routed. "Also ask KiCad" runs KiCad's design rules check as a second opinion (a few seconds, shown as a job; `kicad-cli` is found as `fab` finds it), and its findings stay in the list until the layout changes; without KiCad the tab says so and the netsmith findings stay. The line beside the button (`[data-testid=kicad-state]`, with `data-state` set to what the server reports: `ran`, `unavailable` or `not_run`) is `with KiCad` when the check ran, `KiCad unavailable` (found but the check failed; the red note says why) or `KiCad not run` (found, not asked yet). When the server did not find `kicad-cli` (the project's `kicad` flag is false) the list says so once, as an empty state (`.kicad-missing`: the title `KiCad not found` and the fix, `KICAD_CLI` or install KiCad), and has neither the line nor the red note, asked or not (`KICAD_CLI=/nonexistent` shows it). The words are in `web/src/pcb/kicad.ts` (tested), and the recipe asserts the line against the state the server reports, and that "KiCad not found" appears once when kicad-cli is missing. The check's own answer is kept over a plain findings question that was asked while it ran. netsmith finds `kicad-cli` through `$KICAD_CLI` or `kicad-cli` on `PATH`: on WSL start the editor with `KICAD_CLI="/mnt/c/Program Files/KiCad/9.0/bin/kicad-cli.exe"` (the Windows build runs headless).

### Constraint findings (ADR 0045): `launch --fixture constrained`, `drive pcb-constraints`

The fixture is `scripts/editor-verify-js/fixtures/constrained.nsm` with its physical file (the requirement) and a layout file beside it that puts the header by hand where its `.place` requirement does not allow. The finding has the rule `constraint`, is an error (as `fab` makes it) and says the requirement's line, the part, the drawing measured (`F.Fab`), the measured value, the window and how far over; the picked finding shows all of it (a row is cut after six lines). A click zooms, marks and selects the part; Release (the placer keeps to the requirement) clears it and undo brings it back with the same numbers. The windows and the drag clamp of a requirement are not drawn yet (ADR 0045 C4); the drag limit of a height zone is (below).

## Panels (ADR 0040)

`drive panel` (fixture `led_panel`). A `panel` is listed in the tree with the badge `N`; a click on it opens the PCB view of the board it repeats with the panel drawn instead of the board (`data-testid="panel-preview"`): the boards outlined and named (`LedTile 1` ... `LedTile 6`), the cut lines dashed, the rails with their tooling holes and fiducials, and the panel checks beneath (errors and advice, from `GET /api/panel?name=`). It is read only: the grid is in the source. "Show the board" returns to the board; a board that a panel repeats has a "Panel <name>" button in its toolbar.

## Gotchas

- The drawing the canvas gets is `PcbScene.svg` from `GET /api/pcb/scene?board=`, with the part boxes and pads beside it as data; `POST /api/pcb/edit` is the only way a position changes. Both are typed in `api.rs`.
- `GET /api/pcb/3d?board=` is the same placement as glTF 2.0 binary (`model/gltf-binary`, the bytes `netsmith board3d --glb` writes for an unrouted board; contract in `docs/BOARD3D.md`), with the placement's layout stamp in `X-Netsmith-Layout-Stamp`, equal to `PcbScene.layoutStamp`. To check one by hand: `curl -s -o b.glb 'http://127.0.0.1:<port>/api/pcb/3d?board=<name>'`.
- Recipes click where the scene says (`pcb-helpers.mjs` turns a board position into a window position with the drawing's `getScreenCTM`); a hover or a click that lands on the ratsnest or the reference text would miss the pad, so those layers have `pointer-events: none`.
- Recipes name a part by its instance path (`PART` in `pcb-helpers.mjs`: `stage.output_cap`) and read its designator from the scene (`labelOf`), never write `C1` or `U7`: designators follow the kind of the part and the placer and have changed before. The placer also chooses the turn a part starts with, so a recipe checks a quarter turn against the turn it read, not against 0.
- The server is the binary: after changing the page, `cargo build -p netsmith`, then `cleanup` and `launch` again, or the recipe drives the old page.
- A flip or a first entry changes what the derived placer is told (`place_options`: the faces of the parts with entries), so the other, derived parts may move too. That is the placer's, not the editor's.

- Every kind of drawing is in a group `l-...` (see the head of `crates/netsmith-pcb/src/svg.rs`); add new drawings to a group or the toggles miss them.
- The drawing the page gets is `svg::render_view` (no key); `fab` still writes `svg::render` with the key.
- Routing is per board and is forgotten when the files are built again.
- A board that cannot be placed because of the project's own files (a part with no footprint mapping, a footprint that lacks a pad the symbol uses, a size that does not fit) is a `422` from `/api/pcb/scene`. The page's reads send `Accept: application/json`, so the body is `{code, message, ...}` (`footprint_lacks_pad`, `no_kicad_mapping`, ...; `ApiFailure.code` in `client.ts`), and the message is what the "The board could not be placed" state shows under its title; a fault of the server is a `500` with plain text. `carrier_and_cards` has invented parts with no KiCad symbol: its boards show that state, not a drawing.

## Round 6

- A board opens fitted to the window and no larger than **300 %** (100 % is 12 px to the millimetre; `placeView` in `pcb/Canvas.tsx`; Fit does the same), on a canvas of faint dots that moves and scales with it.
- A **reference** is small beside the pads: its size is the drawing's (0.46 mm) held between 10 and 13 screen pixels (`--pxl`, the size of a screen pixel in the drawing's units with its unit); a **plane label** is smaller still, inside the top left corner of its plane, above the parts (a group of its own, `.l-plane-labels`), with a halo, and the labels of planes that share a corner are stacked a line apart. A label that a part or a designator lies under is moved (round 7, `pcb/planeLabels.ts`, run when the drawing or the zoom changes): the four corners of its plane in turn, then a line or two further in, then along the top and bottom edge every 2.5 mm, the first where it meets no part, designator or earlier label at the zoom shown; with none free it is hidden (`plane-label-off`) and comes back when the zoom makes room. `four-layer` asserts that no visible label lies on a part at six zooms.
- The toolbar has the **Changes vs** switch; the marks are a 2 px outline with a faint tint over the part's courtyard, outside the pick's outline (no glow).

## Plane labels that find no room: the chip `+N` (round 8, then round 9)

`pcb/planeLabels.ts`: `placeAll` puts each plane's label at the first of its places (the four corners, a line or two further in, along the edges) where it meets no part and no label placed before it, so two labels never meet; one with no place is hidden (`plane-label-off`) and `fitPlaneLabels` hands the hidden planes back (`HiddenPlane`: the words of the label, net, layer, tone, the plane's box). Round 8 stood a tag `+N` in the first free place of the drawing, with a tooltip; at a zoom where a board is a few pixels long that was two characters of SVG text over it, with no way to reach the names by keyboard.

Round 9 (`pcb/PlaneMore.tsx`, `drive pcb-plane-more` on `four_layer`): the tag is a **chip** pinned to the bottom left corner of the canvas (`PanZoom`'s `overlay`, so it neither moves nor scales with the drawing: the same size and as readable at every zoom, and clear of the zoom bar), a button `[data-testid=plane-more]` with the layers icon and `+N`, named `2 planes have no room for a label at this zoom: GND · In1.Cu, 3V3 · In2.Cu. Show the list` (`aria-haspopup=menu`, `aria-expanded`). It opens a menu (`plane-more-list`, opening upward): a row for each hidden plane with a swatch in its layer's tone (`--pcb-plane-1` to `-4`) and `Zoom to it`. Pointing at a row or focusing it outlines that plane on the board (`rect.plane-peek`); choosing one (click, Enter) zooms the board to the plane, which makes room for its label; ArrowDown, ArrowUp, Home and End move (and stop the canvas from nudging the picked parts with the same arrows), Escape closes and gives the focus back to the chip, Tab closes. With the Planes layer switched off the labels go with the planes (`.l-plane-labels` is hidden by the same rule) and no chip is shown: a plane that is not drawn has no label to find room for. `four-layer` still checks that no two labels meet at any zoom and that the chip counts the hidden ones; nothing is drawn in the SVG for it (`.plane-label-more` is gone). A hint `Zoom to fit` for a small board in a large canvas was considered and left out: a board that small is already at Fit (the zoom is capped at 300 %), so the hint would say something that does nothing.
