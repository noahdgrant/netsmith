# PCB view

## How to get to it

The section "PCB" (key 6). The board is placed as soon as the tab opens (the physical file `<stem>.physical.nsm` beside the source, if there is one, is read with the board: ADR 0048; the layout file `<stem>.pcb.toml`, if there is one, is applied: ADR 0032); "Place and route" runs the router and draws the tracks. "Layers" toggles groups of the drawing.

## Driving it

`scripts/editor-verify drive pcb`; `drive four-layer` after `launch --fixture four_layer`.

1. The tab shows the outline, the footprints with pads in the copper's colour (a muted copper on the front, teal-blue for a part on the back, a tone and a ring for a hole that goes through), and no key (the side panel lists the nets). The drawing's marks are thin and soft (`src/pcbdraw.css`; hairlines are a count of screen pixels, so they stay hairlines at any zoom), a keepout is hatched, a pick glows in the selection's blue. Needs KiCad's footprint libraries (`NETSMITH_KICAD_FOOTPRINTS` or an install netsmith finds, ADR 0021); without them the view says why.
2. Click "Place and route": a chip says `routed: N tracks, M vias` and the button becomes "Route again". An unrouted connection or a placement problem is listed in red.
3. Layers: untick "Silkscreen": the silk groups (`.l-silk`) are hidden.
4. A four-layer board draws each plane as a translucent fill with a dashed border in the tone of its layer (`--pcb-plane-1` to `--pcb-plane-4`: the server's rectangle carries `data-layer="In1.Cu"`, `pcb/planes.ts` turns `In1.Cu` into tone 1, `In2.Cu` into 2, `In3.Cu` into 3 and the layers from the fourth in into 4, and `pcb/drawing.ts` writes `GND · In1.Cu` on the border as `text.plane-label[data-layer]`; a server with no layer attribute leaves the tone to the plane's place among the planes, as round 5 did) and lists it in the Nets tab (`plane In1.Cu`).
5. The "DRC" chip shows the last `fab` result for the board: "run Fab to check" until the build panel has run it.
6. `2D | 3D` in the toolbar (key `D`) switches to the board in 3D: `pcb-3d.md`.

### A big board: `drive big-pcb` after `launch --fixture big`

The toolbar is in groups and folds by the width of its own view, not the window's (`responsive.css`, a container query): below 1180 px the tool choice and Layers lose their words (the names stay for a screen reader and the tooltips), `took` and the cursor readout go; below 900 px the DRC chip goes; below 640 px only the findings pill is left of the chips; below 560 px it wraps. It is one row at 1280 and at 1000 px. The toolbar is in groups: the primary action and the status pills (the findings pill is `[data-testid=findings-chip]`), the tool choice as a segmented control (Select, Keepout) with undo and redo as icon buttons, then the cursor, the grid, `Layers` (a panel that is also the legend: each layer has a swatch of the board's own colour; `Show all`) and the side panel's toggle. The part's panel is the shared inspector (an eyebrow, the title with the value, a definition list, Rotate, Flip, Lock, Release, and the pads with their nets as chips).

The drawing has no key: the board fills the canvas, and the nets are in the Nets tab of the panel at the side ("Panel" in the toolbar shows or hides it), each with its colour and pad count. The filter narrows the list; hovering a net lights it on the board and dims the rest, a click picks it (a click on a track or via picks its net too; a click on a pad selects its part, which lights its nets). "Place and route" shows the job under the toolbar and in the status bar: the stage (`BigBoard: placed 89 parts on a 70 by 76 mm board`, `routing, try 1 of 3: 12 of 40 connections done`), a clock, and a Cancel button that stops the router and keeps what it routed (the chip then says `stopped`). The editor, and the other tabs, stay usable while it runs; when it ends the toolbar says how long it took. A route started here is kept when another tab is opened meanwhile.

## Watching the placer and the router (issue #764): `launch --fixture outline_and_pours`, `drive pcb-live`

While the server places a board for the view (`GET /api/pcb/scene`), the view draws the parts where the placer has them (`[data-testid=pcb-live]`, `data-parts`; each `.live-part` the box round its courtyard, `.front` or `.back`, in the `.live-region` it places them in), the placer's last line (`[data-testid=placing-line]`, the socket's `progress` with the task `place`) and **Cancel** (`[data-testid=cancel-placing]`, `POST /api/pcb/place/cancel`). A cancelled placing answers 499 and the view offers **Place again** (`[data-testid=place-again]`). While the board is routed, the tracks laid so far are drawn over it (`.pcb-host [data-testid=pcb-live-tracks]`, `data-tracks`) and go when the route answers. `.pcb-view` counts the glimpses the page was shown (`data-live-place`, `data-live-route`). A run in a terminal with `--to-editor=<port>` shows in the status bar (`[data-testid=outside-run]`, `data-ended` once it has ended; a click closes it). The server keeps a board it placed until a file changes, so a recipe that wants a placing writes the layout file again first; it holds the scene with a `gate()` (`route.fetch()` first, so the server places and the glimpses come, then the answer waits).

## The canvas: select, move, rotate, flip, lock, undo (ADR 0030 E0 and E1)

`launch --fixture outline_and_pours` (twelve parts, a cutout, a keepout, a physical file that sizes them, and a layout file that places the keepout: the launcher takes the keepout's `.x` and `.y` out of the example's physical file so the recipes can move it); `drive pcb-select` and `drive pcb-edit`. Both start by putting `main.pcb.toml` back as the fixture has it, so they can run in any order.

- **Select**: click a part (its courtyard is the target; the smallest under the pointer wins), Shift or Ctrl+click adds or removes, a drag on empty board draws a box that takes every part it touches, a click on empty board clears, Ctrl+A takes all. The list on the right ("Parts") selects too and zooms to the part. The inspector over the list shows the selected part's place, turn, face, who placed it, its pads with their nets (click a net to light it) and a link to its source line; a double click on a part opens its declaration. The nets of the selection are lit, everything else dims, and the ratsnest lines that end on the selection are drawn thicker.
- **Hover**: a tooltip names the part, its value, kind and package, and for a pad its number and net; over a track or via, the net; over a keepout's edge, what it forbids.
- **Pan and zoom**: the wheel zooms about the pointer, the right or middle button drags the board (the main button belongs to selecting and moving), "Fit" or a double click on the background fits. The grid dots appear when they are far enough apart to read; the cursor's place (mm from the outline's corner) is in the toolbar.
- **Move**: drag a part (or the selection: all the unlocked parts move by the same amount). It snaps to the grid in the toolbar (0.05 mm to 2.54 mm, or none; kept in the browser). The ratsnest follows while dragging. The part's entry is written to `<stem>.pcb.toml` when the button goes up.
- **Keys** (not while typing in a field or the source editor): `R` turns the selection 90 degrees counter-clockwise (`Shift+R` the other way; a selection turns about its middle), `F` puts it on the other face (the footprint is mirrored left to right; a selection mirrors about its middle), `L` locks or unlocks (a locked part has a padlock, does not move on drag, `R` or `F`, and the placer leaves it where it is), `Delete` hands the part back to the placer (its entry is removed), arrows nudge by the grid (Shift: ten times), `Esc` cancels a drag, then the tool, then the selection, `K` is the keepout tool (below). The buttons in the inspector do the same.
- **Any turn**: the part inspector's `Turn` row is a number field (`[data-testid=part-turn]`) for an unlocked part: an angle typed and Enter (or leaving the field) turns the selection to it, about each part's own origin, and writes it to the layout file; any angle, not only the 90-degree steps of `R` and Rotate (issue #996). A locked part shows its turn only. `drive pcb-edit` types 45.
- **Undo and redo**: `Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+Shift+Z` or `Ctrl+Y`, and the toolbar buttons, over every edit of the canvas, including keepouts. The history is the page's; it is dropped if the layout file or the board's physical file changes under it (the server answers 409: `PcbScene.layoutStamp` covers both files, so an edit of the physical file alone moves it too).
- **What is written**: only the parts touched get an entry (`at`, `rotation`, `side`, `lock`, `source = "hand"`), the other tables and the other parts' entries are kept, and a part with no entry is placed by the placer as before. An edit throws away the routed copper (the chip goes back to "placed"); the next route keeps the copper of the nets the edit left alone and routes only the rest, and an edit back to a placement routed before (an undo) shows its copper again at once (issue #825).

## Regenerate one module (issue #825): `launch --fixture lights`, `drive pcb-regenerate`

The fixture is three modules (`a`, `b`, `c`) of a resistor and an LED on one rail. Pick a part of a module: the inspector offers **Regenerate a** (`[data-testid=regenerate]`; the innermost module that holds every part picked). The server places that module again with every other part held (`POST /api/pcb/regenerate`) and answers with a proposal, drawn over the board: the region as a dashed box (`.regen-region`) and each part at its new place as a filled box in the accent colour (`[data-testid=regen-ghost]`, `data-regen-part`). The card at the top of the side panel (`[data-testid=regen-card]`) lists the parts placed again, those held, the nets routed again, the routes file records that no longer match, the totals before and after, and the findings that appear or go. Nothing is written. **Another** asks for the next seed, **Reject** drops it, **Accept** sends its changes as one edit (`source = "regen"`): one step of undo. Route after it: the toast says `kept the copper of 2 nets, routed 3 again`. Undo: the old places and the old copper are back with no router, and the toolbar says `routed`. The side panel's **Modules (3)** tab is the tree of the board's modules (`[data-testid=module-tree]`, one `.module-row` per module with `data-module` its path, nested by `aria-level`, built by `moduleRows` in `pcb/regenerate.ts` as the server's `module_paths` reads them): a click on a row picks the parts of that module, and its button (`[data-testid=regen-module]`) proposes that module; the row of the module proposed has the class `proposed`.

## Modules lit (issue #1129): `launch --fixture lights`, `drive pcb-modules-lit`

All the parts of a module lit in the 2D and 3D views, each module in its own colour (the theme's `--module-1` to `--module-6`; the store is `pcb/modules.ts`). In the Modules tab the pointer on a row lights its module while it is there (the shared hover, `kind: 'module'`), and a click keeps it lit, picks its parts and makes the module the page's selection; Ctrl or Shift and a click adds a module, a click on one lit takes it off, and `[data-testid=modules-clear]` puts them all out. A lit row has `data-lit` (its colour's number). On the 2D board each module is a `.pcb-overlay .module-group` (`data-module`, `data-slot`, `hovered` while only hovered) of `.module-part` boxes and a dashed `.module-box`; the svg has `has-mod` and the parts of no module lit are dimmed (`g[data-path]` without `mod-lit`). In 3D, `view3d.moduleTints()` lists the parts tinted with their colours. The part inspector's `[data-testid=light-module-btn]` lights the part's module (`[data-testid=light-module-which]` chooses one around it). A card of the schematic's overview picks its module, which is lit on the board. `GET /api/view` has `modules` (`path`, `colour`, `parts`) and `module:<path>` in `highlight`; `view editor-3d --highlight module:a,module:b` lights two, and `--select module:c` picks one.

## Height limits (ADR 0045 section 11): `launch --fixture height_limits`, `drive pcb-height`

The fixture is `scripts/editor-verify-js/fixtures/height_limits.nsm` with its physical file (a lid keepout over the left half that allows 3 mm on the top face; `.tall.height` 9 mm, `.flat.height` 1.5 mm, `.bare` none) and a layout file that puts the three headers side by side under the lid. A keepout that limits height is an amber outline with no fill (`data-limit-only`) and its limit and face in its corner, `3 mm, top` (`data-height-limit`); the scene has each part's `body` and `height` and each keepout's `maxHeight`.

- **Hover**: a part says `stands 9 mm tall` (or `height not stated`) and, for each limit it stands under, `lid allows 3 mm here: 6 mm too tall` / `fits` / `unchecked`; a keepout's edge says `parts at most 3 mm tall`. The part inspector has a `Height` row, the keepout inspector `Max height`, the keepouts list `max 3 mm tall`.
- **Findings**: picking a finding about a keepout (`too_tall`, `height_unchecked`, `in_keepout`, `pad_in_keepout`) draws that zone's outline in solid amber with `lid: at most 3 mm` (`[data-testid=keepout-lit]`); picking it again puts it out.
- **Drag**: a part with a stated height stops before a step that would put its body over a limit it is too tall for, and a note beside the pointer (`[data-testid=tip]`) says `limited by lid`, the part's height and the limit; the note goes when the drag ends. A part that starts under a limit it is too tall for can be dragged out; a part that fits, or states no height, goes under the lid freely. The board's own zones hold a drag, not one a part carries. Arrow keys, rotate and flip are not held (the finding names what they leave).
- A recipe that drags starts with `Fit`: a drag that starts or ends off screen lands on the side panel.

## The 2D | 3D switch stays put (issue #784)

`drive pcb-toggle-fixed` (fixture `two_boards`): the `2D | 3D` switch is the first thing of the toolbar, at the same place (the recipe compares its box) before and after a route, with the keepout tool's options shown, on the back side, in 3D (which hides the Side, Tool and grid controls) and back; the status group stays on one line (`white-space: nowrap`), as a wrapped chip makes the bar taller and moves everything.

## Keepouts, height limits and the side looked at (issue #770)

`drive pcb-sides` (fixture `enclosure_limits`: an enclosure's seven height-only limits over the whole board, on both faces, one keepout that forbids parts, a header on the back and two on the front). The Layers menu has **Keepouts** and **Height limits**, apart (the keepouts are the zones that forbid something, hatched; the limits are `data-limit-only` rectangles and the `data-height-limit` labels, also those of a keepout that forbids and limits). A height limit is an outline with no fill, dotted, with `3.9 mm, bottom` at its corner; limits that start at one corner have their labels a line apart (`stackedLabels`, `web/src/pcb/drawing.ts`), so seven overlapping limits leave the parts under them readable. The server gives each zone `data-layers` (its copper layers, space separated) for the side switch.

The **Side** switch in the toolbar (`[data-testid=pcb-side-front|back|both]`, key `B` goes front, back, both) says which face the view looks at. *Both* (the default) is the board as the file has it; *Front* draws the zones of the top layer and dims the back (its parts and copper, at 22 %: still there to be picked); *Back* draws the board seen from the back, mirrored left to right as KiCad's flipped view is (the `.pcb-stage` has `transform: scaleX(-1)`, and every text is turned back about its middle so it reads, `pcbdraw.css`), the zones of the bottom layer, the front dimmed. A zone of inner layers alone is drawn in *Both* only. The picks, the drags and the arrow keys follow the picture (the left arrow moves a part toward the left of the screen). The view moves with the board when it is turned over. A part on the back is `back` in the part list (`[data-testid=side-tag]`) and `Face back` in the inspector.

## Keepouts (ADR 0029, ADR 0030)

`drive pcb-keepout` (fixture `outline_and_pours`). The outline, the cutouts and the keepouts the source declares are drawn (`data-keepout`, `data-cutout`); the outline and the cutouts are not edited. The Keepout button, or `K`, is the rectangle tool: its options (layers, and what it forbids: tracks, vias, copper, parts) are in the toolbar while it is on; drag a rectangle on the board (it snaps to the grid) and it is drawn and written to the board's physical file as `.keepout1: Keepout { .x &= ... }` at the end of the unit (a keepout of the physical file in that shape is moved, resized and deleted the same way, in place; one drawn in the layout file by an earlier editor is an error that gives the member to write in the physical file). A picked keepout has eight handles to resize it by and can be dragged by its border (a drag inside it moves it once it is picked); the inspector changes its layers and what it forbids; `Delete` removes it; undo and redo cover all of it. A keepout the source sizes and does not place (the example's `antenna`) is moved the same way and only its `at` is written; one the physical file states in another shape, with its place and size stated once each as numbers (a `.max_height`, a sum, micrometres), is moved and resized the same way and only those numbers are rewritten (tag `physical`; its layers and what it forbids are not changed, and it is not deleted); one whose place is computed from another member, or that a part carries, is shown and not moved. The router, the placement check (a part in a keepout that forbids parts is a finding), the placer and KiCad's rule areas obey a drawn keepout like any other.

## Summary (issue 1003)

`drive pcb-summary`. The Summary tab of the side panel is `netsmith layout summary` as numbers (`GET /api/pcb/summary`, `PcbSummary`): the totals (wire, ratsnest, crossings, decoupling mean and max, `[data-testid=summary-totals]`), the parts near the selected parts within a distance (`summary-near`, `summary-within`), the distance between two pads typed as `R1.1` and `U1.3` (`summary-pad-a`, `summary-pad-b`, `summary-measure`; the line says centre to centre and copper to copper and the nets, `summary-pads`; a part or pad the scene does not have is refused by the page with the pads the part has, `summary-refused`, and sends no request; `summary-clear` takes the pairs off), each face's fill and free area with its three largest free rectangles (a click zooms to one), each module's box and intruders, each decoupling capacitor's distance (a click selects it) and the longest nets. It is asked again when the placement (the layout stamp), the selection, the distance or the pairs change. The words and the box arithmetic are in `web/src/pcb/summary.ts` (tested).

## Where a part fits (issue 1002)

`GET /api/pcb/fit?board=&part=` (`near=`, `step=`, `side=`; `PcbFit`, `client.pcbFit`) is `netsmith layout fit --format json` for the placement the scene draws: poses with `fits`, the nearest place first, and what is in the way where none fits. It has no control in the page yet; the tests are `editor_fit` of netsmith-workspace and the route's test in `crates/netsmith-editor/tests/server.rs`. Nothing is written.

## Findings (ADR 0030)

`drive pcb-findings`. The chip in the toolbar says how many findings there are (red if any is an error); it opens the Findings tab. Each finding has a rule (`overlap`, `outside_outline`, `in_keepout`... netsmith's; `clearance`, `courtyards_overlap`... KiCad's), a message with the numbers, and its place in mm from the outline's corner. A click zooms to it, marks it on the board and selects the parts it names. The toolbar's "Download KiCad board" (`[data-testid=pcb-kicad]`, a link to `GET /api/pcb/kicad?board=`, issue #1001) downloads the board as it is placed and not routed, with these findings, as `<board>.placed-kicad.zip` (the `.kicad_pcb`, the `.kicad_pro` and the findings list, what `netsmith layout export` writes); it works while the placement has findings or the layout file is refused, and `drive pcb-findings` checks the zip. netsmith's checks are recomputed after every edit; the connections still to route and netsmith's design rules check of the copper (tag `netsmith`, source `drc`; the rules are KiCad's names: `clearance`, `hole_to_hole`, `copper_edge_clearance`, `silk_over_copper`...) are findings only once the board is routed. "Also ask KiCad" runs KiCad's design rules check as a second opinion (a few seconds, shown as a job; `kicad-cli` is found as `fab` finds it), and its findings stay in the list until the layout changes; without KiCad the tab says so and the netsmith findings stay. The line beside the button (`[data-testid=kicad-state]`, with `data-state` set to what the server reports: `ran`, `unavailable` or `not_run`) is `with KiCad` when the check ran, `KiCad unavailable` (found but the check failed; the red note says why) or `KiCad not run` (found, not asked yet). When the server did not find `kicad-cli` (the project's `kicad` flag is false) the list says so once, as an empty state (`.kicad-missing`: the title `KiCad not found` and the fix, `KICAD_CLI` or install KiCad), and has neither the line nor the red note, asked or not (`KICAD_CLI=/nonexistent` shows it). The words are in `web/src/pcb/kicad.ts` (tested), and the recipe asserts the line against the state the server reports, and that "KiCad not found" appears once when kicad-cli is missing. The check's own answer is kept over a plain findings question that was asked while it ran. netsmith finds `kicad-cli` through `$KICAD_CLI` or `kicad-cli` (or `kicad-cli.exe`) on `PATH`: on WSL start the editor with `KICAD_CLI="/mnt/c/Program Files/KiCad/9.0/bin/kicad-cli.exe"` (the Windows build runs headless).

### Constraint findings (ADR 0045): `launch --fixture constrained`, `drive pcb-constraints`

The fixture is `scripts/editor-verify-js/fixtures/constrained.nsm` with its physical file (the requirement) and a layout file beside it that puts the header by hand where its `.place` requirement does not allow. The finding has the rule `constraint`, is an error (as `fab` makes it) and says the requirement's line, the part, the drawing measured (`F.Fab`), the measured value, the window and how far over; the picked finding shows all of it (a row is cut after six lines). A click zooms, marks and selects the part; Release (the placer keeps to the requirement) clears it and undo brings it back with the same numbers. `drive pcb-windows` (same fixture) checks the rest of ADR 0045 C4 and C5: each part's window (`[data-req-part] .req-window`, lit as `.req.on` for a picked or dragged part) and the ghost of a part outside it (`[data-testid=req-ghost]`), the hover's and the inspector's list of requirements (`[data-testid=requirement-list]`), a drag that stops at the window's edge with the note `limited by power.place.left (...)`, and "Require here" (`[data-testid=require-here]`, disabled with the line to change in its title for a part whose place the file states as a window), which writes `.aux.place.x &= ...` and the rest to the physical file; undo takes them out again. The words and the clamp are `web/src/pcb/windows.ts` (tested); the server's side is `netsmith-workspace/src/required.rs`. The recipe writes the fixture's physical file back first.

## The routes file (epic #599): `launch --fixture routed_led`, `drive pcb-routes`

The fixture is `scripts/editor-verify-js/fixtures/routed_led.nsm` (the LED example) with a layout file and a routes file that `netsmith routes import` wrote from its own fab, then shaped with the `netsmith routes` commands: GND locked with a via added, the anode net unlocked with track 2 locked, VCC's record deleted (the router routes it). The launcher copies `x.routes.toml` beside a fixture as `src/main.routes.toml`; the recipe puts the fixture's file back first.

- **Drawn**: the routes file's copper is on the board before the router's (the server applies it as `fab` does). Over each of its tracks the overlay draws a line along the track and round each via a ring (`[data-testid=fixed-copper]`, `.fixed-shape[data-route-net][data-route-track|data-route-via]`), in the text's colour so it reads on the copper of either face in every theme: solid on a locked net (`.net-locked`: the router adds nothing to it), dashed on a net the router may still add to. A locked shape has a small padlock. The router's own copper has no mark. A Layers toggle hides the marks of its layer too.
- **Routes tab** (side panel, shown when the routes file has records for the board): each record with its tracks and vias numbered as `netsmith routes show` numbers them, `lock` on a locked net or shape, `stale` on a record whose pads moved and `not drawn` on its shapes that are not on the board. A click picks one and zooms to it.
- **Pick and edit**: a click on a marked shape picks it (lit in the selection's blue; the inspector shows its layer, width, ends, lock). `L` (or the inspector's Lock) locks or unlocks the picked shape, or the picked net; `Lock net` locks the shape's whole net; `Delete` deletes the shape, or the net's record. The server makes each change with `netsmith_fabricate::routes::edit`, the code of `netsmith routes lock`, `unlock` and `delete` (`POST /api/pcb/routes`), writes the routes file, places the board again (unrouted) and the toast says what it did. A refused change (a locked shape, a shape the record does not have, a new error of the design rules check when a net is locked again) says why in a toast and writes nothing; a routes file changed on disk since the page read it is a 409 and the board is read again.
- **Draw and move** (`drive pcb-routes-draw`): the toolbar's Track (`X`) and Via (`V`) tools show when the board has a layout file (the routes file goes beside it). Track: a click on a pad, a fixed track's end or a via starts a track of its net (`[data-testid=track-draft][data-net][data-points]`, drawn at the net's width from `scene.netWidths` in the layer's colour with a dashed line in the selection's blue); each click adds a corner, on a pad, end or via of the net within reach, else on the grid; a double-click or Enter ends it, Backspace takes the last corner off, Esc drops it. The layer is the toolbar's `[data-testid=track-layer]`: `scene.trackLayers`, top, the process's inner signal layers and bottom (`drive pcb-routes-inner`, fixture `six_layer`: a track on `inner2` at the net's inner width, `scene.netWidths[].inner_width`, its draft in the inner tone, `.track-draft.layer-inner`); a plane is not offered. The track is one edit, a straight track for each segment (`add_track`, the code of `routes add-track`); a refused one (the design rules check's errors in the toast) comes back as a draft to change. Via: a click places the process's via (`scene.viaDiameter`, `viaDrill`) on the net under the pointer, on an anchor of it or the grid; a ring (`[data-testid=via-ghost]`) shows where. Select: a press at a straight track's end, or on a via, and a drag moves that place with every track end and via of the net there (`.fixed-net.dragging` while it moves; one edit of `move_track`/`move_via`); an arc's end and a locked shape are refused with the reason. A board with no routes file gets one with the first track, with the net's pad hash as `routes add-track` writes it.
- **Edited from the CLI** (`drive pcb-routes-cli`): the server polls the routes file with the layout file and pushes `rebuilt`, so a `netsmith routes` command run in the project (the recipe runs it with `execFile`, cwd the project) shows in the open page with no reload. A change by another program drops the placed board, so the scene comes back unrouted (`routed` false, no `routed` chip, the router's copper gone from the drawing) until the next Place and route, as after an edit made in the editor. `netsmith routes` takes mm from the outline's top left corner; the scene's places carry `originX`/`originY` on top. A command the CLI refuses (a locked shape, an unknown net, a design rules error) exits 1 and leaves the file, its stamp and the page as they were. The editor's own write carries the stamp of the file as the page read it: one held on its way (`page.route`, the CLI run in the handler, then `route.continue()`) while the CLI writes is answered 409 `changed on disk since the board was read`, the page fetches the scene again, and its steps of undo stay. The browser logs the 409 (`allowConsole`). The recipe prints how long the page took to show the CLI's track, and asserts nothing about it.
- **Walkaround** (`drive pcb-routes-walk`, issue #726): while a track is drawn, the page asks the server (`POST /api/pcb/walk`, one request at a time, the newest pointer place first, so the page is busy until the answer is in) for the way from the track's last corner to the pointer round the copper there, and the draft shows it: `[data-testid=track-draft][data-walk]` is `asked` (no answer yet), `path` (`data-walk-corners` is the number of corners of its own the server chose, hollow dots; 0 is the straight segment) or `refused` (the segment is red and a tip, "No way there", says why and names the obstacle: what an end is too near, or what the straight way runs into, as `pad 1 of D1 (net GND)`, `a track of net SDA`, `the board edge`, `keepout USB`). A click fixes the corners the walkaround chose as ordinary corners of the draft (`data-points` counts them); where there is no way the click is refused with the reason in a toast and adds nothing. The board it checks against is the one the routes file's check reads (placed, with the routes file's copper, not the router's), in the layer picked, at the net's width, and a corner turns by 45 degrees at most from the segment before. The track is still ended through `routes::edit_all`, whose check has the last word.
- **The router's copper gives way** (`drive pcb-routes-shove`, issue #726, second slice; fixture `routed_led`, route first with the toolbar's Place and route): the router's own copper is no obstacle to the walkaround, since an edit of the routes file throws it away and the board is made again from the file, unrouted. The walk's answer says which tracks and vias of the router the track (the draft so far and the segment) crosses (`crossed`); the canvas draws them faded inside an amber halo (`[data-testid=router-shoved][data-tracks][data-vias]`, `[data-net]` groups inside it) and a note at the bottom left of the board says `Crosses 1 router track` (`[data-testid=track-crossed]`). The marks stay while the pointer rests on the corner just clicked (the draft remembers the last answer's `crossed`; Backspace forgets it until the pointer moves). Enter writes only the drawn track; the toast then adds "the router's copper was discarded: route again to lay it round the new copper", the drawing loses the router's copper (routes-file copper is drawn in the same layers, so count a net the file has no record of, as the recipe counts VCC), and Place and route lays it round the new track. Routes-file copper that is locked is still an obstacle and never moves; unlocked copper is shoved (next bullet).
- **Push and shove** (`drive pcb-routes-push`, issue #726, third slice; fixture `routed_led`, the recipe writes its own routes file): with the toolbar's mode `[data-testid=track-shove]` on `shove copper aside` (the default; `go round only` is the walkaround alone), the walk (`POST /api/pcb/walk` with `shove: true`) finds the way through the unlocked tracks and vias of the routes file, then moves those that keep a space of the design rules check from the whole track out of its way (`netsmith_router::shove`): the answer's `shoved` has each moved shape with where it was and where it goes, and `stuck` says which shape could not move far enough (the walk then goes round all of it). The canvas draws the new places in the layer's colour inside a blue halo and the old ones faded (`[data-testid=shove-preview][data-tracks][data-vias]`, `.shove-track[data-net][data-track]` inside it) and a note at the bottom left says `Shoves 1 track` (`[data-testid=track-shoved]`) or, when a shove failed, `Goes round, nothing shoved` and the shape (`[data-testid=track-stuck]`). A locked track, a track of a locked net, a pad, a keepout, a zone and the edge never move. An end on a pad stays and the track turns about it; an end that meets another track of its net slides along that track, so corners keep their angles. Enter writes the drawn track and the moves as one edit of two or more nets (`move_track`/`move_via` after the `add_track`s, one check of the design rules, one 409 check): the toast says "added a track to net VCC; moved track 1 of net GND". The edit's answer has an `undo` for each net it changed, and one Ctrl+Z puts all the records back (one Ctrl+Shift+Z makes them again). An arc is shoved whole (`mid` in the `move_track`) and the end of a track on the body of a moved track (a tee) rides it. Gotcha: a track that runs exactly vertical or horizontal has a bounding box of no width, so wait for `.fixed-shape` as `attached`, not visible, as the recipe does.
- **A via, a drag and a layer change shove too** (`drive pcb-routes-push-more`, issue #1009; fixture `routed_led`, the recipe writes its own routes file): the via tool under the pointer and a drag of a track's end or a via in Select ask `POST /api/pcb/shove` (the via, or the shapes the drag moves and which they are, `moving`) with the toolbar's mode (shown for the via tool too; a drag follows it), and the canvas shows `[data-testid=shove-preview]` and the note as for a track. With no room (`reason`), a tip "No room there" names what is in the way and a click or drop is refused with a toast ("No via there: …", "The drag is refused: …"). A click or drop writes the via or the moves and the shoved shapes in one edit ("place a via on net VCC, shoving 1 shape"). Picking another layer in `[data-testid=track-layer]` while a track has a corner puts a via at its last corner (`[data-testid=track-draft][data-runs][data-vias][data-layer]`, the earlier runs in their layer's colour, `.track-draft-via`); where the via has no room the toast says "No via for a layer change there" and the layer goes back; Backspace at the via takes the layer change off. Enter writes each run on its layer and the vias. The walk sends the runs before the change (`earlier`) and the vias, so a shove moves copper out of the way of all of it. The ground a walk or shove is asked on is built once per board placement (the second walk builds none).
- **The router's crossed nets routed again** (`drive pcb-routes-shove`, issue #1009): after a walk answer that crosses router copper the page asks `POST /api/pcb/reroute` (the walk with the way found) and draws the crossed nets routed again round the track, dashed (`[data-testid=router-again][data-tracks][data-left]`), and the crossed note adds "Dashed: routed again round it (VCC) (a preview)" (`[data-testid=track-again]`). Nothing is written: the commit still discards the router's copper.
- **Undo**: every edit of the routes file (a track, a via, a drag, a delete, a lock) is one step of the toolbar's undo and redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z) with the layout's. The server answers each edit with the records of the nets it changed before and after as text (`undo`, one for each net: an edit of several nets, a track with shoved copper, is still one step), and undo sends `restore` for each with them turned round, in one edit, refused when a file's record is not what the step left (it changed since). A delete renumbers the shapes after it, which is why the step is the record and not the change turned round.
- **Stale records**: a record whose pads moved, or for a net the board does not have, is a warning in the Findings tab (rule `routes_pads_moved` or `routes_no_net`, tag `routes file`) and in the problems dock (stage Routing), with the message `fab` prints. Locking the net again writes the record with its pads as they are.
- A change to the routes file by another program (`netsmith routes ...` in a terminal) places the board again, as a change to the layout file does.

## Copper areas (issue 1085): `launch --fixture routed_pours`, `drive pcb-copper`

The Summary tab's "Copper areas" section is `netsmith board copper` (`GET /api/pcb/copper`, `PcbCopper`; the data is `netsmith_pcb::CopperAreas`, so the page and the command cannot differ): a line for each copper layer from the top (`summary-copper-layer`, with `data-layer` and `data-kind` of `plane`, `pour` or `signal`, its net and a pour's fields, `default` for one the design does not state), the zones of the routes file counted apart as hand zones (`summary-copper-zones`, a `summary-copper-zone` line each with its net, layers, area and settings), a layer whose pour or plane a hand zone replaces marked "not drawn", and the teardrop fills (`summary-copper-teardrops`, `data-counted`): not counted until the board is routed here, then counted apart from both. It reads the source and the routes file, so it needs no placing; it is asked again when the routes file, the layout or the routed state changes. The words are in `web/src/pcb/summary.ts` (tested).

## Pads in the pours (issue 1187): `launch --fixture routed_pours`, `drive pcb-fill`

The Summary tab's "Pads in the pours" section is `netsmith board fill` (`GET /api/pcb/fill`, `PcbFill`; the data is `netsmith_pcb::FillReport`, so the page and the command cannot differ): a closed `summary-fill-area` for each pour, plane and zone of a net on each layer (`data-layer`, `data-net`, `data-starved`) that says its source, net, area, the pads it joins counted by kind (2 or more spokes, 1, none, solid, none), the pads of other nets it clears and its copper islands kept and removed; opened, a `summary-fill-pad` line for each pad of its net (`data-pad`, `data-join` of `solid`, `thermal` or `none`, `data-starved`, red when starved). `summary-fill-counts` (`data-routed`, `data-starved`) has the counts of the board, and a `summary-fill-starved` line names each starved thermal relief with its place in the board file's frame. It fills the board as the PCB view has it placed (and routed), so it is asked again when the layout, the routes file or the routed state changes; filling takes longer than the rest of the tab. The words are in `web/src/pcb/summary.ts` (tested).

## What the fill took out and the pads it starves (issue 1188): `launch --fixture routed_pours`, `drive pcb-fill-marks`

The 2D view marks, over the copper, what the report of the fill (`GET /api/pcb/fill`, the same report the Summary tab reads and `netsmith board fill` prints) says: each copper island the fill of a pour took out, hatched (`#pcb-hatch-island`) and outlined, dashed (`.l-islands > path.removed-island[data-layer][data-net][data-area]`, from the island's `outline` in the report), and each pad the fill starves, ringed with a dot at its centre (`.l-starved > g.starved-pad[data-pad]`, a `<title>` with how many spokes join it). The colours are `--pcb-island` and `--pcb-starved` of each theme; a mark of the far side is dimmed as its copper is, and `Removed islands` and `Starved pads` in the Layers menu hide them apart. The report is asked after the pours (`pcbstore.ts`, `loadFill`); `[data-testid=removed-islands][data-current=true]` and `[data-testid=starved-pads][data-current=true]` are the marks of the placement drawn. The recipe copies `fixtures/routed_pours_fill.routes.toml` over the routes file (VCC routed on the back round the header's ground pad, which starves it, and a closed ring of VCC on the back whose inside the fill takes out) and puts the fixture's own file back at its end. `netsmith view pcb --islands --drc` draws the same marks in its picture. The markup is `web/src/pcb/fillMarks.ts` (tested).

## The pours and zones (epic #599): `launch --fixture routed_pours`, `drive pcb-pours`

The fixture is the routed LED board on four layers (`scripts/editor-verify-js/fixtures/routed_pours.nsm`): a ground plane on the first inner layer, a plane on the second, ground poured on the back, and in the routes file a ground zone on the front with a hole in it and, over it at a higher priority, a zone of the anode net.

- **Drawn**: the fill of each pour, plane and routes-file zone, per copper layer and net (`.l-pours > path.pour[data-layer][data-net]`), under the planes' outlines, the pads, tracks, vias and labels and over the board. It is the fill `fab` plots and the 3D view shows (netsmith's own filler: clearances round other nets' copper, thermal reliefs, holes, the edge band), **not** KiCad's refill of a zone. The server sends it apart from the scene (`GET /api/pcb/pours`, `PcbPours`: an SVG path for each net on each layer, points rounded to a micrometre and simplified within 2 micrometres), so the board is drawn first and the fill when it arrives; `[data-testid=pours][data-current=true]` is the fill of the placement drawn (`false` while an edit's new fill is on its way: the old one stays, never a bare board).
- **Colour**: the layer's, never the net's: the front's and the back's copper colour, a plane's tone (`--pcb-plane-1` to `-4`, `data-plane`), with transparency (`fill-opacity`) and a thin edge; light and dark themes have their own tokens. The pointer goes through it (`pointer-events: none`).
- **Layers menu**: Front copper and Back copper hide the fill of their layer with the tracks, Planes hides the planes' fill. A hole of the fill is a hole (`isPointInFill` is false in it); a zone of higher priority has the area.
- Not drawn: KiCad's own fill of a zone, a fill that follows a part while it is dragged (it is made again once the part is dropped), the zones' outlines as marks (the fill is the only thing shown).

## Panels (ADR 0040)

`drive panel` (fixture `led_panel`). A `panel` is listed in the tree with the badge `N`; a click on it opens the PCB view of the board it repeats with the panel drawn instead of the board (`data-testid="panel-preview"`): the boards outlined and named (`LedTile 1` ... `LedTile 6`), the cut lines dashed, the rails with their tooling holes and fiducials, and the panel checks beneath (errors and advice, from `GET /api/panel?name=`). ## Gotchas

- The drawing the canvas gets is `PcbScene.svg` from `GET /api/pcb/scene?board=`, with the part boxes and pads beside it as data; `POST /api/pcb/edit` is the only way a position changes. Both are typed in `api.rs`.
- `GET /api/pcb/3d?board=` is the same placement as glTF 2.0 binary (`model/gltf-binary`, the bytes `netsmith dev board3d --glb` writes for an unrouted board; contract in `docs/BOARD3D.md`), with the placement's layout stamp in `X-Netsmith-Layout-Stamp`, equal to `PcbScene.layoutStamp`. To check one by hand: `curl -s -o b.glb 'http://127.0.0.1:<port>/api/pcb/3d?board=<name>'`.
- Recipes click where the scene says (`pcb-helpers.mjs` turns a board position into a window position with the drawing's `getScreenCTM`); a hover or a click that lands on the ratsnest or the reference text would miss the pad, so those layers have `pointer-events: none`.
- Recipes name a part by its instance path (`PART` in `pcb-helpers.mjs`: `stage.output_cap`) and read its designator from the scene (`labelOf`), never write `C1` or `U7`: designators follow the kind of the part and the placer and have changed before. The placer also chooses the turn a part starts with, so a recipe checks a quarter turn against the turn it read, not against 0.
- The server is the binary: after changing the page, `cargo build -p netsmith-cli`, then `cleanup` and `launch` again, or the recipe drives the old page.
- A flip or a first entry changes what the derived placer is told (`place_options`: the faces of the parts with entries), so the other, derived parts may move too. That is the placer's, not the editor's.

- Every kind of drawing is in a group `l-...` (see the head of `crates/netsmith-pcb/src/svg.rs`); add new drawings to a group or the toggles miss them.
- The drawing the page gets is `svg::render_view` (no key); `fab` still writes `svg::render` with the key.
- Routing is per board and is forgotten when the files are built again.
- A board that cannot be placed because of the project's own files (a part with no footprint mapping, a footprint that lacks a pad the symbol uses, a size that does not fit) is a `422` from `/api/pcb/scene`. The page's reads send `Accept: application/json`, so the body is `{code, message, ...}` (`footprint_lacks_pad`, `no_kicad_mapping`, ...; `ApiFailure.code` in `client.ts`), and the message is what the "The board could not be placed" state shows under its title; a fault of the server is a `500` with plain text. `carrier_and_cards` has invented parts with no KiCad symbol: its boards show that state, not a drawing. A layout file the placer refuses (a part dragged out of its requirements' window and locked there, two locks over each other) is not a `422`: the view shows the board with the file's places, the free parts where the placer ended, and a `placement_refused` error among the findings, beside the overlaps and the requirements measured on the board (issue #779).

## Round 6

- A board opens fitted to the window and no larger than **300 %** (100 % is 12 px to the millimetre; `placeView` in `pcb/Canvas.tsx`; Fit does the same), on a canvas of faint dots that moves and scales with it.
- A **reference** is small beside the pads: its size is the drawing's (0.46 mm) held between 10 and 13 screen pixels (`--pxl`, the size of a screen pixel in the drawing's units with its unit); a **plane label** is smaller still, inside the top left corner of its plane, above the parts (a group of its own, `.l-plane-labels`), with a halo, and the labels of planes that share a corner are stacked a line apart. A label that a part or a designator lies under is moved (round 7, `pcb/planeLabels.ts`, run when the drawing or the zoom changes): the four corners of its plane in turn, then a line or two further in, then along the top and bottom edge every 2.5 mm, the first where it meets no part, designator or earlier label at the zoom shown; with none free it is hidden (`plane-label-off`) and comes back when the zoom makes room. `four-layer` asserts that no visible label lies on a part at six zooms.
- The toolbar has the **Changes vs** switch; the marks are a 2 px outline with a faint tint over the part's courtyard, outside the pick's outline (no glow).

## Plane labels that find no room: the chip `+N` (round 8, then round 9)

`pcb/planeLabels.ts`: `placeAll` puts each plane's label at the first of its places (the four corners, a line or two further in, along the edges) where it meets no part and no label placed before it, so two labels never meet; one with no place is hidden (`plane-label-off`) and `fitPlaneLabels` hands the hidden planes back (`HiddenPlane`: the words of the label, net, layer, tone, the plane's box). Round 8 stood a tag `+N` in the first free place of the drawing, with a tooltip; at a zoom where a board is a few pixels long that was two characters of SVG text over it, with no way to reach the names by keyboard.

Round 9 (`pcb/PlaneMore.tsx`, `drive pcb-plane-more` on `four_layer`): the tag is a **chip** pinned to the bottom left corner of the canvas (`PanZoom`'s `overlay`, so it neither moves nor scales with the drawing: the same size and as readable at every zoom, and clear of the zoom bar), a button `[data-testid=plane-more]` with the layers icon and `+N`, named `2 planes have no room for a label at this zoom: GND · In1.Cu, 3V3 · In2.Cu. Show the list` (`aria-haspopup=menu`, `aria-expanded`). It opens a menu (`plane-more-list`, opening upward): a row for each hidden plane with a swatch in its layer's tone (`--pcb-plane-1` to `-4`) and `Zoom to it`. Pointing at a row or focusing it outlines that plane on the board (`rect.plane-peek`); choosing one (click, Enter) zooms the board to the plane, which makes room for its label; ArrowDown, ArrowUp, Home and End move (and stop the canvas from nudging the picked parts with the same arrows), Escape closes and gives the focus back to the chip, Tab closes. With the Planes layer switched off the labels go with the planes (`.l-plane-labels` is hidden by the same rule) and no chip is shown: a plane that is not drawn has no label to find room for. `four-layer` still checks that no two labels meet at any zoom and that the chip counts the hidden ones; nothing is drawn in the SVG for it (`.plane-label-more` is gone). A hint `Zoom to fit` for a small board in a large canvas was considered and left out: a board that small is already at Fit (the zoom is capped at 300 %), so the hint would say something that does nothing.
