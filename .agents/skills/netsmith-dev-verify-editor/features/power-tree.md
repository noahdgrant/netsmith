# Power tree

## How to get to it

The section "Power tree" (key 3). For a board of a system it shows the whole system: every rail a part draws from, and the stages between them, joined left to right; the joins between boards are stages of kind "Board link".

## What it draws

A left-to-right hierarchy (`src/flow/layout.ts`, pure and deterministic, shared with the system view): sources on the left, each converter, protection stage, filter or switch as a small box (a "stage") between its input rail and its output rail, the output rails with their loads on the right. Edges are right angles with rounded corners, each in a free channel: between layers in a lane of its own, through a lane of its own in a layer it passes, and, for an edge that closes a cycle (the 3.3 V rail feeds a boost converter that feeds the 3.3 V rail back), along a band under the whole drawing, so none runs behind or through a card, lit or not. The ends of the edges sit on an 8 px grid (a card's anchor line is on it, and the ends of several edges on one side are a step apart) and a node is shifted a step or two when that makes an edge straight, so a wire is straight unless it must turn and no jog is smaller than a step, and the stage box carries the label (its kind and what it makes, "Buck 5 V", then its name and type), so no label sits on a line. The kind comes from the stage's type name (Buck, Boost, Ldo/Linear, Protect/Surge, Filter, Switch, Diode), else from what the voltage does.

A rail card (`src/views/Power.tsx`, numbers from `src/power/format.ts`): the name, the nominal voltage and its range, what supplies it (a line with a leading arrow and negative numbers), the total load as `min .. max`, a bar and a line "Headroom 11 mA of 100 mA" (or "Over by ...", or "No supply limit stated"), and one summary line "28 loads". The summary opens the list: the five heaviest loads, "+N more", loads that settled at 0 A in ONE line "22 loads at 0 A" that opens on a click, and loads with an unsettled current. Currents are in A, mA or uA with three significant digits and tabular figures. The converters a rail feeds are not listed as loads ("feeds 3 converters" says it). "List all loads" opens every card.

Click any card or stage: what feeds it is lit in the selection's blue (solid edges) and what it feeds in the same blue dashed, the rest dimmed (orange is for the primary action only), and the minimap shows both (a source rail lights its whole downstream, a leaf its upstream, a card in the middle both); a lit edge takes the route it had; Escape clears. Arrow keys (with the tree focused, or after a click) move along the tree: Left to what feeds it, Right to what it feeds (back the way it came), Up and Down to the neighbours in the column; Enter opens or closes the loads of the selected rail. Hover a load (or focus it): its path, the part, the pin, the current and "Show in source", which goes to where the rail is declared; clicking the row does the same, and Back returns. The stage name is a link to its source. The minimap (bottom left) shows the whole tree and the window; click or drag in it to move. The zoom bar has Fit and 1:1 (one pixel to one pixel: the text is 12 px or more there; "Fit" shrinks everything to show the whole tree).

The Issues strip lists, worst first, a rail whose total can top its supply's limit (red), a rail with no source, a rail or stage with a current or efficiency the solver has not settled (amber); each is a button that selects the card, and the card has a badge. The editor's own model gives all of it (`/api/power`: nodes, edges and `stages`, with the efficiency of each converter); the checker is not involved.

## Driving it

`scripts/editor-verify drive power` (fixture `two_boards`), `drive single-board` (launch with `--fixture regulator`), `drive big-power` (`--fixture big`), and, for the invented board in `scripts/editor-verify-js/fixtures/power_tree.nsm` (8 rails, a buck, a boost, a linear regulator, a protection stage, a filter, a load switch, a backup diode, 22 reference pins at 0 A on one rail), `launch --fixture power_tree` then `drive power-tree power-issues` (9 rails and 9 stages: the board has a cycle).

`power-tree` asserts, at 1366 x 768 and 2000 x 1000: 9 rail cards and 9 stages; every forward edge runs left to right; **no stretch of any edge (the one that closes the cycle too) meets a card it does not join, none is shorter than 6 px, every end is on the 8 px grid**; the cycle's edge goes round under every card; no two cards overlap and every name lies inside its card; no text under 12 px; the 3.3 V card (range, "Total load 2.5 mA .. 82 mA", headroom against 100 mA, "28 loads"); the list (five heaviest, "+1 more", "22 loads at 0 A" opens to 22 rows and folds again); **a click on the first rail (VIN) lights all its downstream cards and edges in the blue tint and none in the orange; a click on a leaf (VANA) lights its upstream chain in orange only; a click in the middle (V5) lights both, with a tint on the cards and in the minimap; a lit edge keeps its route and still meets no card**; the arrow keys and Escape; hover (part, pin, current) and "Show in source" (one pane stays one pane, Back returns); a stage name goes to its source. `power-issues` changes the answer in flight (the solver will not build an overdrawn board) to show a red card, "No source", an unsettled current and an unsettled efficiency, and an issue button that jumps to its card.

## Gotchas

- Every number comes from the solved model (`Model`, `Balance`), not from text. A supply is a current range that ends at zero or below; the solver's end of a range that stops at zero can be a hair above it (`5e-324`), which `crates/netsmith-pipeline/src/power.rs` treats as zero (`NEGLIGIBLE`), or the supply would count as a load and its converter would have no edge.
- A rail turns red only if the demand's top exceeds the capacity. A board that fails the solver's own check does not build, so you see it only on a stale drawing; the unit tests in `power.rs` cover it.
- `/api/power` has no field for what a rail is for; the stage kind is read from its type name, and a type named otherwise is a plain "Converter" unless the voltage says buck or boost.
- The graph is fitted to the section, which has the whole window unless the window is split. The tree opens fitted with 56 px of air round it, centred, at most 150 % (a two-node graph is not blown up to a window; a tree that would need less than 75 % to fit opens at 75 % from its top left, because below that its smallest text is under 8 px: the minimap, the scroll and Fit, which shows the whole tree whatever the scale, find the rest). Fit does the same as the opening view only for a tree that fits at 75 % or more; 1:1 is true size.
- Fixture stages are `part`s with no pads, so `power_tree.nsm` solves and shows in the editor but cannot be written to KiCad.
