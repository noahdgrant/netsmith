# Schematic view and click-through

## How to get to it

The section "Schematic" (the second, key 2; Source is the first). The board shown is the one in the selector at the top. The toolbar above the drawing has the module navigator's toggle, Previous and Next, the module picker (`Board > instance Module`, a drop-down list with a filter), `?` (opens the editor's cheat sheet at the Schematic group) and "Details". The **module navigator** (`.sheetnav`) is a collapsible outline on the left: "All modules", then each module in the order it is declared in the source (not alphabetical) with its instance and module name, its part count, and badges for the nets that leave it (`⇄ n`) and its problems; a filter box (module names, or a designator on the module) shows from six modules up; it is open by default when the view is 900 px wide or more. "Details" shows the inspector (`.sinspector`, the pattern of `components/Inspector.tsx`: an eyebrow `.ih-eyebrow` (`Part`, `Net`, `Bus`, `Module`, `Board`), the title `.title` with icon buttons, a definition list, folding sections for pins, parts and notes, pins and nets as chips) on the right: it is open by default only when the view is 1500 px wide or more.

## Driving it

`scripts/editor-verify drive schematic`

1. Select `TwoBoards/led`. The drawing has `data-board="LedBoard"`.
2. Click the resistor `R1` in the drawing: it is selected (a chip `PART R1` at the bottom left, the inspector shows it with "Go to source" and "Show on PCB"). Double-click it (or Enter, or "Go to source"): the Source section opens at `.r1: Resistor {}` and a button "Back to Schematic" appears in its file tabs. Escape (Alt+Left outside the text, the button, or the browser's Back button) returns to the schematic as it was left.
3. Open Source, click a line that declares the LED, go back to the schematic: `D1` is highlighted in the drawing (class `mark`). A line that declares a module (`.stage: LdoStage`) marks every part inside it.
4. Click `D1` in the parts list: it is selected (the row is highlighted, the inspector shows `Led`); the list has a filter box.

### A big board: `drive big-schematic` after `launch --fixture big`

One module is shown at a time: the navigator, Previous/Next, `[` and `]` (and Page Up/Down) and the picker choose it; the arrow keys pan. A board with more than one module opens on the first in source order; the pick is kept for each board in localStorage. "All modules" is a grid of cards in source order, each with its title, counts and a drawing of the module; a click on a card selects the module that card draws (the shared selection, kind module, key the instance path) and opens the module; the board's own module of loose parts is no module and just opens. Each module keeps its zoom and place for the session (leaving and coming back, even through the Source section, finds it as it was).

**Zoom is in true scale**: 100% is the drawing at its size on a 96 dpi screen (1 mm is 3.78 px; the drawing's text, 1.27 mm, is 4.8 px). A module opens fitted when its text is then between 7 and 16 px; a module that would fit with smaller text opens at 146% (7 px text) at its top left with a chip "Zoomed in to keep the text readable" and a Fit button; a small module is not enlarged past 16 px text. **The keys are commands** of the editor's registry, group *Schematic* (`web/src/commandset.ts`, `schematicCommands`): `[` `]` (also Page Up/Down) previous/next module, `F` or `0` fit, `W` fit the width, `Shift+1` 100%, `Shift+2` zoom to the selection, `+` (`=`) and `-` zoom, the arrows pan, `Tab` and `Shift+Tab` next/previous pin of the selected net, `Enter` open the selected part in Source. They are in the palette and in the cheat sheet (`?`) under *Schematic*, work only while the Schematic is the pane in use (`when`), and leave a text field its letters (a plain key never runs from a field). Tab, Enter and the arrows are held to the drawing (`keyFrom`): with a button or a list focused they keep their own meaning. The view hands out its zoom, modules and pins to the commands through `views/schematic-actions.ts`; the canvas's own key handling is off (`keys={false}` on `PanZoom`). The toolbar's `?` button runs the cheat sheet scrolled to the Schematic group; the view has no key list of its own.

**Selection and wayfinding**: the selection is the editor's shared one (`selection.ts`; `views/schematic-selection.ts` is the schematic's only door to it), so a part, net or module picked in go to anything, the Problems dock, the PCB or the BOM shows here and a pick here shows there; the top bar chip names it and Esc clears it everywhere. A pick made elsewhere is also *revealed* (see `schematic-integration.md`). A click on a part selects it; a click on a wire, a net label, a flag or a pin selects its net, which stays lit with the rest dimmed (hover does not change it) until Esc or a click on empty space. The inspector shows the net's pins grouped by module, each a link ("N pins on M modules"); Tab and Shift+Tab (drawing focused), or the ‹ › buttons of the top bar chip, walk the pins, switching module and keeping the pin in view (a ring marks it, `.pin.cur`). A click on a flag follows the net to the module that carries it: one other module, it goes; several, a small menu lists them (`.flag-menu`). In the navigator and the overview, the modules that carry the selected net have an orange dot or outline.

A board with more than 16 parts is drawn by module (`crates/netsmith-view/src/sheets.rs`): each module instance is a dashed box titled `instance: Module`, laid out on its own, and the boxes are set out in a grid about 1.6 times wider than tall. The parts that are in no module are a box named for the board. A box with more than 20 parts is cut into `name (1/2)` and `name (2/2)` along the connections. Every net on a module is wired, whatever its size, with orthogonal wires on a grid that go round parts and texts and cross each other only at right angles (`crates/netsmith-view/src/layout/grid.rs`). A name is used only where a wire cannot do: a net that goes on to another module leaves through a flag shaped like KiCad's hierarchical label, wired from its pins, with the net's name in it; a rail is a power symbol (below). A net with a generated name (`net-<module>.<part>.<pin>`) is always wires, never a name or a rail. A module is one drawing (up to 60 parts, `MAX_SHEET_PARTS` in `sheets.rs`; the old drawing cut a module of more than 20 in two because it put every column in one row).

The placement is what a person draws (`crates/netsmith-view/src/layout.rs`, the design goal at its top: a schematic is for a human to read): the main parts (an IC, a connector) in a row left to right along the signal; a two-terminal part that serves one pin (a decoupling capacitor, a pull-up, a feedback divider, a bootstrap capacitor, an inductor, a crystal) next to that pin, on its side; parts in series (a divider) in one stack with what is on the node wired out sideways; supplies above, grounds below. A wire never crosses a text, and texts have a halo. A pin with nothing on it has a muted X (tooltip: `not connected: <part> <pin> (<kind>)`); a pin the checker says must be connected and is not has a red ring and X and the checker's message (a board with one does not build, so this shows only in a part's own pin table under construction).

### Rails and flags (round 2 of the review)

A rail is **one straight wire across its group of parts**, at the bottom (a ground) or the top (a supply) beyond every part, with **one symbol**; every pin or run of pins on the rail hangs from it by a straight wire (pins next to each other on one side share a bus first). The symbol is directly above (a supply) or below (a ground) the terminal of the outermost satellite on the rail (a capacitor), or at the part's own centre if there is one part, else at the left end. The capacitors that decouple a supply pin stand in one level row, an even pitch apart, the same distance from the part, hanging from the supply rail. Flags are in two columns at the edges of the module, one per net, each on its own row (one pin pitch apart), joined to the net by wires; a flag is 1.3 mm high so a grid step is left between two. Nothing overlaps: power symbols (with their names), flags, X markers and the texts of parts keep a grid step from each other and 0.6 mm from the wires of other nets (`no_two_items_are_closer_than_a_grid_step`, and the recipe measures it in the page).

### Power symbols

A net that the checked model says is the return of a rail (the `neg` side) is drawn with the ground symbol (bars that shrink), a net a rail draws from with the supply symbol (a stem and an arrow head, the name beside it), by the model and not by name (`crates/netsmith/src/editor/analysis.rs`, `rails_of`). A symbol is only at the end of a wire, never on one, directly above (a supply) or below (a ground) the pins it serves, which share a bus; pins of one rail near each other share one symbol. Grounds carry their names only when the board has several. Hovering a symbol lights the whole net; its tooltip names it. The KiCad export does not use them.

### Proof in a browser

`scripts/editor-verify launch --fixture schematic_bench` (the invented project `examples/schematic_bench`: four modules, `adc`, `mcu`, `buck`, `modem`) then `drive schematic-rules` measures the page's own SVG with the browser's text boxes and asserts each rule above (wires not names, parts beside their pins, a symbol only at the end of a wire, no wire over any text, an X on every open pin, a box for each unit of the three-unit amplifier and the two-unit processor, the processor's supply capacitors beside its power unit and not its signal unit, a table for the signal unit's 64 pins, a regulator as one row with its capacitors hanging from it, 22 parts on one module). `SHOT_LABEL=after scripts/editor-verify drive schematic-shots` takes each module at 1366 x 768 and 2000 x 1000. Look at the screenshots.

### A symbol of several units, or of many pins

A part whose symbol has several units is one box for each unit (ADR 0050: `U4A`, `U4B`; selecting the part lights all its boxes), each with the pins the unit has on the sides the symbol puts them; a unit of more than 60 pins is a box with columns of pins in labelled groups (by the start of the pin names, the unconnected last) that says "N pins", and a power unit has its supply pins along the top edge and its grounds along the bottom edge. A decoupling capacitor stands by the unit that owns the supply pins. Pin names are as big as net names there. The KiCad export keeps the units.

1. The drawing has a `.module-sheet` for each of the 24 instances and one for the rest, in a shape that is not a strip.
2. `VIN` is a supply symbol with its name in each regulator stage, never a wire across the page.
3. The navigator names each module, in source order; a click opens its module; its filter finds one. The keys `[` and `]` step through the modules. A click on a card in "All modules" selects the module and opens it (`drive big-schematic`, `drive schematic-integration`).
4. Wheel zooms, a drag pans; a module keeps its zoom, F fits it, Shift+1 is 100%.

Only the open module's drawing is shown (the page regroups the server's SVG by module, `src/schematic/load.ts`; the other modules' groups are hidden, `.sheet-slot`), so a locator for `.module-sheet` or `.part` also finds hidden ones and the overview's copies: scope it with `.sheet-slot:not([style*="none"])` when it matters.

## Gotchas

- Parts have no fill, so a click in the middle of a symbol would fall through to the module; the stylesheet gives `.part` `pointer-events: bounding-box`.
- Dragging pans; a click that ends a drag is swallowed. Wheel zooms around the pointer; double-click on empty space fits.
- When the source has a mistake the last drawing that built is shown dimmed under the stale strip (`.stale-strip`, the shared one of every view); the schematic has no banner of its own.
- The key `?` and the toolbar's `?` button both open the cheat sheet of the whole editor; the button scrolls it to the Schematic group (`section[data-group="Schematic"]`, marked `.aimed`). `drive schematic-keys` (fixture `schematic_bench`) checks the keys in the palette and the cheat sheet, that they work, that a text field keeps its letters, and that the BOM does not offer them.
- Esc closes the schematic's flag menu before it clears the selection (`data-esc-closes` on it tells the `selection.clear` command to wait).
- There is one selection chip, the top bar's (`.selchip`); the drawing has none. The "zoomed in to keep the text readable" chip (`.hold-chip`) and the ring (`.pulse-ring`) are not selection chips.
- Moving symbols is not built (ADR 0019 designs the sidecar file).
