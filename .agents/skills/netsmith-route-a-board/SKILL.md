# Route a board

The router draws copper for every connection the routes file does not make. The **routes file**, `<stem>.routes.toml` beside the source, holds the copper a person, an agent or an import decided: tracks, vias and zones of nets, each shape optionally locked. Only `netsmith routes` writes it, and it is committed with the source. The router's own copper is a cache (`.netsmith/routes/`, never committed): `fab` never writes it into the routes file. Where the part stands is the layout file (`netsmith-use-layout`); the format, the refusals and the rip-up rounds are in the guide's "The routes file" and "Connections left: rounds of rip-up" (`docs/language/23-the-shape-of-a-board.md`). `netsmith routes --help` and each subcommand's help have the flags.

## Loop

1. **Route and read what is left**: `netsmith board fab`. A board the router could not finish still gets its files in `fab/`, marked `INCOMPLETE`, and the run ends in the error `N connections could not be routed`; never order from it. Read `fab/<board>/routing-failure.txt` (each connection left with its net and two pads, the stage of the router it last failed in, and what stands in its way: the nets whose copper a way across would cross, or the pads, vias and board edge that wall an end in) and `routing-report.json` (the same as data, with a crowding map: 1 mm squares, `crowding.counts` row by row from `crowding.origin`; the highest squares are where parts need more room). `netsmith routes show --format json` lists the routes file's records, numbered shapes, what of it is not used and the connections still to route. It routes nothing.
2. **Decide who owns the copper.** A shape is the person's when they drew it, a drawing or a mechanical fit fixes it, or it is a net the router must not touch (a sense line, a power path). `routes lock --net N` stops the router adding copper to that net (a connection it still lacks is left, and `fab` says so); `--track N`, `--via N` or `--zone N` keep one shape from `routes delete`, `routes tidy` and the editor's drags. Lock nothing the person did not ask for: the router's copper is the router's. There is no command that copies router copper into the file; draw what must be kept.
3. **Read the failure before drawing.** The stage and the thing in the way say which fix is cheap:
   - a pad walled in by its neighbours, or `pad(s) U1.12, ...` named as the wall: move a part or give it room (`netsmith-use-layout`, `layout fit`; a `.routing_margin` on the board or the busy part makes the placer and `layout fit` keep room round its courtyard), or narrow the process's track and space within the factory's limits. `fab` already warns `pad N of ... is walled in by ...` after placing, before routing, and `layout check` says it as `walled_in`: act on that warning before a long route;
   - other nets' copper in the way and a crowded square: more room round those parts, a layer more, or a plane or pour for a net that crosses everything;
   - `stopped after the route timeout`: raise `board fab --route-timeout SECS` (0 is no limit); the `Board: routing: ...` lines on stderr say what each try ended with;
   - `failed in the negotiation`: the router already let the connections fight over the crowded cells for its bounded rounds (each connection left ripping up the single connections in its way, and theirs in turn, a few levels deep, then each overlap dearer each round, the cells fought over dearer for the rest of the try, chapter 23 _Connections left: negotiation_), and none gave way; the bounds are counts, so more time changes nothing: give the place room (a part moved, a layer) instead;
   - nothing left to give: `--rip-rounds N` sets the rounds of rip-up of whole nets for a run (`rip_rounds` in `netsmith.toml` for the project, 0 turns them off); try more before drawing by hand.
   - a net of a connection that only other pins would shorten (a GPIO function): `netsmith board pins show`, and `board fab --pins auto|locked`; `netsmith-read-errors` has the pin messages.
4. **Route one net at a time** before drawing: `netsmith routes route N` (or one connection, `N:U1.3-C2.1`) routes it with the router round the copper there and writes its tracks and vias to its record, unlocked; the text says the narrowest place of the way and the widest track that has room there. Run it with `--dry-run` first: it writes nothing. When the net has no way it names the nets whose copper stands in the way (exit 1, nothing written), or what walls it in when no net's copper does (pads, vias, a locked net's copper, the edge). `--rip A,B` lets it remove the unlocked tracks and vias of those nets, route the net through the room, and route them again after it (a ripped net with no way back may rip one more of them in turn, `--rip-depth N` for deeper; when one cannot be put back it tries again in another order, `--tries N`, default 3, and each try is said: the order, who ripped whom, what was not put back); ask before ripping copper the person drew, and read the result with `routes show`. A locked net or shape is never ripped, nor is a net a pour or a plane joins (GND on a plane): the failure text never lists one in a `--rip` list, and `--rip` of one is refused as having no copper to rip, so go round it, move a part or draw by hand; and a result that would leave a ripped net more open or add a finding of the design rules check is refused. Its bends follow the board's corner style, rounded with arcs unless the board says `.corner: TrackCorner` and `.corner &= TrackCorner::Mitered` (45 degree corners, no arcs; `view pcb` then draws any arc as its chamfer); `--corner mitered` or `--corner rounded` changes it for one run, and `routes tidy` keeps it.
   - **A knot or a tangled corner**: when hand-drawn copper (or several nets' copper) blocks a connection in one place and ripping net by net does not settle it, `netsmith routes route --region 12,8,6,5 --dry-run` (X,Y,W,H in mm, the outline's frame) rips the unlocked copper inside that box, cuts what crosses its edge (the outside part stays), and routes every connection it carried, plus the open ones through the box, together with the router's negotiation from the cut ends. It writes only when no connection is left open that was made before and the weighted cost does not rise (both totals said); a region it cannot close names the connections and writes nothing: box a little more room, or unlock what holds it. Locked shapes are never touched. Box the knot, not the board; read the result with `routes show` and `board nets --compare`.
5. **Draw by hand** what is left, one shape at a time, in the placement's frame (mm from the outline's top left corner, x right, y down, as `layout set`):
   - `netsmith routes add-track --net N --layer top --from X,Y --to X,Y [--mid X,Y for an arc] [--width MM] [--lock]`; `--layer` is `top`, `bottom` or an inner signal layer; the width defaults to the net's own;
   - `routes add-via --net N --at X,Y [--layers top,inner1] [--lock]` (the process's via by default); a net whose current needs several vias side by side (`via_current`) takes them as one group, `--at X,Y` repeated or `--count N --pitch MM` round one `--at`, checked together (vias stand side by side when their copper is no more than the process's least clearance apart, or the board's `.process.via_group_gap`: a pitch above the via's diameter and that gap leaves them apart);
   - `routes add-zone --net N --layer bottom --rect X,Y,W,H` (or `--outline X,Y` per corner, or `--board-outline`; `--pads`, `--clearance`, `--priority`). A zone of another net cuts the design's pour on its layer (the pour fills round it, as in KiCad); a zone of the pour's own net replaces the pour there (restate it with `--board-outline` to keep it whole), unless it is made with `--patch`: then the pour is kept and the zone's settings hold inside its outline alone, the way to join one pad solid (`--pads solid --patch --rect` round the pad) or change the clearance or spokes in one place of a pour. The command says which. Prefer stating a plane or pour in the source (`netsmith-write-nsm`) over a zone, unless the user wants a hand shape.
   - **the escape of a fine-pitch part** (a BGA, an LGA, a QFN's lands): `netsmith routes sites U1 [--net N] --format json` lists where a via of the process fits between its pads, each site's `at`, the nearest `pads` with their nets, and `free` or `held_by` (each holder's `net`, `what`, `track` or `via`, `at`, `rule`, `required`, `measured` and `shortfall`: move or reroute it by the shortfall); `pockets` names a fill (layer, net) a via there would cut into a new island holding pads or vias, and `--zone-safe` lists those sites apart: prefer the others under a plane; the sites in its pads are in `in_pad`, apart, since a via in a pad needs the process to fill it (`filled`, `.via_in_pad`). Each site is judged by the check of `routes add-via`, so a free site takes `routes add-via --net N --at X,Y` (its places are the routes file's frame); plan the escape from it rather than computing sites from pad places. The router's copper of `fab` is not on the board it reads.
   - **a net that must change layer at a chosen place** (a via beside a part, on the far side of an obstacle): `netsmith routes route N --via X,Y [--via X,Y] --dry-run` (`X,Y,top,inner1` for a blind via) places the via, or uses the record's via there, joins each pad to the nearest via first and then routes the rest; a via the design rules check refuses names what blocks it and nothing is written. Use it in place of `add-via` and routing by hand.
   - **a net that must run on an inner layer** (under a part, away from the outer copper): `netsmith routes route N --layer inner2 --dry-run` puts a via beside each pad, joins the pad to it, and routes the vias on that layer alone. Only an inner signal layer is taken (`.process.inner2.use &= "signal"`): a plane (an inner layer the process gives a net, as both inner layers of a usual four-layer board are) is refused with the board's layers listed, which is also why such a board's copper is all on the outer layers. Add `--via X,Y` to choose the vias.
   - **fan out a fine-pitch part as a group** (a BGA, an LGA): `netsmith routes fanout U1 --dry-run --format json` assigns a site to each pad inside the outer ring whose net leaves the part (negotiated across the group) and routes pad to via; `escaped` has each pad's via `at`, `left` each pad it could not fan out with its `blocker` (no site in reach, a site held by another net's copper, what walls the line in). Narrow with `--net A,B` or `--pads B2,C3`, limit the sites with `--sites X,Y;X,Y`, or give a plan, `--plan FILE` of `PAD = X,Y` lines (the routes file's frame; a planned site the check refuses is refused, naming the blocker, and nothing is written). Drop `--dry-run` to write; exit 1 when a pad is left (the others are written). Prefer it to adding vias one by one.
   - **one place for a via** (anywhere, before `add-via`): `netsmith routes check-via --at X,Y [--net N] [--diameter MM --drill MM --layers A,B] --format json` says `legal`, and for each rule broken in `breaks` the `item` in the way (`pad`, `track`, `via`, `hole`, `outline`, `keep-out`, `mask`, `process`), its `net`, `required`, `measured` and `shortfall` in mm; `margins` are the spaces kept with little room, the least first. Move the via by the shortfall rather than guessing; exit 1 when it does not fit. Its `pockets` (and a `note: the via cuts ...` line of `add-via` and `move-via`) say the via would seal a piece of a pour, plane or zone holding pads or vias off from the rest: pick another place unless the piece is joined otherwise.
   - **a via in the wrong place** (to make room, or to move a link that changes layer): `netsmith routes move-via --net N --via K --to X,Y [--dry-run]` (K as `routes show` numbers it) moves it and lays every layer's tracks that end in it again in 45 degree legs, checked as `add-track` is; refused, nothing written, with the findings or the connection lost named. Do not delete and redraw the via and its tracks by hand. A locked via or track is refused: unlock it (`routes unlock --net N --via K` or `--track M`), move, and lock it again.
   - a pad or via a pour leaves open, or a pocket of a pour cut off from the rest (`routes show` reports the open connection): `netsmith board fill --islands` lists each region of each fill, the pads and vias it joins, whether it reaches the net's main region, and for a cut-off region the tracks, vias, pads and rule areas that bound it, the ones that part it from the main region first (`it parts the two`): move or reroute that track (`routes move-via`, `routes route --region`), or join the pad (`routes join-pad`).
   - a pad the fill leaves unjoined or starved (`board drc`'s `unjoinable_thermal` or `starved_thermal`, `board fill`'s `unjoinable` and `starved`): `netsmith routes join-pad REF.PAD [--dry-run]` draws the shortest track of its net from the pad into the fill (16 directions, at most 5 mm), or a track and a via into the fill on another layer, checked as `add-track` is; refused with why the shortest was refused when none fits. Do not script a direction from `board fill --format json` (its `outline` has each fill's pieces and holes, for a look at the room). Where the person wants that pad (or a part's ground pads, as beside an RF pin) joined solid, or with spokes of another width, state it on the pin in the source instead (`.gnd.pour_connection &= PadConnection::Solid`, `.gnd.spoke_width &= 0.3mm..`; the guide's "A pad's own connection"): the fill and the board file take it over the pour's, and `board fill` names the pin.
   - a pad's piece of a pour cut off from the rest of the fill by a track, pad or via of another net, a hole, a rule area or a pour of a higher priority (`board drc`'s `pad_island_cut`: it names the pads, what cut the piece off and the piece; the check's connectivity joins a pad by the pour's outline and does not see it, so only `fab`'s router would, and join it): move what it names off the piece's only way to the rest of the fill (a track or a via; a pad or a hole means moving its part, `netsmith-use-layout`), or give the pad a via or a track of its own (`routes join-pad REF.PAD`). `routes add-track` and `routes add-via` refuse a track or a via that cuts such a piece (a warning of `board drc`, an error with `guidelines = "error"` or `fab --strict`).
   - copper near a rule area (`note [near_rule_area]`, issue #1591): a part, track, via or pour within the process's `.rule_area_reach` (3 mm) of a module's antenna keep-out or another rule area that forbids copper or parts, under it on the other face, inside it where it does not forbid that kind, or beside it. A note, not a failure: tell the person what it names and let them decide; move a track or via off, or leave it when the module's guide allows it.
   - copper of another net at a switch node (`note [switch_node_copper]`, issue #1585): a via or track within the process's `.switch_node_keepout` (1 mm) of a buck regulator's switch node, or under its body, with no plane between. A note, not a failure: move the via or track off the node, or put it below a plane; `board nets --power-stage` lists each regulator's input loop, boot and support capacitor routes (length and vias) and feedback length, for a route that is long or crosses layers.
   - a pour or plane the routes file replaced (`note [pour_replaced]`, issue #1612): a zone of the net of the process's pour on its layer (`routes add-zone` says "not drawn now") replaces the pour there, so the net has on that layer only the zone's copper and a via or pad of the net outside it joins nothing on it; a `via_dangling` that ends with "the process's pour of net N on L is replaced by the routes file's zone ..." is that. A note, not a failure: widen the zone to the pour's reach (`--board-outline` restates the pour whole), or give the via a track or another zone, or delete the zone (`routes delete`) to get the pour back; a zone meant only to change the join in its outline is a patch (`routes add-zone --patch`, or `patch = true` in the file), which keeps the pour and has no note.
   - a via whose hole or ring comes near a pad (`note [via_hole_near_pad]`, `note [via_ring_near_pad]`, issue #1579): its hole within the process's `.via_hole_pad_reach` (0.1 mm) of a pad's copper of any net and layer, or its ring in a pad (`.via_ring_pad_reach`, 0 mm); the line after the findings counts the vias and names the pads, and `fab-notes.txt` counts the hole ones in its line of vias whose hole touches or comes near a pad's edge. A note, not a failure, and the router is not held to it: move the via (`netsmith routes`) where the hole could open in the pad's mask opening, or keep it.
   - **many shapes at once** (a route of tens of segments, a script's output, more than a handful of shapes): write them to one document and run `netsmith routes add shapes.toml --format json` (or `-` with the document on stdin; with the MCP server, the tool `routes_add` with `shapes` the document as a JSON object), not one `add-track` a shape: `[[track]]`, `[[via]]` and `[[zone]]` tables with the fields of the verbs' flags (`net`, `layer`, `from = [x, y]`, `to`, `width`; `at` for a via, a list of places for a group; `rect`, `outline` or `board_outline = true` for a zone). Each shape is checked in turn (tracks, then vias, then zones) on the board with the ones before it, and the file is written once. A refused shape is skipped, and its entry in `shapes` has `result = "refused"`, `said` and `findings` (each `rule`, `at`, `shapes`): move that shape and send only the refused ones again. Use `--stop-on-refusal` when a later shape depends on an earlier one (a track to a via), and `--dry-run` to check a document before writing.
   - **copper drawn by your own tool** (a script or a router of your own, written with `routes add` or `routes import`): read the rules it must keep from `netsmith board rules --format json` (issue #1513), never from `board drc` findings or an exported KiCad board: the least clearance and the clearance between each two net classes (`clearance.pairs`, and a pad's own in `clearance.pads`), each net's class, clearance, wanted `width` and `least_width` (its current's, outer and inner) and `vias` side by side, the mask margin of each footprint with `mask.to_track`, the hole spaces (`holes`), `pad_to_pad`, `copper_to_edge`, the via sizes, the rule areas in board coordinates with the kinds they forbid (`view pcb` hatches them on its picture and prints each, `rule area: antenna of U1 forbids tracks, vias, copperpour on F.Cu, B.Cu`; the editor's PCB view hatches them with a tip, its Layers menu's `Rule areas`), and the layers and pours. They are the numbers `board drc` judges by, read by the same function; keep a margin over them, not under. Places are in the frame of the board file: take `outline_at` from them for the frame of the routes file.
   Take pad places from `netsmith layout parts --format json`, not from the picture. An end that touches nothing yet is allowed, since a route is drawn a shape at a time; `fab` finds one left so. End a track inside the pad it joins (on its centre, or at least on its edge): a round end that only touches the pad's edge is not joined, as in KiCad, and `board drc` says `it only touches`. A command exits 0 when it wrote, 1 when it **refused** and 2 when it could not run. Read a refusal: it names a short, a crossing or a clearance with the place, and the file is as it was. Fix the shape; never loosen a rule to get it in. `routes delete` removes a net's record or one numbered shape, and refuses a locked one: ask before `routes unlock`. A `fab` warning that the routes file has a net the board does not have (a net renamed) is answered by `routes delete --net NAME`, with the name it prints: that record is deleted whole, locked or not, and the command says what it deleted; rename the net in the file by hand instead to keep its copper.
5a. **RF lines and nets on one layer** (issue #1274): a net of a `TrackImpedance` is an RF net, placed first and short and routed first, on one outer layer (the one all its pads are on) with no via; `.pin route one_layer` in the physical file does the same for any net, and `.pin route any_layer` lets an RF net change layers. Such a net with no way on its layer is left, never given a via: give it room on that layer (move the parts in its way, `netsmith-use-layout`), or ask the user before writing `route any_layer`. A via or a second layer on such a net, in the routes file or a board from elsewhere, is a `[constraint] ... route one_layer ... is violated` finding of `fab` and `board drc`: delete that copper (`routes delete --net N`) and route it again. Never add a via to one with `routes add-via`.
5b. **A line through parts in series** (issue #1275): a `TrackPath` (`.first` and `.last` on the two end pins, `.impedance`) gives each net of its chain (a DC block, a 0 ohm link between) the target, so they are RF nets as above. `board nets` lists `path NAME: ...` with the chain's length against its `.budget_length`, and `board drc` and `fab-notes.txt` say a path over it (`track path: ...`): shorten it by moving the path's parts nearer each other (`netsmith-use-layout`), never by loosening the budget. A `.fence` puts ground vias beside its tracks after routing; `fab-notes.txt` says when no pour or plane gave them a ground. Given `.frequency`, its tracks' loss is worked out against `.budget_loss` and said in the same places, when the build-up has a loss tangent (`.process.stackup.loss_tangent`, from the laminate's datasheet: no standard build-up states one, and then the notes say why there is no figure); a path over it is shortened, or its tracks widened on a thicker dielectric, never the budget loosened.
6. **Tidy copper you did not draw shape by shape** (an import, a script, many hand edits): `netsmith routes tidy --dry-run` lists, by kind, net, layer and place, the corners it would cut to 45 degrees (`track_angle`), the collinear pairs it would make one, the dangling vias and stubs it would remove (a stub only on a net the copper finishes), the `acid_trap`s it would remove, and the defects `board drc` does not name: tracks of no length (`zero_length` in the JSON `counts`) and copies of a track (the narrower of two straight tracks of a net and layer with the same two ends; `copies`), tracks inside a pad (from a pad's or via's centre to a point still inside it; `in_pad_tracks` in the JSON `counts`), the longer of two ways between the same two places of a net, tracks that overlap on one line or lie inside another, and runs of tiny tracks round a corner, and with `--widen` the tracks narrower than their net wants (its current, its class, its impedance target) that it would widen, each as far as the clearance to its neighbours allows (with `--widen-to-guideline`, the tracks of a net over its track-drop budget towards the width `board nets` says the path needs, with the net's drop before and after; read the refused widenings for where there was no room); `netsmith routes tidy` makes them (`--format json` for the same as data; `--reuse-routes` tidies the router's kept copper too). It never touches a locked net or shape, and it refuses a change that adds any finding of `board drc` or changes the connections left: read the refused list, each with the finding it would add, and fix those by hand or leave them. On a big board it says on stderr how far each pass has got, net by net, with the time it has left (`--progress json` for lines a program reads); Ctrl+C leaves the files as they were. Tidy before you lock what you keep.
6b. **Resize copper to its current** (a load's current raised, a `track_current` finding; a load whose peak is a burst states `.rms_current` beside `.current`, and the copper is sized for heat by the rms): `netsmith routes widen --net VIN --dry-run` widens each track of the net (repeat `--net`; none for every net) to the widest width that keeps every clearance, up to the width its net wants, found per track, and lists each track left narrower with the width it reached and what holds it (`held by Track [GND] ... allows 0.331 mm`; `short` in the JSON); `netsmith routes widen --net VIN` writes it. It changes nothing but widths, and refuses a widening that adds a `board drc` finding. Move or reroute what holds a short track (`routes move-via`, `routes route --reroute`) and widen again.
7. **Check without routing**: `netsmith board drc` (`--format json` has the rule, place, nets and parts of each finding; exit 1 on an error) judges the board as it stands, the placement with the routes file's copper, and every connection the file does not make is an `unconnected_items` error, so run it to check a hand-drawn shape, not to see what the router would do. `routes show` says the connections still to route. Its notes (and `fab-notes.txt`) list the nets routed far over their straight length (`routed long:`) and the nets with the most vias (`most vias:`): look at each that must be short. A note `pair skew:` names a diff pair whose two lines differ by more than its limit (its `.skew`, else the router's 0.1 mm), and `length group:` a group of nets matched in length whose spread is over its tolerance.
8. **Measure the lines**: `netsmith board nets` (`--route` to route first, `--lengths` longest first, `--format json`) gives each net's routed length (the copper from pad to pad, joined as the design rules check joins it, so a branch that meets a track through a pad's copper or by its round end counts: a second way of one connection and a dead stub are left out and named on a `net ...: ... not counted` line, and the editor's PCB view draws them dashed (`Not counted` in its Layers menu); remove them from the board) and straight length, vias, layer changes and layers, and each diff pair's two lengths, skew against the pair's limit (its `.skew`, else 0.1 mm), coupled length and share, least and largest gap, and vias side by side, from the copper `view pcb` draws, by the router's own measure. The router routes a pair (named `dp`/`dm`, a `PairImpedance`, a `DiffPair`, a `Can`'s or a `Usb2`'s lines) as one object: pulled from its pads to one pitch (the stated spacing, else the nets' clearance), its vias side by side, the shorter line lengthened by a meander to the pair's `.skew` (a board narrows it, `.lines.skew &= 0mm..0.05mm`; a `Can` offers 2.5 mm). A pair is routed round copper already there, and `netsmith routes route` of the second line of a pair runs it beside the first's copper at the pitch (route one line, then the other); when a board's tries leave connections, the router tries the pairs as two separate lines and `fab` says so (`routed as two separate lines`). A pair over its skew or barely coupled has no room: give it room (move the parts that sit between its lines, or the pads that make it cross), or draw it by hand as a pair, both lines at the pitch and each via beside its partner's. A `LengthGroup`'s nets (`.lane.lines[0]` up, its `.tolerance`) are lengthened towards the longest before the pairs; `board nets` lists each group's lengths and spread, and a group over its tolerance has no room for its meanders: give the shorter nets straight runs. `netsmith board nets --currents` lists each net's one current, the number `track_current`, `via_current` and the track drop all hold it to, with its source (`sum` of the rails and parts on it, `analysis` by the DC analysis, or one `carrier` that states more than the analysis finds, a storage element's `.discharge_peak_current` on the nets of its terminals among them) and the least track and vias side by side it needs: read it before you widen copper or add vias by hand, and lower the current at its source (the rail or part it names) when it is more than the net carries. Its `track drop` section lists each power net's voltage drop along its tracks against the budget of `fab`'s `track drop` guideline (1 % of the rail), worst first, the same measure `fab` makes (a divider or a pin that states its current draws only its own), and `board drc` notes each net over it: widen the rail (`.min_track &=` the width the note gives), re-route the net and read `board nets` again, with no `fab`. To set a rail's typical current beside its worst case, add `--at-current NET=A` (repeatable, in `board nets` and `board drc`): each named net's drop and share of the budget at the design's current and at A (`track_drops_at_current` in JSON), in proportion to the current. It is a report for the user: the widths, `track_current`, `via_current` and the `track drop` guideline keep the design's current, so never use it to claim a rail is within budget; to change what is sized, change the current at its source. Under a net at half its budget or more both list the path's pieces, the one that drops most first (`#n track (x, y) to (x, y) on <layer>, <width> mm wide, ...: <mV> (<share> of the drop), needs <w> mm`; `track_drops[].path` in JSON, `track` the index in the board's copper): when one narrow piece tops the list, widen only that track and leave the rest of the rail as it is. Its last section, `quality`, counts for each net the defects the check does not see (own-net crossings, duplicate ways, unneeded copper such as dead stubs, nudges and overlaps, tracks shorter than two widths, vias of one net side by side), worst first, and `board drc` notes the totals: run `routes tidy` for the duplicates and the unneeded copper, re-route or redraw a net with crossings, and use `netsmith board nets --gate` (with `--max-<kind> N` where a count is allowed) as the check that the copper is clean. Its first line, `objective:`, is the router's quality objective: the nets' routed length over their straight length, vias, layer changes and the nets over twice their straight length. When those are poor on a board that routes, route again with weights, `board fab --via-weight 45` (fewer vias; the router's own is 15 mm of track a via), `--length-weight 2` (shorter tracks) or `--layer-change-weight MM` (on `routes route` too, for one net); any weight also runs the polish pass, which routes each signal connection again on the finished board and keeps cheaper copper, and the reroute pass, which takes the nets that detour most (over 1.25 times their straight length) with the nets in their way, lays them again with the worst first and keeps that only when the total by the weights gets shorter with every connection made (`fab` says `rerouted net ...`). For one net that loops round its neighbour in the routes file, `netsmith routes route N --reroute --rip M --dry-run` lays N and M again, N first, and says both totals; without `--dry-run` it writes the change only when it is shorter. To keep them for the project, state `length_weight`, `via_weight` and `layer_change_weight` in `netsmith.toml`: `board fab`, `project order`, `routes route` and the editor's routing then route with them (a flag wins over its key for one run), so an order routes the board as `fab` did. Compare the `objective:` lines before and after; without a weight the router routes as before. To judge a round of hand routing or tidying, copy the routes file first (`cp src/main.routes.toml before.routes.toml`, or save `board nets --format json` to a `.json` file), change the copper, then `netsmith board nets --compare before.routes.toml --gate` prints each row (signal length, short tracks, layer changes, via pairs, vias, tracks, connections left, track drops over budget, design rules errors) before, after and the change, and exits 1 when a row got worse (`--tolerance PCT` allows that much; `--max-<kind> N` still holds the defect counts): keep the round only when it passes, never re-derive the numbers by script. A `power net ...:` line follows for each net whose current needs vias side by side and that no pour fills: the router keeps such a net to one layer where it can (a change of layer costs it each via of the group) and changes layer through one group sized for its current; a `group(s) short` there is a place where the group did not fit, which `board drc` names as `via_current`: give the place room, or add the vias with `routes add-via --count N`.
9. **Route again**: `netsmith board fab --reuse-routes` starts from the router's kept copper (the editor writes it; it says how many nets it kept and routed again), so a change to one net does not route the whole board. Plain `fab` routes every net and gives the same board on every machine: use it for the last run, and an order never takes the flag. Add `--save-placed` only to give `netsmith dev route` a board.
10. **After a part moved**: `fab` warns `the pads of net N are not where the routes file's record was written` and keeps the record's copper; `open at J1.2 (track 3's end at X, Y)` names each track end or via that now runs into nothing, by the pad nearest it, and the router routes the connections left. Read `board drc` for a short where a moved pad landed on kept copper, delete the shapes that ran to the moved pad (`routes delete --net N --track 3`) or draw them to its new place, then `routes lock --net N` to write the record again. Lock is refused only for an error of the record's copper where it ran to the moved pad (an error of the net's copper within about a pad's size of where the warning says it is open), which it lists; other findings of that net or of other nets do not stop it, and `board drc` still reports them.
11. **Stop and ask** when the fix is a change of the board, not of copper: more layers, a larger outline, a part moved off a place the user fixed, a requirement or pin the user states, a rule loosened (`--allow-drc-violations` is never yours to use for an order), or when two attempts at the same connection fail the same way.

## Not for this skill

`netsmith dev route` routes a saved placed board with the router's tuning flags (`--tries`, `--stages`, `--arcs`): it is the tool for working on the router, never for finishing a board. `netsmith routes import BOARD.kicad_pcb` takes copper from a KiCad board (every net locked; the port skill, `netsmith-port-a-kicad-board`, says when). Run it with `--format json` and read its `nets`: each has `locked`, the `tracks`, `vias` and `zones` taken and `refusals`, the design rules check's errors of that net's own copper (`rule`, `at`, `shapes`); `skipped` says what was left out: with `--unlocked` it leaves out a pour's stitching vias, which the next `fab` lays again on the same grid, and with every net locked it keeps them (`fab` lays no copper on a locked net). Either way a round of `fab` and `routes import` does not add a second grid of vias beside the first. The KiCad board's zones come with the copper (its hand zones, with their settings, as `routes add-zone` writes them); the zones of the process's planes and pours that `fab` wrote are left out, also when they were changed in KiCad since (the uuid `fab` gives them says so), since the process draws them: change the pour in the source, or pass `--process-zones` to keep them as hand zones. The text counts the zones (`zones: 1 taken, 3 left out (2 of the process's own planes and pours, 1 rule area(s))`) and the JSON names each left out in `zones_left_out` (`net`, `layers`, `reason`, `said`); a rule area is never taken (the routes file holds copper only), so state a keepout in the source. The import's JSON names each via it left out (`stitching_vias_left_out`), and `routes show` counts a net's stitching vias that `fab` lays apart from the file's (`stitching`), so the vias of a net, the file's and those, are the same whether it was imported locked or not. `--no-places` imports only copper: it writes no place and prints no per-part note of how a part is placed (turned, moved, pads numbered the other way round), only `places not imported: N` (JSON: `places_not_imported`, and `refitted` empty); the notes about copper stay. A locked net's copper cannot be deleted, tidied or routed round until it is unlocked, so when nets have refusals, or the router must finish what the import leaves, import with `--unlocked` and lock only the nets the person wants kept (`routes lock --net N`) once their copper is clean.
