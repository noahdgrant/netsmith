# Route a board

The router draws copper for every connection the routes file does not make. The **routes file**, `<stem>.routes.toml` beside the source, holds the copper a person, an agent or an import decided: tracks, vias and zones of nets, each shape optionally locked. Only `netsmith routes` writes it, and it is committed with the source. The router's own copper is a cache (`.netsmith/routes/`, never committed): `fab` never writes it into the routes file. Where the part stands is the layout file (`netsmith-use-layout`); the format, the refusals and the rip-up rounds are in the guide's "The routes file" and "Connections left: rounds of rip-up" (`docs/language/23-the-shape-of-a-board.md`). `netsmith routes --help` and each subcommand's help have the flags.

## Loop

1. **Route and read what is left**: `netsmith board fab`. A board the router could not finish still gets its files in `fab/`, marked `INCOMPLETE`, and the run ends in the error `N connections could not be routed`; never order from it. Read `fab/<board>/routing-failure.txt` (each connection left with its net and two pads, the stage of the router it last failed in, and what stands in its way: the nets whose copper a way across would cross, or the pads, vias and board edge that wall an end in) and `routing-report.json` (the same as data, with a crowding map: 1 mm squares, `crowding.counts` row by row from `crowding.origin`; the highest squares are where parts need more room). `netsmith routes show --format json` lists the routes file's records, numbered shapes, what of it is not used and the connections still to route. It routes nothing.
2. **Decide who owns the copper.** A shape is the person's when they drew it, a drawing or a mechanical fit fixes it, or it is a net the router must not touch (a sense line, a power path). `routes lock --net N` stops the router adding copper to that net (a connection it still lacks is left, and `fab` says so); `--track N`, `--via N` or `--zone N` keep one shape from `routes delete`, `routes tidy` and the editor's drags. Lock nothing the person did not ask for: the router's copper is the router's. There is no command that copies router copper into the file; draw what must be kept.
3. **Read the failure before drawing.** The stage and the thing in the way say which fix is cheap:
   - a pad walled in by its neighbours, or `pad(s) U1.12, ...` named as the wall: move a part or give it room (`netsmith-use-layout`, `layout fit`; a `.routing_margin` on the board or the busy part makes the placer and `layout fit` keep room round its courtyard), or narrow the process's track and space within the factory's limits. `fab` already warns `pad N of ... is walled in by ...` after placing, before routing, and `layout check` says it as `walled_in`: act on that warning before a long route;
   - other nets' copper in the way and a crowded square: more room round those parts, a layer more, or a plane or pour for a net that crosses everything;
   - `stopped after the route timeout`: raise `board fab --route-timeout SECS` (0 is no limit); the `Board: routing: ...` lines on stderr say what each try ended with;
   - nothing left to give: `--rip-rounds N` sets the rounds of rip-up of whole nets for a run (`rip_rounds` in `netsmith.toml` for the project, 0 turns them off); try more before drawing by hand.
   - a net of a connection that only other pins would shorten (a GPIO function): `netsmith board pins show`, and `board fab --pins auto|locked`; `netsmith-read-errors` has the pin messages.
4. **Route one net at a time** before drawing: `netsmith routes route N` (or one connection, `N:U1.3-C2.1`) routes it with the router round the copper there and writes its tracks and vias to its record, unlocked; the text says the narrowest place of the way and the widest track that has room there. Run it with `--dry-run` first: it writes nothing. When the net has no way it names the nets whose copper stands in the way (exit 1, nothing written), or what walls it in when no net's copper does (pads, vias, a locked net's copper, the edge). `--rip A,B` lets it remove the unlocked tracks and vias of those nets, route the net through the room, and route them again after it; ask before ripping copper the person drew, and read the result with `routes show`. A locked net or shape is never ripped, nor is a net a pour or a plane joins (GND on a plane): the failure text never lists one in a `--rip` list, and `--rip` of one is refused as having no copper to rip, so go round it, move a part or draw by hand; and a result that would leave a ripped net more open or add a finding of the design rules check is refused.
5. **Draw by hand** what is left, one shape at a time, in the placement's frame (mm from the outline's top left corner, x right, y down, as `layout set`):
   - `netsmith routes add-track --net N --layer top --from X,Y --to X,Y [--mid X,Y for an arc] [--width MM] [--lock]`; `--layer` is `top`, `bottom` or an inner signal layer; the width defaults to the net's own;
   - `routes add-via --net N --at X,Y [--layers top,inner1] [--lock]` (the process's via by default); a net whose current needs several vias side by side (`via_current`) takes them as one group, `--at X,Y` repeated or `--count N --pitch MM` round one `--at`, checked together (a pitch above the via's diameter leaves them apart);
   - `routes add-zone --net N --layer bottom --rect X,Y,W,H` (or `--outline X,Y` per corner, or `--board-outline`; `--pads`, `--clearance`, `--priority`). A zone of another net cuts the design's pour on its layer (the pour fills round it, as in KiCad); a zone of the pour's own net replaces the pour there (restate it with `--board-outline` to keep it whole). The command says which. Prefer stating a plane or pour in the source (`netsmith-write-nsm`) over a zone, unless the user wants a hand shape.
   - a pad the fill leaves unjoined or starved (`board drc`'s `unjoinable_thermal` or `starved_thermal`, `board fill`'s `unjoinable` and `starved`): `netsmith routes join-pad REF.PAD [--dry-run]` draws the shortest track of its net from the pad into the fill (16 directions, at most 5 mm), or a track and a via into the fill on another layer, checked as `add-track` is; refused with why the shortest was refused when none fits. Do not script a direction from `board fill --format json` (its `outline` has each fill's pieces and holes, for a look at the room). Where the person wants that pad (or a part's ground pads, as beside an RF pin) joined solid, or with spokes of another width, state it on the pin in the source instead (`.gnd.pour_connection &= PadConnection::Solid`, `.gnd.spoke_width &= 0.3mm..`; the guide's "A pad's own connection"): the fill and the board file take it over the pour's, and `board fill` names the pin.
   - **many shapes at once** (a route of tens of segments, a script's output, more than a handful of shapes): write them to one document and run `netsmith routes add shapes.toml --format json` (or `-` with the document on stdin; with the MCP server, the tool `routes_add` with `shapes` the document as a JSON object), not one `add-track` a shape: `[[track]]`, `[[via]]` and `[[zone]]` tables with the fields of the verbs' flags (`net`, `layer`, `from = [x, y]`, `to`, `width`; `at` for a via, a list of places for a group; `rect`, `outline` or `board_outline = true` for a zone). Each shape is checked in turn (tracks, then vias, then zones) on the board with the ones before it, and the file is written once. A refused shape is skipped, and its entry in `shapes` has `result = "refused"`, `said` and `findings` (each `rule`, `at`, `shapes`): move that shape and send only the refused ones again. Use `--stop-on-refusal` when a later shape depends on an earlier one (a track to a via), and `--dry-run` to check a document before writing.
   Take pad places from `netsmith layout parts --format json`, not from the picture. An end that touches nothing yet is allowed, since a route is drawn a shape at a time; `fab` finds one left so. End a track inside the pad it joins (on its centre, or at least on its edge): a round end that only touches the pad's edge is not joined, as in KiCad, and `board drc` says `it only touches`. A command exits 0 when it wrote, 1 when it **refused** and 2 when it could not run. Read a refusal: it names a short, a crossing or a clearance with the place, and the file is as it was. Fix the shape; never loosen a rule to get it in. `routes delete` removes a net's record or one numbered shape, and refuses a locked one: ask before `routes unlock`. A `fab` warning that the routes file has a net the board does not have (a net renamed) is answered by `routes delete --net NAME`, with the name it prints: that record is deleted whole, locked or not, and the command says what it deleted; rename the net in the file by hand instead to keep its copper.
5a. **RF lines and nets on one layer** (issue #1274): a net of a `TrackImpedance` is an RF net, placed first and short and routed first, on one outer layer (the one all its pads are on) with no via; `.pin route one_layer` in the physical file does the same for any net, and `.pin route any_layer` lets an RF net change layers. Such a net with no way on its layer is left, never given a via: give it room on that layer (move the parts in its way, `netsmith-use-layout`), or ask the user before writing `route any_layer`. A via or a second layer on such a net, in the routes file or a board from elsewhere, is a `[constraint] ... route one_layer ... is violated` finding of `fab` and `board drc`: delete that copper (`routes delete --net N`) and route it again. Never add a via to one with `routes add-via`.
5b. **A line through parts in series** (issue #1275): a `TrackPath` (`.first` and `.last` on the two end pins, `.impedance`) gives each net of its chain (a DC block, a 0 ohm link between) the target, so they are RF nets as above. `board nets` lists `path NAME: ...` with the chain's length against its `.budget_length`, and `board drc` and `fab-notes.txt` say a path over it (`track path: ...`): shorten it by moving the path's parts nearer each other (`netsmith-use-layout`), never by loosening the budget. A `.fence` puts ground vias beside its tracks after routing; `fab-notes.txt` says when no pour or plane gave them a ground. Given `.frequency`, its tracks' loss is worked out against `.budget_loss` and said in the same places, when the build-up has a loss tangent (`.process.stackup.loss_tangent`, from the laminate's datasheet: no standard build-up states one, and then the notes say why there is no figure); a path over it is shortened, or its tracks widened on a thicker dielectric, never the budget loosened.
6. **Tidy copper you did not draw shape by shape** (an import, a script, many hand edits): `netsmith routes tidy --dry-run` lists, by kind, net, layer and place, the corners it would cut to 45 degrees (`track_angle`), the collinear pairs it would make one, the dangling vias and stubs it would remove (a stub only on a net the copper finishes), the `acid_trap`s it would remove, and the defects `board drc` does not name: nudges (a track from a pad's or via's centre that ends still inside it), the longer of two ways between the same two places of a net, tracks that overlap on one line or lie inside another, and runs of tiny tracks round a corner, and with `--widen` the tracks narrower than their net wants (its current, its class, its impedance target) that it would widen, each as far as the clearance to its neighbours allows; `netsmith routes tidy` makes them (`--format json` for the same as data; `--reuse-routes` tidies the router's kept copper too). It never touches a locked net or shape, and it refuses a change that adds any finding of `board drc` or changes the connections left: read the refused list, each with the finding it would add, and fix those by hand or leave them. On a big board it says on stderr how far each pass has got, net by net, with the time it has left (`--progress json` for lines a program reads); Ctrl+C leaves the files as they were. Tidy before you lock what you keep.
7. **Check without routing**: `netsmith board drc` (`--format json` has the rule, place, nets and parts of each finding; exit 1 on an error) judges the board as it stands, the placement with the routes file's copper, and every connection the file does not make is an `unconnected_items` error, so run it to check a hand-drawn shape, not to see what the router would do. `routes show` says the connections still to route. Its notes (and `fab-notes.txt`) list the nets routed far over their straight length (`routed long:`) and the nets with the most vias (`most vias:`): look at each that must be short. A note `pair skew:` names a diff pair whose two lines differ by more than its limit (its `.skew`, else the router's 0.1 mm), and `length group:` a group of nets matched in length whose spread is over its tolerance.
8. **Measure the lines**: `netsmith board nets` (`--route` to route first, `--lengths` longest first, `--format json`) gives each net's routed length (the copper from pad to pad: a second way of one connection and a dead stub are left out and named on a `net ...: ... not counted` line; remove them from the board) and straight length, vias, layer changes and layers, and each diff pair's two lengths, skew against the pair's limit (its `.skew`, else 0.1 mm), coupled length and share, least and largest gap, and vias side by side, from the copper `view pcb` draws, by the router's own measure. The router routes a pair (named `dp`/`dm`, a `PairImpedance`, a `DiffPair`, a `Can`'s or a `Usb2`'s lines) as one object: pulled from its pads to one pitch (the stated spacing, else the nets' clearance), its vias side by side, the shorter line lengthened by a meander to the pair's `.skew` (a board narrows it, `.lines.skew &= 0mm..0.05mm`; a `Can` offers 2.5 mm). A pair is routed round copper already there, and `netsmith routes route` of the second line of a pair runs it beside the first's copper at the pitch (route one line, then the other); when a board's tries leave connections, the router tries the pairs as two separate lines and `fab` says so (`routed as two separate lines`). A pair over its skew or barely coupled has no room: give it room (move the parts that sit between its lines, or the pads that make it cross), or draw it by hand as a pair, both lines at the pitch and each via beside its partner's. A `LengthGroup`'s nets (`.lane.lines[0]` up, its `.tolerance`) are lengthened towards the longest before the pairs; `board nets` lists each group's lengths and spread, and a group over its tolerance has no room for its meanders: give the shorter nets straight runs.
9. **Route again**: `netsmith board fab --reuse-routes` starts from the router's kept copper (the editor writes it; it says how many nets it kept and routed again), so a change to one net does not route the whole board. Plain `fab` routes every net and gives the same board on every machine: use it for the last run, and an order never takes the flag. Add `--save-placed` only to give `netsmith dev route` a board.
10. **After a part moved**: `fab` warns `the pads of net N are not where the routes file's record was written` and keeps the record's copper; `open at J1.2 (track 3's end at X, Y)` names each track end or via that now runs into nothing, by the pad nearest it, and the router routes the connections left. Read `board drc` for a short where a moved pad landed on kept copper, delete the shapes that ran to the moved pad (`routes delete --net N --track 3`) or draw them to its new place, then `routes lock --net N` to write the record again. Lock is refused only for an error of that net's own copper, which it lists; other nets' findings do not stop it.
11. **Stop and ask** when the fix is a change of the board, not of copper: more layers, a larger outline, a part moved off a place the user fixed, a requirement or pin the user states, a rule loosened (`--allow-drc-violations` is never yours to use for an order), or when two attempts at the same connection fail the same way.

## Look

There is no picture of the copper by itself yet (issue #1086). `netsmith view 3d <file.nsm> --view top --picture board.png`, with `--section AXIS=MM` to cut it, shows the board, and `netsmith board copper` sums up the planes, pours and zones by layer. Quote numbers from `routing-failure.txt`, `routing-report.json`, `routes show` and `board drc`, not from a picture.

## Not for this skill

`netsmith dev route` routes a saved placed board with the router's tuning flags (`--tries`, `--stages`, `--arcs`): it is the tool for working on the router, never for finishing a board. `netsmith routes import BOARD.kicad_pcb` takes copper from a KiCad board (every net locked; the port skill, `netsmith-port-a-kicad-board`, says when). Run it with `--format json` and read its `nets`: each has `locked`, the `tracks`, `vias` and `zones` taken and `refusals`, the design rules check's errors of that net's own copper (`rule`, `at`, `shapes`); `skipped` says what was left out. A locked net's copper cannot be deleted, tidied or routed round until it is unlocked, so when nets have refusals, or the router must finish what the import leaves, import with `--unlocked` and lock only the nets the person wants kept (`routes lock --net N`) once their copper is clean.

## Report

Tell the user which connections were left and why (the stage and what was in the way), what you changed (a part moved, a `--rip-rounds` or timeout, each shape drawn with its net, layer and place, what `routes tidy` changed and refused, each lock and why), what `board drc` and the last plain `fab` say, and whatever is still open. A board `fab` marks `INCOMPLETE` is not ready to order, and `netsmith-prepare-fab-order` is the next step only when it is not.
