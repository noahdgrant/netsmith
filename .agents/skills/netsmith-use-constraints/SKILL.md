description: State where parts must be on a board (a case, a panel, a carrier) as requirements in the board's physical file, run fab, and read the constraint findings. Use when a user gives positions, distances from an edge, rotations or a face for parts, or when fab reports `[constraint]`.

# Use constraints

A requirement on where a part goes is a line on the part's built-in `.place` (ADR 0045), and it is written in the board's **physical file**, `<stem>.physical.nsm` beside the source, in the unit `physical for Board { ... }` (ADR 0048); the same line in the board itself is an error that names the file. The placer keeps to it, the layout file keeps the solved position, and `netsmith board fab` checks it and stops when it is not met. The language guide's sections "The physical file" and "Where a part goes" have the fields and the real errors.

## Loop

1. **Get the numbers from the user's drawing.** Ask for the case, panel or carrier drawing, or for the numbers. Never invent one: an unstated requirement is better than a guessed one. Put the source in a comment above each line (`// Case drawing, slot A: ...`). A DXF of the case gives the outline, cutouts and mounting holes with `netsmith import dxf` (see `netsmith-design-a-board`), but not the requirements on parts: read those off the drawing yourself. A STEP model or a PDF is read with tools outside netsmith; turn it into numbers (millimetres, from the board's top left corner, y down) and write those. A STEP file of the case may also be named as a case model in the `case { }` section (`.shell: CaseModel { .file &= "case/box.step" }`, see the guide's _A case model_), for the fit check and the 3D view; it gives no outline, hole or requirement by itself (`netsmith import case model box.step` prints the outline, cutouts and mounting holes from the model's section, and with `--limits` the height limits over and under the board as `Keepout`s with `.max_height`, for you to review against the drawing before you paste it), so write the numbers anyway. Place it in the board's frame with `.x`, `.y`, `.z` and `.rx`, `.ry`, `.rz` (for a pose given as a matrix, a STEP assembly's transform or a CAD matrix, `netsmith import case place` prints the six lines and refuses a mirror) until `dev board3d --format json` shows no `case_collision` of the board itself (the board on its bosses is contact, not a collision).
2. **Give the board a fixed outline** in the `case { }` section of the physical file when a requirement is measured against its size: `case { .outline: Outline { .width &= 62mm; .height &= 30mm } }`. Positions are from the outline's top left corner. A requirement written with `.outline.height` on an outline that is a range is refused. When several boards go in one case, write what the case fixes once, as `enclosure Name { ... }` in a file of `src/` of its own (the contents of a `case { }` section, unchanged), and put each board in it with `case Name` in its unit, after `import src::<file>::Name`; what one board adds stays in the unit's own `case { }` section (the guide's _A shared enclosure_, ADR 0132).
3. **Write the lines** inside `physical for Board { ... }`. `.<part>.place.<field>` where the field is `x`, `y` (the centre of the part's body), `left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom` (the body's edges), `rotation` (degrees: any angle, 45 included; the placer itself turns by right angles only, unless the part states `.place.turn_step &= 45`, which lets it try the eight steps of 45 degrees) or `side` (`"top"` or `"bottom"`).

   ```nsm-fragment
   // Case drawing, slot A: the receptacle's shell is centred and its mouth sticks out 0.5 mm.
   .usb.place.left     &= -0.5mm +/- 0.05mm
   .usb.place.y        >= .outline.height / 2 - 0.1mm
   .usb.place.y        <= .outline.height / 2 + 0.1mm
   .usb.place.rotation &= 270
   .usb.place.side     &= "top"
   ```

   The body is the outline the footprint draws on `F.Fab`, else its courtyard. A tolerance (`+/-`) needs a constant on both sides; a window round an expression is two bounds (`>=`, `<=`).
4. **A part inside a module** is named by its path in the physical file: `.block.j.place.y &= 8mm +/- 0.2mm`. A path may cross a module boundary there only to state `.place` or `.height`; anything else (a value, a connection) is refused with "is not physical intent". A module's own `.place.left`, `.right`, `.top`, `.bottom` bound the box of all its parts, and `.block.place.side &= "bottom"` puts every part of it on that face (a part that needs the other face is stated part by part, and the module states none). A board assembled on one face only states it once with no path, `.place.side &= "top"` (or `"bottom"`), which puts every part of the board on that face; a part or module on the other face is then an error that names both lines, so a board with exceptions states no face for itself. A relation between two parts (a pitch from a datasheet, an aligned pair) is the equation `.b.place.x - .a.place.x == 17.5mm +/- 0.05mm` or `.a.place.y == .b.place.y`, along one axis, between parts (not modules) and not a turn; the placer moves the two parts together until it holds and `fab` measures it (provisional, ADR 0045 section 12). Two parts the layout file locks or the requirements pin, with a relation they break, are refused before placing with the numbers. A numbered row of parts on a pitch (test pads, a bank of switches) is one statement, `row .pads.tp1 .. .pads.tp12 along x pitch 2.54mm +/- 0.05mm`, in place of two equations for each pad: it puts the run on one line with each neighbour at the pitch (owner decision 2026-11-04, ADR 0048), a finding names its line and the two neighbours, and a gap in the numbers, an end that is not a number, a first number above the last or parts of two modules are refused with the statement quoted.
4b. **A part near a pin** (an antenna connector by a radio's antenna pin, a crystal by its oscillator pins: a datasheet's or an RF layout's "within N mm") is `.antenna within 8mm of .radio.ant` (issue #1274): a part (or a pin of a part), `within`, one length, `of`, a pin of another part. It is measured pad centre to pad centre from the part's nearest pad on the pin's net (any pad when none is on it) to the pin's nearest pad; the placer holds it as a hard rule and `fab` reports it as `[constraint] ... within 8 mm of radio.ant ... is violated: the nearest of pad 1 of J2 is 19.5 mm from pad 1 of U1 ...` (`measure` `distance` in the JSON). The number comes from the datasheet or the user; say where in a comment. A line that must run on one layer with no via is `.radio.ant route one_layer` in the same file (`netsmith-route-a-board`); a net of a `TrackImpedance` is placed short and routed on one layer by default.
4a. **Geometry that is the module's** (a datasheet's row spacing, two parts on one line) goes in the module's own physical file, so every board that uses it gets it: the module names it with `physical "<stem>.physical.nsm"` (the file beside its source), and the file holds `physical for Module { ... }` with relations whose places cancel (`.b.place.x - .a.place.x == 17.5mm +/- 0.05mm`, a `row`), never a position (`&=` on a `.place` is refused there). `anchor .dock = .row_a` there gives a part a name the board's file can use (`.radio.dock.place.left`), so a rename in the module breaks one line. A board's file may only narrow the module's windows; a wider or disjoint one names both lines (guide, "A module's own physical file").
4b. **A distance kept free round a part** (a module's design note: 3 mm round it for soldering and rework, room for an antenna) is a soft requirement, not a position: `soft margin 3mm round .radio.u1` in the physical file (issue 1310). The distance is one fixed length from the part's courtyard, the part is named with a leading dot (a part, not a module), and a part states one. The placer keeps other parts' courtyards out of it where it can; where it could not, `fab` warns (`R2 is 1.20 mm from R1, courtyard to courtyard, and the soft margin round R1 asks for 3 mm (widget.physical.nsm:9)`), an error with `--strict` (it stops `fab` after placing), and `layout check` says it as `soft_margin`. Take the number from the design note and quote it in a comment above the line; never write one you were not given. It is no `.routing_margin` (a hard rule between courtyards) and no `Keepout`. A warning left standing is for the user to decide: tell them which margin the placer could not keep.
4c. **A distance between kinds of parts** (a project's rule of thumb where no datasheet gives a number: a radio module and a switching regulator, a crystal and a buck converter) is `keep 10mm between BuckIc and Crystal` in the board's physical file (issue 1277), the two kinds imported there: a part or a generic of std or of the project, which reaches every part made from it or providing it (`std::parts::regulator::BuckIc`, `std::parts::passive::Crystal`; a project with no generic for its radio declares one). It is a hard requirement: the placer keeps every part of one kind that far from every part of the other, courtyard to courtyard in the plane of the board whatever faces they are on, and `fab` stops after placing where it could not (`requirement `keep 10mm between BuckIc and Crystal` (widget.physical.nsm:9) is violated: `u2` (U2) is 4.2 mm from `y1` (Y1), courtyard to courtyard, and it asks for 10 mm (5.8 mm short); ...`, in `requirements` of `--format json` with `measure` `distance`); `layout check` says it as a `requirement` of two locked parts. Write only a rule the user or the project gives, and quote its source in a comment; a rule that reaches no two parts is a note. Not built: a distance by face, a keepout band between kinds.
5. **A part that hangs over the board edge** (a plug that sticks out of the case) is allowed only where the requirement says so: a window for the body's own `left`, `right`, `top` or `bottom` that is at or beyond the same edge of the outline (`.plug.place.bottom >= .outline.height`, or `.usb.place.left &= -0.5mm +/- 0.05mm`). A window on `x` or `y` does not say it, and neither does a bound on a module. A part the layout file locks must state every edge it is past, like any other part: a lock does not say it. `fab` lists each stated overhang as a warning. The rule is the owner's decision of 2026-11-03 (ADR 0045, section 10; the part about modules is still open): tell the user which overhangs you stated. The pads must still be inside the outline and the process's copper to edge space away from it, and the body is not checked.
6. **A height limit** (a lid rib, a bracket): a `Keepout` in the physical file over the area with `.max_height &= 3mm` and `.layers &= "top"` (or `"bottom"`; two keepouts give the two faces their own limits), and `.height` on each part whose height the user or the datasheet gives (on the part; the physical file may narrow it). A part picked from a catalog row with a `height` cell (a datasheet figure, its source in the row; `fab-notes.txt` names those rows) needs no `.height`, and a `.height` you state wins over the row. A board of a system needs no keepout for the board mated on it: each mate gives each of its boards one, `System/member over connector`, carried by its connector, over the other board's outline with the least mated height as its limit (guide, _The area another board covers_, issue #1331). It is known before placing only when the other board's connector is locked in its layout file or pinned by its physical file; otherwise `fab` says so in a `note:`, and the system scene still checks after placing. Never write that keepout by hand. Never write a height you do not have: a part with none is reported by name as unchecked, which is correct; a project's own catalog row may carry the `height` (ADR 0094). A part that swells over its life (a cell, a supercapacitor) states `.height_growth &= 0.8mm` from its datasheet: the check adds it to the height before it compares the height with a limit, and the finding says `... tall on the top face when grown (its 2.50 mm height with its 0.80 mm `.height_growth` added)`. Never state a growth you have not read. A module on sockets or spacers whose maker allows only low parts under its body (an under-height rule), where the footprint's outline covers only the socket rows, states the body's area in the part's definition or its use's block: `.underside_keepout: UndersideKeepout { .x &= -10mm; .y &= -5mm; .width &= 20mm; .height &= 10mm; .max_height &= 0.5mm }` (`import std::pcb::UndersideKeepout`; the part's own frame, or `polygon (x, y) ...` of straight edges in place of the four fields, issue #1335). It limits the other parts on the module's face under it, and, on a connector of a system's mate, the other board's parts under it; the finding names both (`... and the underside keepout of `module` allows 0.50 mm there`). Take the area and the figure from the datasheet's drawing, never guess them. This is provisional (ADR 0045, section 11): tell the user you used it.
7. **Pins that protrude** (a flat part on one face over the pins of a through-hole part that come through from the other): the part's `.pin_protrusion` (a length, ADR 0045 section 15) in the physical file, for example `.radio.row_a.pin_protrusion &= 0.2mm`, says how far the pins protrude out of the far side, whether they are cut after soldering or bought short. The part over them must be fitted by hand (`.assembly &= AssemblyMode::Hand` in the circuit source), state its `.height`, and `protrusion + height` must fit the height limit on its face there; `fab` then writes a `trim:` line. **Never state a protrusion to make a finding go away**: cutting the pins is a step a person does, so state it only when the user or the drawing says the pins are cut or short, and say you did. Provisional (ADR 0045 section 14).
8. Run `netsmith board build`, then `netsmith board fab`. Read the first error only.
9. **Check heights in 3D as data** before or after `fab`: `netsmith dev board3d <file.nsm> --format json` (no KiCad, writes nothing, `--board` for a file of several boards) places the board as `fab` does and gives, for each height limit, the tallest part under it and the `margin` (negative is too tall), `unknown_heights` with the reason for each, `overlaps` of bodies on one face, each part's `z_bottom`/`z_top` and `provenance` (`stated`, `catalog`, `unknown`), and `coverage`. An unknown height is `null`, never zero: ask for the number. `docs/BOARD3D.md` has the frame (origin at the outline's bottom-left corner, y up, z = 0 on the top face) and every field.
    Locked parts and the physical file: `netsmith layout check` (no KiCad, nothing placed) says which locked entries of `<stem>.pcb.toml` break a requirement, and which entries `fab`'s placement check refuses (overlaps, the edge, keepouts), with the entry's line, and `netsmith layout set <path> --at X,Y --lock` writes an entry and warns when it does.
10. **Look at the board**: `netsmith view 3d <file.nsm> --view iso --picture board.png` draws the same placed scene to a PNG (no KiCad, no GPU, the same bytes every run) and you read the file with your image viewer. `--highlight J1` (a part's designator or path), `--highlight limit:lid` (a height limit's volume, red where a part is too tall) or `--highlight net:GND` light up what you ask about and dim the rest; `--section x=12.5` cuts the scene at 12.5 mm (frame as in `docs/BOARD3D.md`, "Pictures for agents"); `--view top|bottom|front|back|left|right|iso|iso-bottom`. Use it to see that a part sits where the requirement says and under the limit, and quote the numbers from `dev board3d --format json`, not from the picture. With the editor open on the project, `netsmith view editor-3d --view top --highlight limit:lid --picture lid.png` does the same in the page the user is looking at (same flags; `--section`, `--highlight` and `--xray` move their view, so they see what you see), and `netsmith view editor-3d --format json` reads back what it shows; it needs a page open and exits 2 with the reason when none is (see `docs/BOARD3D.md`, "Driving a running editor").

## Reading a finding

```text
error [constraint] constraint usb.place.left (widget.physical.nsm:14) is violated: the left edge of the body of J1 (F.Fab) is 0.18 mm from the left edge of the outline, and it allows -0.55 to -0.45 mm (0.73 mm over)
```

It names the line to change, the part, the drawing it measured, the measured value, the window, and how far over. A finding for a relation names both parts, by designator and path, and the difference between them ("the centre of the body of J4 (radio.row_b, F.Fab) minus the centre of the body of J3 (radio.row_a, F.Fab) is 16 mm along x, and it allows 17.45 to 17.55 mm (1.45 mm over)"). `netsmith board fab --format json` has the same as data in `requirements` of the finding: `id`, `source`, `parts`, `measure`, `unit`, `measured`, the window as `min` and `max` (left out where open) and `over` (a face has `side` and `allowed_side`); read the numbers there instead of from the sentence. The lines that cannot all hold are in `conflicts` (`kind`, `lines` with each `source`, `text`, the window it allows as `measure`, `unit`, `min` and `max` (or `allowed_side`), or the layout file's `lock`, `parts` with the box each courtyard lies within, `overlap`), `netsmith board build --format json` has them in findings of code `place` (a lock that breaks a requirement also `requirements` and `placement`); also a courtyard past an edge nobody stated (`past_edge`, with `past`) and a part bigger than the outline (`too_big`, with `turns`), proved before placing; a `lock` that breaks a requirement gives `requirements` and `placement` of the board as the lock leaves it; and the placement check's faults where the placer ended in `placement` (`kind` such as `overlap` or `hole_too_near`, `parts` with their courtyards, the numbers); `dev board3d --format json` has both, and for a layout file the placer refuses it reports the board with the file's places and `placement_refused` (exit 1). Change the range if you misread the drawing, or change the circuit. Never edit the layout file to hide a failure: the check measures the layout file's positions too, and a part `lock`ed there against a requirement is refused before placing ("is locked in the layout file ... unlock it and let the placer move it, or change the requirement"). A message that says no position meets the requirements names each one with its numbers: two lines that cannot both hold, or a window narrower than the part.

A part too tall for a height limit is ```tall` (R2) stands 9.00 mm tall on the top face, and the keepout `ribs` allows 3.00 mm there ...``; ``height of `r3` (R3) is unchecked`` is a warning that the part states no height (an error with `constraints = "error"` written in the manifest or `board fab --constraints error`; `--allow-drc-violations` never waives it): ask for the number, do not invent it. A part too tall stops `fab` at placement; when a person must look at the board before the limit or the part changes, `board fab --for-review` writes the files to look at with the finding listed under `FOR REVIEW` in `fab-notes.txt` (never an order: `netsmith-prepare-fab-order`). `board fab` and `dev board3d` also say one `height_unknown` warning that lists such parts, the one with the least room under its limit first and each naming the limit that makes it tight (state that part's `.height` first), and the body of a module on pin strips that overlaps another part, with the `.standoff` it lacks; neither stops `fab`.

A case model's fit (`case_*` in `dev board3d --format json`'s `case_findings`, in `fab`'s output and `fab-notes.txt`): ``case_collision: J1 (`tall`), 9.00 mm tall (stated), is inside the solid `lid` of the case model `lid` by 1.00 mm ...`` stops `fab` whatever the flags say: move or lower the part, or fix the model's placement if the board itself collides. ``case_collision: the pins of J2 (`edge`), stated to stand 5.00 mm out of the far face, ...`` is a part's `.pin_protrusion` into the case below: cut the pins shorter (and state it), or move the part off the boss or rib. `case_clearance`, `case_reach` (a connector at the edge behind the wall, or too deep in its opening) and `case_boss` (a mounting hole nothing holds) are warnings to read with the user, never to silence by widening `.clearance` or `.reach` without their drawing. `case_unchecked` is a part with no height under the case, a standard height that collides, or a shell that is not closed: state the height.

A violated requirement stops `fab`, and `--allow-drc-violations` does not waive it (it waives design rules, not requirements). Do not weaken that on your own. Only `constraints = "warn"` under `[project]` in `netsmith.toml`, or `netsmith board fab --constraints warn` for one run, lists them as warnings; `--constraints error` forces errors for one run over a manifest that says `warn`. If the user wants to see the board anyway, ask first, use the flag for that one run, and say so in your report.

A part over the pins of a through-hole part is ```cell` (U1) lies over the pins of `row` (J1), whose pins protrude 0.40 mm (widget.physical.nsm:41), and that is not allowed: ...``: too tall for the height limit (it says the protrusion that would fit), no height, or assembled by the board house. Without a stated protrusion it is the plain overlap with a sentence naming `.row.pin_protrusion`: ask whether the pins are cut or short before you state it.

A courtyard past an edge nobody stated, for a part the lines pin, is refused before the placer starts, at `fab`, `build` and in the editor's findings (ADR 0045 section 14), with "at least ... wherever its requirements put it" where the lines leave a window. It is ```plug` (J1) is not wholly inside the board outline: its courtyard is 4.00 mm past the right edge, and the design does not say it may hang over an edge; ...``. It names the edge, how far, and the line that would allow it (`.plug.place.right >= outline.width`). Do not add that line to make the error go away: ask whether the part is meant to stick out. ``a pad of `plug` (J1) is 13.00 mm outside the board edge`` is never allowed by an overhang: move the part. If the placer says `the parts need ... placing them in the outline anyway ended with ...`, the parts do not fit the outline, and the numbers say how many pairs overlap and how many parts are outside; it then lists the ten worst pairs and parts by name (area, place, edge) and names the rest. A body whose centre is off the board is fine when an edge field (`.left`, `.right`, `.top`, `.bottom`) states the overhang (provisional); the same window on `.x` or `.y` is refused.

## Report

Tell the user which requirements you wrote and from which drawing, each one `fab` reports as not met with its numbers, and the overhangs you stated (`fab` warns of each), what is not built yet: a relation whose end is a module's box or a turn, or among three places (they stay `not evaluated`), and the PCB view's line between the two parts of a relation; a part's height from a catalog or its 3D model, and a polygon outline (`import dxf` refuses an outline that is not a rectangle with equal rounded corners).
