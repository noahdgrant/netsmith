# Use constraints

A requirement on where a part goes is a line on the part's built-in `.place` (ADR 0045), and it is written in the board's **physical file**, `<stem>.physical.nsm` beside the source, in the unit `physical for Board { ... }` (ADR 0048); the same line in the board itself is an error that names the file. The placer keeps to it, the layout file keeps the solved position, and `netsmith fab` checks it and stops when it is not met. The language guide's sections "The physical file" and "Where a part goes" have the fields and the real errors.

## Loop

1. **Get the numbers from the user's drawing.** Ask for the case, panel or carrier drawing, or for the numbers. Never invent one: an unstated requirement is better than a guessed one. Put the source in a comment above each line (`// Case drawing, slot A: ...`). A DXF of the case gives the outline, cutouts and mounting holes with `netsmith dxf import` (see `netsmith-design-a-board`), but not the requirements on parts: read those off the drawing yourself. A STEP model or a PDF is read with tools outside netsmith; turn it into numbers (millimetres, from the board's top left corner, y down) and write those.
2. **Give the board a fixed outline** in the `case { }` section of the physical file when a requirement is measured against its size: `case { .outline: Outline { .width &= 62mm; .height &= 30mm } }`. Positions are from the outline's top left corner. A requirement written with `.outline.height` on an outline that is a range is refused.
3. **Write the lines** inside `physical for Board { ... }`. `.<part>.place.<field>` where the field is `x`, `y` (the centre of the part's body), `left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom` (the body's edges), `rotation` (degrees, a multiple of 90) or `side` (`"top"` or `"bottom"`).

   ```nsm-fragment
   // Case drawing, slot A: the receptacle's shell is centred and its mouth sticks out 0.5 mm.
   .usb.place.left     &= -0.5mm +/- 0.05mm
   .usb.place.y        >= .outline.height / 2 - 0.1mm
   .usb.place.y        <= .outline.height / 2 + 0.1mm
   .usb.place.rotation &= 270
   .usb.place.side     &= "top"
   ```

   The body is the outline the footprint draws on `F.Fab`, else its courtyard. A tolerance (`+/-`) needs a constant on both sides; a window round an expression is two bounds (`>=`, `<=`).
4. **A part inside a module** is named by its path in the physical file: `.block.j.place.y &= 8mm +/- 0.2mm`. A path may cross a module boundary there only to state `.place` or `.height`; anything else (a value, a connection) is refused with "is not physical intent". A module's own `.place.left`, `.right`, `.top`, `.bottom` bound the box of all its parts, and `.block.place.side &= "bottom"` puts every part of it on that face (a part that needs the other face is stated part by part, and the module states none). A relation between two parts (a pitch from a datasheet, an aligned pair) is the equation `.b.place.x - .a.place.x == 17.5mm +/- 0.05mm` or `.a.place.y == .b.place.y`, along one axis, between parts (not modules) and not a turn; the placer moves the two parts together until it holds and `fab` measures it (provisional, ADR 0045 section 12). Two parts the layout file locks or the requirements pin, with a relation they break, are refused before placing with the numbers. A numbered row of parts on a pitch (test pads, a bank of switches) is one statement, `row .pads.tp1 .. .pads.tp12 along x pitch 2.54mm +/- 0.05mm`, in place of two equations for each pad: it puts the run on one line with each neighbour at the pitch (owner decision 2026-11-04, ADR 0048), a finding names its line and the two neighbours, and a gap in the numbers, an end that is not a number, a first number above the last or parts of two modules are refused with the statement quoted.
5. **A part that hangs over the board edge** (a plug that sticks out of the case) is allowed only where the requirement says so: a window for the body's own `left`, `right`, `top` or `bottom` that is at or beyond the same edge of the outline (`.plug.place.bottom >= .outline.height`, or `.usb.place.left &= -0.5mm +/- 0.05mm`). A window on `x` or `y` does not say it, and neither does a bound on a module. A part the layout file locks must state every edge it is past, like any other part: a lock does not say it. `fab` lists each stated overhang as a warning. The rule is the owner's decision of 2026-11-03 (ADR 0045, section 10; the part about modules is still open): tell the user which overhangs you stated. The pads must still be inside the outline and the process's copper to edge space away from it, and the body is not checked.
6. **A height limit** (a lid rib, a bracket): a `Keepout` in the physical file over the area with `.max_height &= 3mm` and `.layers &= "top"` (or `"bottom"`; two keepouts give the two faces their own limits), and `.height` on each part whose height the user or the datasheet gives (on the part; the physical file may narrow it). Never write a height you do not have: a part with none is reported by name as unchecked, which is correct. This is provisional (ADR 0045, section 11): tell the user you used it.
7. **Pins that protrude** (a flat part on one face over the pins of a through-hole part that come through from the other): the part's `.pin_protrusion` (a length, ADR 0045 section 15) in the physical file, for example `.radio.row_a.pin_protrusion &= 0.2mm`, says how far the pins protrude out of the far side, whether they are cut after soldering or bought short. The part over them must be fitted by hand (`.assembly &= "hand"` in the circuit source), state its `.height`, and `protrusion + height` must fit the height limit on its face there; `fab` then writes a `trim:` line. **Never state a protrusion to make a finding go away**: cutting the pins is a step a person does, so state it only when the user or the drawing says the pins are cut or short, and say you did. Provisional (ADR 0045 section 14).
8. Run `netsmith build`, then `netsmith fab`. Read the first error only.

## Reading a finding

```text
error [constraint] constraint usb.place.left (widget.physical.nsm:14) is violated: the left edge of the body of J1 (F.Fab) is 0.18 mm from the left edge of the outline, and it allows -0.55 to -0.45 mm (0.73 mm over)
```

It names the line to change, the part, the drawing it measured, the measured value, the window, and how far over. A finding for a relation names both parts, by designator and path, and the difference between them ("the centre of the body of J4 (radio.row_b, F.Fab) minus the centre of the body of J3 (radio.row_a, F.Fab) is 16 mm along x, and it allows 17.45 to 17.55 mm (1.45 mm over)"). Change the range if you misread the drawing, or change the circuit. Never edit the layout file to hide a failure: the check measures the layout file's positions too, and a part `lock`ed there against a requirement is refused before placing ("is locked in the layout file ... unlock it and let the placer move it, or change the requirement"). A message that says no position meets the requirements names each one with its numbers: two lines that cannot both hold, or a window narrower than the part.

A part too tall for a height limit is `R2 (`tall`) stands 9.00 mm tall on the top face, and the keepout `ribs` allows 3.00 mm there ...`; `height of R3 (`r3`) is unchecked` is a warning that the part states no height (an error with `constraints = "error"` written in the manifest or `fab --constraints error`; `--allow-drc-violations` never waives it): ask for the number, do not invent it.

A violated requirement stops `fab`, and `--allow-drc-violations` does not waive it (it waives design rules, not requirements). Do not weaken that on your own. Only `constraints = "warn"` under `[project]` in `netsmith.toml`, or `netsmith fab --constraints warn` for one run, lists them as warnings; `--constraints error` forces errors for one run over a manifest that says `warn`. If the user wants to see the board anyway, ask first, use the flag for that one run, and say so in your report.

A part over the pins of a through-hole part is `U1 (`cell`) lies over the pins of J1 (`row`), whose pins protrude 0.40 mm (widget.physical.nsm:41), and that is not allowed: ...`: too tall for the height limit (it says the protrusion that would fit), no height, or assembled by the board house. Without a stated protrusion it is the plain overlap with a sentence naming `.row.pin_protrusion`: ask whether the pins are cut or short before you state it.

A courtyard past an edge nobody stated, for a part the lines pin, is refused before the placer starts, at `fab`, `build` and in the editor's findings (ADR 0045 section 14), with "at least ... wherever its requirements put it" where the lines leave a window. It is `J1 is not wholly inside the board outline: its courtyard is 4.00 mm past the right edge, and the design does not say it may hang over an edge; ...`. It names the edge, how far, and the line that would allow it (`.plug.place.right >= outline.width`). Do not add that line to make the error go away: ask whether the part is meant to stick out. `a pad of J1 is 13.00 mm outside the board edge` is never allowed by an overhang: move the part. If the placer says `the parts need ... placing them in the outline anyway ended with ...`, the parts do not fit the outline, and the numbers say how many pairs overlap and how many parts are outside; it then lists the ten worst pairs and parts by name (area, place, edge) and names the rest. A body whose centre is off the board is fine when an edge field (`.left`, `.right`, `.top`, `.bottom`) states the overhang (provisional); the same window on `.x` or `.y` is refused.

## Report

Tell the user which requirements you wrote and from which drawing, each one `fab` reports as not met with its numbers, and the overhangs you stated (`fab` warns of each), what is not built yet: a relation whose end is a module's box or a turn, or among three places (they stay `not evaluated`), and the PCB view's line between the two parts of a relation; a part's height from a catalog or its 3D model, and a polygon outline (`dxf import` refuses an outline that is not a rectangle with equal rounded corners).
