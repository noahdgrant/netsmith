# Load current is stated on the consuming rail and summed at the net

A `Rail` has a `current` field: the current drawn from the net through the rail's `pos` pin by whatever is behind the rail. It is positive when that draws and negative when it supplies, so a board's `.input` and a regulator's `.vout` are negative, and a design states only the load side: `.output: Rail { .current &= 0mA..50mA }`. We chose the consuming rail over a field on the supplying rail (`max_current` there) because the load is what a designer knows, and the supply's current then follows from it and can be checked against what the supplying part can give.

`~` between two rails does not equate their currents, though it equates every other field. Equal currents would be wrong for a rail that feeds several loads. The checker instead records one `Balance` per net and scope: the currents of the rails there add up to zero (Kirchhoff's current law). A scope is a board or a module. Its rails are the rails of its direct children, which count as they are, and its own rails. A module's own rail is a port, and from inside it is the supply of what is inside, so it counts with its sign flipped: `regulator.vin.current - stage.vin.current = 0`. A board has no outside to model, so its own rails stand for the outside world and count as they are. Rails deeper than a scope's children are left to the scope of the module that holds them, which is why a module's port hides what is inside it.

The solver uses the balances while it propagates. When exactly one current on a net is unknown, it is the negative of the rest, so a load stated at the board reaches the regulator and from there the board's input. When every current is known, the net must be able to balance, or it is reported with the numbers. (ADR 0038 changes what is checked: a stated current is a budget that what the others determine must lie inside, and otherwise the currents only have to be able to add up to zero; the first version of this sentence, that each current's negative must lie within what the others can give, is superseded.) With two or more unknown currents a net stays unsolved, which is not an error.

`LinearRegulator` states `.vin.current == -.vout.current` (its own quiescent current is ignored), that the current it supplies is at most `.max_current`, and that `(vin - vout) * current` is at most `.max_power`. The catalog gives the XC6206's package power dissipation, 250 mW at 25 C, from its datasheet; it is not derated for a hotter board.

Limits. Only rails add to a sum here; the chains of parts that draw from a net, and the parts' own rails, are added by ADR 0014, which supersedes this paragraph's first sentence. A supply's capacity could not be stated on the rail itself in this version, because `&=` was read as the exact current, which made a stated supply that is larger than the load a contradiction (ADR 0038 changes that: a stated range is an allowance); the capacity of a part belongs to the part (`max_current`). A balance holds in the worst case for each current separately, so it is necessary and not sufficient when loads are correlated.
