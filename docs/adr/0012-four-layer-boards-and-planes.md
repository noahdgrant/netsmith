# Four-layer boards have planes on their inner layers

`std::mfg::jlcpcb::FourLayer` is JLCPCB's multilayer process for four layers: 1 oz outer copper, 0.5 oz inner copper, 1.6 mm, with the multilayer rows of the capabilities page (0.09 mm track, 0.15 mm annular ring) and the page's `JLC04161H-7628` build-up for the `.kicad_pcb` stackup. Every number and its page are in the definition's doc comment. Only the 1.6 mm build-up is sourced, so only 1.6 mm is offered; another thickness is a contradiction on `process.stackup.thickness`, as for any value the process cannot make.

The inner layers are planes, and the board says which net each one is: `.process.inner1.net &= "GND"`, `.process.inner2.net &= "3V3"`. A process is a member of the board (ADR 0008), so this is a board naming a net and ADR 0005 holds as it stands; what changed is its check, which refused `.net &=` outside a board only on a `Pin` and now refuses it on any field named `net`, so a `Plane` in a module cannot name one either. A `Plane` is an interface with one field, `.net`, defined next to `Stackup` in `std::mfg::stackup`; `FourLayer` has two, `.inner1` and `.inner2`, counted from the top. Lowering refuses a plane with no net, naming the layer and the spelling to use, and a plane on a net the board does not have, naming the nets it does. It puts the nets and the build-up in the design's `Stackup::inner`; a two-layer process has none. We did not make the stackup carry the nets, because the example this was asked for writes them on the process and the stackup is the library's to state.

The router draws on the two outer layers only. A plane is poured as a KiCad zone over the whole board, inside the least space to the edge, with the process's space to copper of other nets (the larger of its clearance and its inner-layer hole space, 0.3 mm) and KiCad's thermal reliefs. A through-hole pad on a plane's net and any via of that net reach the plane where they stand. Any other pad on the net has a line to the plane in the ratsnest, and the router satisfies it with a via dropped in the nearest cell where one may go, and a track on the pad's layer to it. Pads of a plane net are never joined to one another by tracks. This is the simplest approach that is sound, and not the best: the via may sit a little way from the pad, and several pads do not share one.

KiCad's design rules check does not fill zones, and a zone with no fill leaves every via on it dangling. netsmith cannot compute a fill itself, so the board file holds the zones unfilled and the fabrication step has KiCad's own Python (`pcbnew`, which ships beside `kicad-cli`) fill them on a staged copy before each check and plot. The `.kicad_pcb` written to disk stays unfilled and deterministic; KiCad fills it when a person opens it and presses `B`. A board with no zone does not touch Python, so the two-layer package is unchanged. A four-layer package differs in the inner Gerbers (`-In1_Cu.gbr`, `-In2_Cu.gbr`, which a package JLCPCB accepted already had) and a line in `fab-notes.txt` that names the layers.

Left open: signals on inner layers, outer pours, a plane that is not the whole board, split planes (two nets on one layer), via-in-pad, other build-ups and thicknesses, and net classes so that power tracks are wider.
