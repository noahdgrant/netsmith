# Effective capacitance is a field of its own, and the rail gives the bias

A `Capacitor` has two capacitances because they are two physical quantities. `.capacitance` is the marked value with its tolerance, as on a bill of materials, in atopile's `Capacitor` ([`cap.capacitance = 100nF +/- 20%`](https://github.com/atopile/atopile/blob/main/.claude/skills/ato/SKILL.md)) and in every vendor listing; this returns it to the meaning it had before ADR 0009, which moved the derated value onto it and called the marked value `.nominal`. `.nominal` is gone, because it was the marked value under another name. The new `.effective_capacitance` is what the circuit can use: the marked value after the DC bias across the part and after its tolerance, in the worst case over the range the bias can take. Neither is a second spelling of the other. A design states what it needs of the one that matters: `.effective_capacitance >= 4.7uF` on a regulator's output capacitor, where the datasheet asks for capacitance in use, and `.capacitance &= 100nF +/- 20%` on a bypass capacitor, where the design means a part.

"Effective capacitance" is the vendors' own term. Analog Devices writes the requirement as `C_EFF = C_BIAS x (1 - TEMPCO) x (1 - TOL)`, with `C_BIAS` the capacitance at the operating DC voltage, and TI defines it as the actual capacitance under DC bias and temperature. No EDA tool found does this natively; atopile matches the marked value and has no bias model. The ADR 0009 machinery is reused unchanged: the catalog's curve of the fraction left at each voltage, the worst case over the range of the bias, and the refusal of a part with no curve or a bias past the end of its curve. That refusal now applies only when the design constrains `.effective_capacitance`; a design that constrains `.capacitance` alone may use any capacitor, with or without a curve. The tolerance was already in the derated value (the lowest value is the bottom of the marked range times the lowest fraction, the highest is the top times the highest), which is ADI's `(1 - TOL)` term for a minimum and its mirror for a maximum.

Temperature is not modelled. ADI's `(1 - TEMPCO)` term needs the change over the part's temperature range, which for X5R and X7R is the class limit of the dielectric code. No datasheet page for these parts stating that limit was read (Samsung's characteristics sheet for C23733 only draws the graph), and a number from memory is worse than none, so the curves stay typical data at 25 C and the effective capacitance is optimistic by whatever the part loses over temperature and ageing. This is open in issue #18.

`.bias` is derived. A part with a `.bias` and a `bridge` (a capacitor) that sits between a rail's `pos` and `neg` has its bias set to that rail's voltage: after pins are joined into nets, the checker finds the rails whose `pos` and `neg` are on the nets of the part's two terminals, in either order (the sign of a bias does not matter), and adds the relation `bias == rail.voltage`. This covers a `~>` chain, plain `~` connections and pins that name the same nets. Only rails the part's own scope can see count: those of the module or board that holds it, and the ports of what that holds (ADR 0004); the voltage of a rail further out arrives at the port by the `~` that joins them. Rails on the same two nets are one voltage, and the first by path stands for them. A capacitor across no rail keeps its bias unset, and a design that constrains its `.effective_capacitance` is told which capacitor, that its bias is not known, and that it must be placed between a rail's pos and neg or state `.bias`. `.bias` stays an ordinary field: a design states it for a capacitor that is across no rail. A stated bias that disagrees with the rail's voltage is a contradiction, reported by the solver as `c.bias must stay within ..., but rail.voltage works out to ...`.

Rejected: keeping the derated value on `.capacitance` (it breaks every BOM and atopile reading of the word), and a class-level fallback for parts with no curve (no standard bounds a class).
