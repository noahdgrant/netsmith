# KCL solves rails, verifies parts

Amends ADR 0014. Amended by ADR 0027: the two-term rule holds only for a complete balance. Amended by ADR 0038: what a balance verifies when every current is known. Amended by the section "Amendment: the Ohm bound, pass-only" below (PROVISIONAL). The user was away; the choice below was taken for the user and is reviewed afterwards.

## The fault

ADR 0014 put the through-current of a chain's first part into the balance of the net the chain starts on, and ADR 0007's propagation solves the one unknown current of a balance as the negative of the rest. With the resistor power rule of ADR 0025 (`.drop * .current <= .max_power`) this gave false failures on a real board: `(r13.drop * r13.current) must always be at most r13.max_power, but -1.7e-199 fW..461 kW is not always below 250 mW`. The resistor heads a divider that starts on a regulated rail and ends on a signal pin. The balance of the rail holds the regulator's supplied current, several loads and that resistor, and the resistor's current was the only unknown. The residual of several wide intervals (the supply 0 to 2.1 A, the loads 0 to 2 A and others) is -2.1 A to 2.1 A, or worse, and a resistor cannot carry that much by itself. It is the dependency problem again: the terms of a balance are independent ranges, so the difference of their sums is wider than any one of them and says nothing about a part.

## Decision

Balance propagation sets the current of a rail or a port (ADR 0007), and sets a part's through-current only from a balance of two terms. From a longer balance a part's current is verified (an inconsistency is an error naming the net and the terms, as before) and never set. Its own relations determine it: Ohm's law, the LED equation, a stated value, or a neighbour in the chain. If they do not, it stays unknown, and every relation that needs it (the power of a resistor, the rating of a fuse, an inductor's saturation, a Zener's power, a Schottky's or a ferrite bead's current) is listed as `not evaluated` like any other relation with an unknown, and is no failure. A relation whose current is determined keeps failing with its numbers.

A balance of two terms is an equality, not a subtraction: the range of one is the negative of the range of the other, and nothing is widened. Two things need it. A fuse in series with a load (`supply.pos ~> fuse ~> load.pos`) carries the load's current, and a rating is checked against it (the guide's fuse example). The inductor of `BuckStage` is in a chain from the IC's switch pin to the output, and its current is the load's: both ends of the chain are two-term balances. Refusing these would have meant stating `.fuse.current == .load.current` in every board, which is what ADR 0014 removed. The rule the user gave (rails and ports only) is kept for everything else.

## What changed

Only `solve_balance` in `netsmith-solve`. No library file changes: the ratings above are already relations that evaluate to unknown when a current is, and the solver already lists those. Existing tests hold, among them the LED branch counted against the regulator (its current follows from the LED equation and the chain), `parts_alone_can_overload_the_regulator`, the derived input of `two_boards` and the 150 mA examples. A first version that refused every part broke four tests that did solve a part's current from a net: the fuse test and three buck-stage tests (the inductor's current from the output), and the guide's buck example, all two-term cases; that is why the rule above has the exception. No test relied on a longer balance.

## Limits

A part at the head of a chain on a busy rail whose current nothing else states is not checked against its rating; the build says so with a `not evaluated` line, and the design can state the current. The worst case is still taken for each current separately (ADR 0007).

## Amendment: the Ohm bound, pass-only (PROVISIONAL)

Found on a real 12 V automotive board: about twenty of its 47 notes were the drop and power relations of ten passive parts on signal nets (pull-downs, a divider, series resistors into an ADC and a PDM input), each waiting on a current that this ADR leaves unset (a net with two or more unknown currents). Their current is not known, but it is limited: when the voltage at each end of a resistor lies in a range the solver knows, `|I| <= max |Va - Vb| / R`, with `R` at its least, and the power is at most `max (Va - Vb)^2 / R`.

**The rule.** After the parts are picked and every relation is judged, each relation still waiting that reads fields of one resistor (a part with a `bridge` and a `resistance` of a value above zero) whose `current` has no value is read once more with that current in `+-I` and the drop across it, if it too has no value, in `+-(Va - Vb)` (and, for `.drop == .current * .resistance`, in the current through the greatest resistance, which is the same range widened by the tolerance). A relation that passes is no longer waiting: its note, and its part in an open circle, go. **Pass-only:** a relation that fails or still cannot be judged under the bound stays as it was, waiting, and nothing is said of it. The bound is never a verdict, and it adds no finding, no error and no value to the model. The reason is the ranges of ADR 0026's own limit: a bound does not know that a pin draws nothing, so a 100 ohm series resistor into an input is bounded at 35 mA and 120 mW between two ends that each swing 3.3 V, over its 62.5 mW rating, while the real current is the input's, a microampere. Used as a verdict the bound would refuse that good design; used to prove, it is sound.

**What a range is.** Each end's net is read from `net_range`'s inputs with more care than the levels check (ADR 0046) reads them, since a range that is too narrow would clear a note that should stand. A net is within the hull of: a rail on it (its stated voltage, and `0 V` on the ground the range is measured from; a net with a rail pin is that rail's, whatever else is on it); a `Logic` line that drives it (its ground to its reference's top); a pin that drives it and is not a `Logic` line, held to the supplies of its own part (the one assumption beyond the levels check: an output pin swings no more than its own part's rails, which the part must state with a voltage and a ground that is the same net); the far ends of the resistors on it (the node of a resistive network lies between its neighbours); and the ground when an input or supply pin is on it. A net with a connector pin (a part that states `.mating`), a passive pin of a part that conducts, a pin with no kind, a rail with no voltage, or a rail or `Logic` whose ground is another net has no range, and so the resistor has no bound. Grounds that mated connectors join (ADR 0040) are not followed: only the same net is the same ground.

**What it does not do.** It does not set a current, so the net's other unknowns stay unsolved (the solver still gives up on a net with two unknowns); a check that needs the real value (a draw budget, a regulator's load) stays open; a part other than a resistor (a diode, a MOSFET, a regulator) is not bounded; a bound whose ends span a whole supply (a fuse across 16 V, 323 A) proves nothing and changes nothing. The assumptions that no part on a net injects a voltage outside its hull, and that a pin's own part's rails limit its swing, are the owner's to review.

**What it costs.** One pass over the nets of each resistor with an unset current, when relations wait, and a `current_bounds` list on the `Solution` that the track planner reads (ADR 0036, amendment 4). No syntax, no change of what any program means: a design that built still builds, with fewer notes. A test that asserted a note for a plain resistor whose power the bound now proves (`a_plain_resistor_leaves_the_net_unsolved`, `a_part_current_is_not_set_from_a_balance_with_an_unmodelled_pin`) was updated. This is the first step of the owner's draft for a full DC analysis (section 7, "the cheap bound"): it is that bound and nothing more.
