# KCL solves rails, verifies parts

The owner reviewed this on 2026-11-02 and accepted it (the status line it had said "accepted (user review 2026-11-02)"). The text before the cut is `git show 8a15457:docs/adr/0026-kcl-solves-rails-verifies-parts.md`. Meets the second test: a balance of wide intervals gives false failures unless one unknown is solved and the rest are verified, which the obvious reading of Kirchhoff's current law gets wrong. It amends ADR 0014, which put the through-current of a chain's first part into the balance of the net the chain starts on, and so ADR 0007, which solves the one unknown current of a balance as the negative of the rest.

The five ADRs fit together as follows. ADR 0007: a rail's current is the consuming side's, and a net's currents add up to zero. ADR 0014: a chain draws from the net it starts on, and a part's `current` is the current through its bridge. This ADR: what a balance may set. ADR 0027: only a complete balance (every pin on the net is accounted for) is used for parts. ADR 0038: a stated current is a budget, and a balance verifies by checking that what the others determine lies inside it.

The fault. With the resistor power rule of ADR 0025 (`.drop * .current <= .max_power`), a real board failed with `(r13.drop * r13.current) must always be at most r13.max_power, but -1.7e-199 fW..461 kW is not always below 250 mW`. The resistor heads a divider that starts on a regulated rail and ends on a signal pin. The balance of the rail held the regulator's supplied current (0 to 2.1 A), several loads (0 to 2 A and others) and that resistor, whose current was the only unknown. The residual of several wide intervals is -2.1 A to 2.1 A, or worse, and a resistor cannot carry that much by itself. It is the dependency problem again: the terms of a balance are independent ranges, so the difference of their sums is wider than any one of them and says nothing about a part.

The decision. Balance propagation sets the current of a rail or a port, and sets a part's through-current only from a balance of two terms. From a longer balance a part's current is verified (an inconsistency is an error naming the net and the terms) and never set. Its own relations determine it: Ohm's law, the LED equation, a stated value, or a neighbour in the chain. If they do not, it stays unknown, and every relation that needs it (the power of a resistor, the rating of a fuse, an inductor's saturation, a Zener's power, a Schottky's or a ferrite bead's current) is listed as `not evaluated` like any other relation with an unknown. A relation whose current is determined keeps failing with its numbers.

A balance of two terms is an equality and not a subtraction: nothing is widened. Two things need it. A fuse in series with a load carries the load's current, and a rating is checked against it. The inductor of `BuckStage` is in a chain from the IC's switch pin to the output, and its current is the load's. Refusing these would have meant stating `.fuse.current == .load.current` on every board, which ADR 0014 removed. The rule the owner gave (rails and ports only) is kept for everything else. A first version that refused every part broke four tests, all two-term cases, which is why the exception is there. The change is in `solve_balance` of `netsmith-solve` and nothing else; no library file changed.

The cost. A part at the head of a chain on a busy rail whose current nothing else states is not checked against its rating; the build says so with a `not evaluated` line, and the design can state the current. The worst case is still taken for each current separately (ADR 0007), so a balance is necessary and not sufficient when loads are correlated.

Rejected: setting a part's current from the residual of a longer balance. It gives false failures, as above. Rejected: refusing every part, as the first version did. Rejected: stating the through-current on every board.

An amendment to this ADR, "the Ohm bound, pass-only", is now the header of `crates/netsmith-solve/src/ohm.rs`. It reads a resistor's current from the voltages at its two ends and only ever proves that a waiting check passes. The header carries the rule, the real board that showed the need (twenty of 47 notes), why it is pass-only, and what it assumes. It is provisional: the owner has not reviewed it. The fault, the two-term exception and the limits are also the doc comment of `solve_balance` in `crates/netsmith-solve/src/solve.rs`. The user's view is `docs/LANGUAGE.md` ("Load current", "A circle through the currents of a net").

Left open, from the amendment: "The assumptions that no part on a net injects a voltage outside its hull, and that a pin's own part's rails limit its swing, are the owner's to review."
