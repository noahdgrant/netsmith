# Fine-pitch routing, vias in pads, and parts on the back

Amends ADR 0008 (the process) and ADR 0012 (four layers, which left via in pad open).

A real board has a module whose pads are 0.6 mm squares on a 1.1 mm pitch, and parts on both faces. The router left the pads inside the module unrouted: it kept half a cell and a margin beyond the rules from every obstacle, which closed the 0.5 mm between two pads, and a track with its two spaces needs 0.3 mm.

## How a line is routed now

1. The line is searched for with the old room (the rules, a 0.05 mm margin and half a 0.1 mm cell).
2. A line that is left is tried again with 0.02 mm beyond the rules (`ROOM_TIGHT`), which a track between two open cells and a stub from a pad's centre to the nearest cell stay inside of. Only the lines that failed pay for this, so a board that routes at the old room routes as it did.
3. A pad still walled in is reached by a **via in the pad** if the process allows it. The via is at the pad's centre, is no wider than the pad's smaller side, and is checked at that exact place against all other copper and holes. A path that starts or ends on a pad's far layer pays 60 mm of track for it, so it is the last resort. A pad on a plane net that cannot get out gets the via and no track.
4. Where the board draws a track wider than the process's least, the tight try also looks for cells where a track of the least width fits; a track through them is **necked down** to the least width there and is the full width elsewhere.
5. Lines still left are **negotiated** for (PathFinder in a small form): each is routed where it would go if no other net were in the way, the nets it comes near are ripped up, the cells they fought over cost more from then on, and what was ripped up is routed again. This goes six rounds and the best state is kept. The earlier rip-up put the nets back if the line did not fit; this one lets lines fail for a round so the others can move.

There is no fan-out stage that puts a via beside each pad of a dense footprint before routing. The search already puts a via on any free cell next to a pad, and a pad with no free cell beside it (a pad inside a grid with 0.5 mm gaps) is the case a via in the pad is for.

## The process says whether a via may stand in a pad

`std::mfg::jlcpcb` has `.via_in_pad: Length`, the largest via the fabricator fills with epoxy and caps with plating, `0mm..0.55mm`. It is an upper bound where the other fields are lower bounds, so the design uses the top of what is left, and a board that does not want to pay for filled vias writes `.process.via_in_pad &= 0mm`. The number is from JLCPCB's capabilities page ("epoxy filled and capped" vias of 0.15 mm to 0.55 mm; "vias can be placed within BGA pads using filled and plated-over vias"). The page calls the range "via diameters"; the router uses a via in a pad only if the copper of its via is no wider than the number. A soldermask-plugged via is not one: it needs no mask opening, so it cannot stand in a pad. When a board has vias in pads, `fab-notes.txt` says how many, in which pads, and that they are ordered filled and capped on the order form (the Gerbers do not say it). Left unverified: that JLCPCB's order form for the two-layer process offers it, and its price.

## `.kicad_pro` gives the Default net class a priority

Without `"priority"`, KiCad 9 ignored the Default class's clearance and checked every board against its own 0.2 mm (measured: a 0.1 mm project reported "clearance 0.2000 mm"). The old room kept 0.2 mm anyway, so no earlier board saw it; routing at the process's 0.1 mm needs the rule to be the process's. This is one added line in every `.kicad_pro`.

## Parts on the back

A part has a `Side`: `Front` or `Back`. A part on the back is its footprint mirrored in its own frame (x negated), then turned by its rotation, as KiCad flips a footprint; its surface-mount pads are on `B.Cu`, and in the `.kicad_pcb` the footprint is written already flipped, on the back layers, with mirrored text. The router starts and ends a line on the layers a pad has, so lines between faces use vias as lines between layers do. Two courtyards on opposite faces may overlap unless one part has a hole through the board. *Amended (accepted by the owner, 2026-11-04):* a part with a hole takes its courtyard on both faces only if its footprint draws the courtyard on both `F.CrtYd` and `B.CrtYd`, or on neither (then the box made from its pads). If the footprint draws it on one of them only, the face of the other layer is judged by the part's pad boxes (every pad, copper or not) each grown by the process's clearance, instead of the whole courtyard: another part's courtyard on that face may cover the rest of its body, and a pad of the one under a pad box is still an error. The sentence is the same for the annealer's overlap cost (`Placed::room_on` and `Engine::rooms_on`), the placement check and the fit test (`could_fit` counts the pads' area on that face). The hole-to-hole and hole-to-copper rules are unchanged.

The placer takes `PlaceOptions` (`crates/netsmith-pcb/src/place.rs`). Its fields are `sides`, `fit`, `keepouts`, `cutouts`, `fixed` (the layout file, ADR 0032), `seed`, `wants` and `overhang` (ADR 0045), `wall` (ADR 0033) and `height_zones` (ADR 0045). A part in `sides` is on that face. Without a size to fit, parts are on the front unless named. With a size, the arrangement is laid out inside it. *Amended (ADR 0045, section 10, provisional):* the rows no longer decide whether the parts fit, since they never turn a part and count a part that hangs over the edge whole. If the rows are too big the annealer is run in the size anyway, first on the faces the parts are on, then, if that fails, with the parts nobody placed shared between the faces by area, largest first; only if the last run ends with courtyards over each other or parts outside does the error give the numbers ("the parts need 63.4 x 41.2 mm ... and the outline is 60 x 40 mm: 3.4 mm over in x; placing them in the outline anyway ended with 2 pair(s) of courtyards overlapping, ..."). The back is laid out in the same rows as the front, in the same area; a part with a hole takes room on both and the other face keeps clear of it. Boxes in `keepouts` (holes that are not parts) are kept clear of by the rows. The fabrication package already handled a part on the bottom: its position row is `bottom`, and its rotation is not corrected because no board of the evidence had one, so `fab-notes.txt` lists it as unverified.

## Left open

Rotation and mirroring of bottom parts in the position file are unmeasured. A footprint's 3D model is copied unchanged for a part on the back. Connectors are not moved to the back. A mask opening drawn in a footprint keeps vias away but not tracks. Fan-out before routing, push and shove, and a router that sees more than two layers are not done. *Follow-up (2026-11-04):* the two-face rule was checked against KiCad 9's DRC (`hole_faces.rs`, 16 boards): KiCad flags nothing the rule passes, `courtyards_overlap` agrees layer by layer, and where the rule keeps a courtyard off a part's pads KiCad's `pth_inside_courtyard` (ignored by default) flags only the holes, so the rule is stricter by about 0.55 mm round the pads.

**Amendment, 2026-11-04 (pin protrusion, ADR 0045 section 14).** A through-hole part whose board states how far its pins protrude (`.pin_protrusion`) no longer keeps a part on the far face off its pads: that part may stand over them if it is fitted by hand, states its height, and the protrusion plus its height fits the height limit there. Its own pads keep their clearance from the pads of the through-hole part. A part that states no protrusion is judged as above.

## Tight boards that place but do not route (issue #3; PROVISIONAL: the causes below are read from pictures of the copper, not proved)

**Reproduction.** `tight_board` in `crates/netsmith-pcb/tests/common/mod.rs` is an invented board, not any real design: 99 parts (80 0402 resistors and capacitors, 18 SOT-23 regulators, half of each on the back) round one module of 80 pads, 0.6 mm square on a 1.1 mm pitch in two rings round a hollow centre (44 + 36 pads), four layers, 106 nets, squeezed by the placer into 27 x 25 mm (`PlaceOptions::fit`). `NETSMITH_AREA=27x25 NETSMITH_SEEDS=0..12 cargo test --release -p netsmith-pcb --test tight measured -- --ignored --nocapture` prints, per seed, the lines left, the tries and the time. At 50 x 42 mm (2100 mm2, as in the issue) this board routes completely in under a second, so the issue's board is denser per part than the invented one; 27 x 25 mm is where the invented board fails the way the issue says (unrouted ends nearly all on the module). Seed 5 does not place in 27 x 25 mm.

**Numbers** (release build, seeds 0 to 11, default `RouteOptions`, lines left per seed):

| | seeds that complete | lines left, 11 placed seeds | per seed (0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10,11) | time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before (7f33de6) | 2 of 11 | 46 | 0 2 4 5 7 9 5 7 5 0 2 | 528 s |
| after | 2 of 11 | 41 | 0 1 5 2 6 6 5 8 6 0 2 | 474 s |

That is a small gain, not the fix: the same two seeds complete, five seeds leave fewer lines and three leave more. Every board still passes the design rules check on what it routed.

**What was found.**
- Of the 41 lines left, 39 have an end on the module, as in the issue. The pictures of the copper show two causes. (1) *Sealed pockets*: a pad of the outer ring next to the board edge has a strip 1 to 1.4 mm wide beside it, the only way out. Two other nets' vias in the strip, one each side of the pad, close it on both layers (a via is 0.45 mm and keeps 0.15 mm), and the pad is left in a pocket with free copper cells and no way out. Negotiation can free it only by ripping a neighbour that then has no way out itself. (2) *The hollow*: a pad of the inner ring leaves into the hollow by a via, and the back face under the module is where the placer put back parts (faces share area), so the way from the via on the back is shut. The channels between pads are one cell (0.1 mm) wide, so one track in a channel takes it from both pads beside it.
- The old negotiation ripped up *every* net its ideal path came near, 12 to 20 nets for a line crossing the module's neighbourhood, and routed them again in the order of length; most of those failed again and the walk was random. The best state is kept, so it never got worse, but it rarely got better.
- More rounds help by chance, not by design: with 50 rounds instead of 6 the old router finished two seeds that 6 rounds did not (219 s for one) and the new one finished one (7 s); another 2 to 3 seeds did not change at 20 or 50 rounds. The cost of a history cell (10 to 400) made no difference on four seeds.

**What was changed** (`route.rs`).
1. *The nets least in the way.* The search for the nets to rip up (`contested`, used by the rip-up and the negotiation) now crosses the copper of other nets at a price (`SOFT`, 100 per cell; a via 300) instead of at none. The path is the one with the fewest crossings, and only the nets it crosses are ripped up. The unit tests `a_soft_search_*` show it crosses a wall when no way round is cheaper and goes round by a gap when one is. This changes which lines the router rips up, so seeds that completed in one order may now complete in another or not at all (seed 10 now needs two tries, not one).
2. *Fixed copper counted once.* `masks_for` stamped every pad, edge and keepout of the board for each of the 30 000 to 40 000 searches of a hard board. The fixed obstacles are counted once for each room and track width (`Base`), and a net's own pads are taken away again for that net (`Counts::stamp` with `up = false`); the masks are what they were (with only this change, seed 1 left the same two lines after the same number of searches). Obstacles away from the window are skipped without a loop. The mask time on seed 1 went from 28 s to 17 s of 67 s. The search itself is now more than half of what is left (33 s).

**Tried and not kept.**
- *Starting the next try with the lines the last left* (a real "failed first" order, up to 4 extra tries): the same lines or others failed; no seed changed, and the time doubled.
- *A finer grid* (0.05 mm cells, margin 0.02 mm): 3 seeds of 3 left 0, 2, 2 against 1, 5, 2 for the coarse grid with the soft search, at twice the time. It changes every existing routed board and is not kept.
- *The placer* (`anneal.rs`): a soft cost for a part standing on the other face under a fine-pitch grid (a footprint of at least 40 pads, most with three neighbours within 1.5 times the nearest, nearest within 1.3 mm), 4 per mm of depth like the gap cost. 12 seeds: 1 completed, 65 lines left, against 2 and 46 before. Moving the parts from under the module raised the density beside it more than it cleared the back, so it is not kept. A hard keep-out would do the same; the escape gap the placer keeps on the same face (0.03 mm a pad, to 2.5 mm) is already 2.4 mm for this module.

**A fan-out stage (tried on a branch, PR #85, not merged; PROVISIONAL; it was `RouteOptions::fan_out`, off by default, not a gain).** Before any line is routed, each pad of a fine-pitch part gets a way out that nothing can shut: a track from the pad to the nearest cell where a via may stand (on the pad's face if that is within 4 mm of track, else by a via), the pad's net's copper from then on, fixed (never ripped up), with a via-sized disc on both layers at its end; every line from the pad starts at that end. A pad is fine-pitch if its part has at least 8 surface-mount pads, its box is narrower than a via with the process's space on each side (`via_diameter + 2 * clearance`), and another pad of the part is nearer than that. Pads are done deepest first (furthest from the edge of the part's pads), so an inner ring takes the gaps between the outer pads before the outer pads' escapes stand there; a tie is settled by the pad's number and place, so the same input gives the same board. An escape is drawn only if a line from its pad was routed. Same 11 placed seeds of the invented board in 27 x 25 mm (release, default options otherwise), lines left per seed (0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10,11):

| | seeds that complete | lines left | per seed | time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| main (PR #77) | 2 of 11 | 41 | 0 1 5 2 6 6 5 8 6 0 2 | 399 s |
| fan-out, every pad of the module that has a line (63 of 80) | 1 of 11 | 89 | 5 5 7 5 15 11 11 11 11 0 8 | 512 s |
| fan-out, inner ring only (36 pads), no disc at the end | 1 of 11 | 53 | 2 1 5 5 9 8 3 8 6 0 6 | 371 s |

It is worse, so it is off. What was seen: (1) on seed 2 (the only seed whose escapes were counted) all 63 escapes were found on the pad's face, none needed a via, and no pad failed to get one. Each fixed escape is a choice the router no longer makes: the nearest cell for a via is not the end that suits the line, and the line that starts there has one direction where the pad had four. (2) The disc at the end made no difference on seeds 2 and 3 (7 and 5 lines left with and without it), so the loss comes from the stubs and where they end, not from the reservation of a via. (3) Inner ring only is better than every pad but still worse than none, and seed 0, which completes without the stage, does not complete with either variant; most lines left are still on the module (3 of 5 on seed 2 with the inner ring only), and I did not check pad by pad whether those pads have an escape. (4) Results move by chance from seed to seed (PR #77's change moved three seeds the wrong way and five the right way), so a change of one or two lines on one seed is no signal; 89 against 41 over eleven seeds is. Not tried: an escape chosen with the line it serves (the nearest via cell toward the line's other end), a reservation the negotiation may take back, and push and shove, which fixed escapes would need next. The placer's cost for the free capacity of the strip round a module was not tried, because the stage did not help.

**Left open** (#3 stays open): a router that can move a track (push and shove) or reserve a pad's way out before the others are routed in a way that does not fix where it goes (the stage above did not help); the placer weighing the capacity of the strip between a module and the edge. A board of this density with a fine-pitch grid next to an edge is the case none of this reaches.
