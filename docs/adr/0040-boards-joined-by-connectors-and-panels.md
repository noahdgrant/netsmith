# Boards joined by connectors, and panels

The status line it had said "accepted (user decisions 2026-11-02)". Extends ADR 0010 (a system joins ports, and names no pins) and answers the panel questions of ADR 0020 and ADR 0035. Meets test 1 (the syntax of a mate and of a `panel` is in users' files) and test 3 (the options below). The text before the cut is `git show 0eaf326:docs/adr/0040-boards-joined-by-connectors-and-panels.md`. The mate's checks, its roles and the current in the contacts are the header of `crates/netsmith-check/src/mate.rs`; JLCPCB's panel figures, what `fab` writes for a panel and the checks are the header of `crates/netsmith-fab/src/panel.rs`; the user's view is `docs/LANGUAGE.md` ("Connectors that mate", "Panels", "Orders"). Tests: `crates/netsmith-check/tests/mate.rs` and `crates/netsmith/tests/panel.rs`.

**Decision 1: a mate derives the signals, and an explicit join of them is refused.** Each board of a kit is a stand-alone PCB with its own ground net, and plug-in boards touch the carrier only through connectors. `~` between the `.mating` of two connectors (a std interface of every header and socket: kind, gender, pitch, rows, positions, `pin_current`, `keyed`) says they are mated, contact N to contact N. The system names no pin and no net. After the nets are known the checker reads a role behind each contact (ground, power, signal, open), checks that the two sides can meet, and joins the ports the mate carries as if `~` had been written. A port join for signals a mate carries is refused, with the mate named, so a fact has one spelling; a port no connector carries still uses `~`. A system reaches a connector's `.mating` and nothing else of a board's connector. Each board keeps its ground net, and the system reports the ground groups. The current through the contacts is a requirement the solver picks a `header` catalog row for.

**Decision 2: a panel is a `panel` definition in `.nsm`.** It holds the board it repeats and the board house's rules (`VCutPanel` of `std/mfg/jlcpcb.nsm`, with its sources), and nothing else: it joins nothing and names no nets. The placement of each cell is in the layout file. An order key may name a panel and its quantity then counts panels. The first step is built: identical boards on a V-cut grid, merged Gerber and drill, a bill and a placement file, and panel checks that name the numbers and the page.

**Rejected.** For the mate: stating the mate as well as the port join and checking that they agree (two statements for one fact, and a third rule to say which wins); a join pin by pin in the system (it names pins, a net by another name); a `harness` definition with an explicit pin map (the general answer for adapters and cables, heavier than a kit needs; the mate is its N-to-N case, so it can come later without replacing the mate); a board port tied to the connector so that the system never reaches it (two lines per connector and a second description that can disagree with the part). For the panel: a table of the order (`[order.x.panel]`: unitless numbers, no ranges, no check against a process, and ADR 0035 said the order table is replaced and not extended if it needs more fields); a feature of `system` (a system is electrical and adds no copper; a panel is a manufacturing unit, boards of one system may be ordered separately and two systems may share a panel).

**The cost.** A port join is refused where a mate says it, a breaking change of `examples/two_boards.nsm`, acceptable under ADR 0001. A connector's rating is data work, a datasheet for each series; the two-row headers have no source and are unrated, so the check of their current is listed as not evaluated. A panel is one board house's rules at a time, and JLCPCB has not accepted a netsmith panel.

**Left open.** Word for word from the ADR: "**Partial mates** (a 1x8 header in a 1x10 socket) and **isolated grounds** (a barrier that must not join grounds): refused in v1. Recommend a later `.contacts` subset on the mate and a `.isolated` mark on a rail, each with its own check, if a design needs them." And: "**Panel assembly with JLCPCB:** the designator scheme, V-cut Gerber format and tab rules are unpublished in what was read. Recommend one real quote with a P0 panel before P2; until then the package states them as unverified." Not built, in issues #16 and #62: edge connectors and their `card_thickness` check, flat cables and wire to board, stacking and `.mated_height`, pin 1 on the PCB, different boards on one panel, routed tabs and mouse bites, rotation and per-cell placement, and a panel view that moves cells.
