# Where a part must be is a requirement the design states, measured on its body, and a violated one blocks `fab`

Meets tests 1 (the `.place` member, the `constraints` key and flag), 2 (the check measures the body and not the origin the layout file stores) and 3 (the layout file alone and a `constrain` statement are the obvious alternatives). I relied on the status line, "accepted (user decisions 2026-11-02)", and on "The user's decisions above are final". The readings that its sections 11 to 15 mark PROVISIONAL are not covered, and the headers named below carry those marks. The text before the cut is `git show 3e54ce1:docs/adr/0045-custom-constraints-from-the-enclosure.md` (432 lines; its sections 1 to 15 are the problem, the options, a staged plan C0 to C8, and logs of what was built).

**The user's need.** "We will need some way for people to design custom constraints like 'X part must be at Y location on the circuit board to fit in the enclosure'." The case fixes where a connector's mouth, a button, a screw and an antenna window are, and how tall anything may be. Before this a person could only put a part where they wanted, with no why, no tolerance and no complaint when it moved.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-11-02, unless dated):

1. A requirement on where a part goes is not part knowledge (ADR 0030 tier 2 fails "does it hold in every design") and not a decision (a decision is where a person put the part). It is stated in the design's source, and the solved position stays in the layout file. This amended ADR 0020 item 7A: the source holds requirements on positions, never a solved one. ADR 0048 moved the home to the board's physical file; the rest stands.
2. The way to state it is a built-in `.place` member of every part and module (`x`, `y`, `left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom`, `rotation`, `side`), narrowed with `&=` and the ordinary relations. A tolerance is a range (`2.0mm +/- 0.1mm`); the placer aims at the middle and a finding prints the measured value, the window and the margin left.
3. A position is measured on the part's body box (the footprint's `F.Fab` outline, else its courtyard, else its pads), and the layout file's `at` stays the footprint's origin. The case states the receptacle's shell, and a requirement on the body survives a change of package.
4. A violated requirement is an error that blocks `fab`, under its own key `constraints = "error" | "warn"` and `--constraints warn|error`, separate from `guidelines` (ADR 0030 decision 3). There is no waiver: `--allow-drc-violations` never waives one, and to relax a requirement a person edits the range. A module offers a placeable handle through the tied values of ADR 0042 (ADR 0048 replaced that by a path from the physical file).
5. A part may hang over the board edge only when its own edge field says so (`.plug.place.bottom >= outline.height`); a window on `.x` or `.y`, a lock and a module's `.place` do not; `fab` warns on each; no limit on how far; a pad is never excused (2026-11-03 and 2026-11-04).
6. A part's height is `.height` (the owner's spelling, 2026-11-03). A `Keepout` with `.max_height` limits the faces `.layers` names and forbids nothing else; an unknown height under a limit is a warning, an error when constraints are strict; the placer keeps the body plus 0.25 mm out of a limit zone (2026-11-03 and 2026-11-04).
7. `.pin_protrusion` says how far a through-hole part's pins protrude; per instance, in the physical file; a hand-fitted covering part may stand over them if the stack fits the zone's limit, and a covering part with no height is an error (2026-11-04).
8. The outline comes from the case as a DXF (`netsmith dxf import`); splines and ellipses are read as chords (2026-11-02). A polygon outline is not built.

**Rejected.** The layout file only (no relation, no expression, no link to the outline, and a drag could rewrite the requirement). A `constrain` statement (a verb and a mini-language beside the quantity algebra). Reading a STEP model for heights or an outline (a CAD kernel; the agent exports a DXF), SVG, a waiver, and a 3D-model height. Two homes for one fact (ADR 0001).

**The cost.** Hard windows may make a board that does not route or cost area near a dense module. The body is only as good as the footprint's `F.Fab`, and the finding says which drawing it used. A wrong frame (y up, inches) puts every number out by the same amount, so the import prints its transform. Relations between parts are not solved by the solver, so a mistaken one shows at placement and not at `build`. Until catalog rows carry heights most height checks say "unchecked", which is honest. The window grid is 0.05 mm.
