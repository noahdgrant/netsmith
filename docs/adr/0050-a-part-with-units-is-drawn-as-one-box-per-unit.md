# A part whose symbol has several units is drawn as one box for each unit

The editor's schematic drew every part as one box. The KiCad symbol of the processor module on the private board has two units, `U1A` (the supply pins and the 25 grounds) and `U1B` (every signal pin); the owner's hand-drawn schematic puts them on the sheet as two boxes, the grounds as one tall column of pins in a small box of their own with the decoupling capacitor and the supply beside it, and the signals in the box that has the crystal and the names. Drawn as one box (a table of 82 pins in labelled groups) the grounds were a row of 25 pins along the bottom edge of the signal box, with a bus wire under it, and the supply pins were wired from its top edge.

**What is drawn.** A part whose library symbol (the symbol the part's `kind` maps to, `kicad.toml`) has two or more units that have pins, and whose every pad the design uses is a pin of one of them, is drawn as **one box for each unit**, as the library draws the unit: its own shapes and pins, in the library's order and on its sides, upright (a unit is not turned, as a table of pins is not). A unit of more than 60 pins, or whose names would run into each other, is drawn as the box the whole symbol would be (`glyph.rs`: grouped, merged), with only its pins. A pin belongs to exactly one box (a pin of unit 0, which KiCad shares by all units, goes with the first). Each box has the part's reference and the unit's letter, `U5A`, `U5B` (the unit's number, 1 is `A`), the part's value, and the tooltip says which unit. A part with one unit, a symbol with no unit that has pins, a part with no symbol (the box the editor makes from the pinout), and a part that uses a pad its symbol lacks are drawn as before.

**Where the data comes from.** `netsmith_kicad::Symbol` already knows the unit of every pin (`SymbolPin::unit`, `Symbol::units`). The rule reads that and nothing else: not the part's name, not the pins' names or kinds. The netlist does not know units, and does not need to: a net's pins are pads, and each pad is on one unit.

**How the layout does it** (`layout/units.rs`). The engine draws a derived design in which each unit is a part of its own (the pads of the unit, the nets with each pin on its unit's part), so that everything that already stands beside a main part (a decoupling capacitor beside the supply pins, a rail's bus and symbol, a bus of an interface, a crystal beside its two pins, flags) works for a unit unchanged. The result is told back to the design: `layout.parts[i]` for `i` below the number of parts is the part `i` (for a part with units, the box with most pins), the other units' boxes follow after them all, and `PlacedPart::part` is the design's index in each. A board drawn by module puts all the boxes of a part on its sheet (`Sheet::parts` lists them). The references in the layout's texts are the unit's (`U5A`); everything that reads the design (the tooltip, `data-ref`, the pick of a part in the editor) gets the part's own reference, so selecting `U5` lights both boxes.

**What `check` and the metrics do.** `check` rebuilds the nets from the drawing as before, pin by pin, and has one rule more: each pad of a part is drawn in exactly one of its boxes (`unit-pin`); a pad the design uses that none of the part's boxes draws is still `pin-net`. The metrics count each box as a body: `parts` counts boxes, so a part of two units is two there, and `units` counts the boxes that draw a unit of a part with several. A box of a unit is never a satellite or a spine's IC by its shape.

**What the KiCad exporter does not change.** It already writes every unit of a symbol with the part's reference (`netsmith-kicad::schematic`), and is not touched. The netlist, the board, the BOM and the language are not touched. Only the editor's view draws two boxes, and the view's own pictures are not the KiCad schematic's.

**Rejected.** Splitting a generated box (a part with no symbol) by the kind of its pins into a power box and a signal box: the data is there (power pins against the rest) but it is a guess about the part, not something the source or a library said, and the box would not be one a library has. A part is split when its library says it has units. Drawing the units one box above the other, as KiCad's symbol editor does: that is the one-box drawing this replaces.

**Left open.** Which box is the part's first (the one that holds the index of the part in `layout.parts`, and the end of a bus or the IC of a spine): the one with most pins here, which is the signals of a processor and not its supply. Whether the box of a power unit should stand at the top right of the sheet, as the hand drawing has it: it stands in the row of the signal unit, level with its middle. A unit with no pin that any net uses is drawn all the same, with an X on each pin, as KiCad's schematic has it.
