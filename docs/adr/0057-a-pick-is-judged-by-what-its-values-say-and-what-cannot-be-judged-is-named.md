# A pick is judged by what its values say, and what cannot be judged is named

The first full build of a real board showed four ways in which the solver chose parts without something the design had said, or gave up without saying why. This ADR records what was found for each, what was done, and what was left. Every example in the tests uses invented values.

## A bound on a value worked out from a part takes part in the pick

`.ratio == .bottom.resistance / (.top.resistance + .bottom.resistance)` with `.ratio >= 0.05` picked a pair that failed afterwards, and picked the pair right when the bound was written on the resistors. The same happened to a module's declared `.quiescent` with a bound from the board, and to a `?=` window on a frequency that an equation works out from the resistor on RT.

The mechanism: `try_candidate` (`crates/netsmith-solve/src/solve.rs`) checks, for each candidate, only the relations that name a field inside the part. `.ratio >= 0.05` names `.ratio` and a number, so it was not among them. It was checked once, after every part had been picked, and the pick was made without it.

Now a candidate is also judged by a relation that names a value that the candidate's choice made known or narrower (any field whose range differs after the candidate's values are set and propagated), if no part still to be chosen feeds any field the relation names. The parts that feed a relation (`Coupling::feeding`) are found once per solve: from the fields it names, through the left side of every equation to what it reads, through every connection, and through the other terms of a balance of currents, to the columns of the parts. The last condition matters: while the other half of the divider is not chosen, `.ratio` is a range of what it may still be, and a bound that fails on it can hold for the part that is chosen; a test (`a_bound_waits_for_the_part_that_has_not_been_chosen_yet`) holds the pair that meets the bound back until its second resistor. The check after the picks is unchanged and is still the judge, so a pick is never accepted for want of this check, only refused earlier.

The window case needed the same: with the resistor picked inside the window the frequency is worked out from it, the stated window then holds the equation to the frequency it gives, and a resistor outside the window is refused where it is picked.

Rejected: judging every relation that names a changed field at once, without waiting for the parts that feed it. It refuses pairs that the second part would have saved, so a design that built before can fail. Rejected: moving the bound onto the resistors by rewriting the model (what a person does by hand). The relation is the design's statement and is checked as written; rewriting it would change the message and could not follow an equation through two named values.

A bound on a member that a part's field is held to by `part.field &= .member` is the other direction, and it did not reach the pick; ADR 0065 (stage 0) makes it a bound on the part.

## A generic inductor is held to what the stage asks of it

A generic inductor's saturation current is "the least that any relation demands". The relations were read before the part's marked inductance had set the ripple, so `current + ripple / 2 <= saturation_current` could not be evaluated and the generic took only the part's own `current <= saturation_current`. The stage's check then failed for every generic (the closest was a 1 mH inductor).

Now the values that follow from a generic candidate's marked columns are worked out first (one propagation), and the demand on each open column is read after them. The need is the highest end of every bound that now has a value, as before: the generic part takes it exactly, so the stage's check holds with nothing to spare, and the need goes with the part to the step that resolves it to a catalog row, which refuses the row that does not meet it (`resolve`). A stage whose IC states a minimum saturation current, or whose load plus half the ripple is over every row, gets a generic part that no row stands for and `fab` refuses by name. That is the sound behaviour: a generic part has no rating, so the demand becomes a requirement on the row, as capacitor voltage does elsewhere in the guide.

The second symptom, that the inductor's check used a switching frequency of the IC's whole RT range, is not a separate fault. When no resistor in the catalog meets the frequency window (`E24` has no 41.2 kohm), the resistor's pick fails and nothing sets its value, so the frequency stays at the range the IC states for RT; the inductor is picked in another group from that range. Before the change above the resistor was also picked without the window, and the inductor then saw the range of whatever was picked. With the picked resistor the value does reach the inductor's check (`the_frequency_resistor_the_solver_picks_reaches_the_inductor_s_check`). What is left: the inductor's group does not wait for the resistor's group when the resistor's pick fails (`next_group` only orders groups by the picks a column needs, not by every value a check reads). I did not generalise the order: it can change which solution is found first for designs that build.

## An open circle with a check inside it is a finding

A circle that nothing starts, and whose checks are therefore not evaluated, was a note. It is now also a guideline finding (rule `open circle`, `Solution::open_circles`): a `warning:` that names the fields, the checks that read the circle or a value worked out from it, and a field to start from (the first one that an equation sets from a stated value of the same kind, as the start of ADR 0053 is), and an error with `--strict` or `guidelines = "error"`. It is given only when a bound (`>=` or `<=`) is among the relations not evaluated. A circle with no such check, for instance one through a part with no rating, gets the note alone, and no build is refused for it. A circle that also reads a value nothing states (the case that `cycles::find` leaves out) is reported too, with that value named, because a range on the circle alone would not start it.

Warning, not error, by default: the design may be right, the checks are what is missing, and the guideline mechanism of ADR 0036 already gives a project the choice. An error by default would refuse boards that build today with a note; a note alone is what let a board pass with a check unmade.

Rejected: an error always. It cannot be waived, and a board whose circle is real and whose check is one the author knows holds would have to state a range that is only there to silence it. Rejected: reporting every relation that was not evaluated as a finding. Notes of that kind are in every board that has data missing (ADR 0046); a finding that fires for all of them would be ignored.

What this does not do: a circle that is also a stuck value is found only when the group of fields has a cycle. The first repro of the problem (a divider behind a series resistor, no start) is not a circle of equations either; it is the next section.

## A circle through the currents of a net is an open circle too (issue #18)

Added on 2026-11-06 after the owner said yes to extending the finding to a net with two unknown currents. The choices below are the agent's (A6), for review.

### The mechanism

Take a divider of two resistors on a rail `after`, behind a resistor `series` from the supply (invented: 14 V to 22 V, 68 ohm, a draw limit of 40 uA on `.top.current`). The relations that wait are the draw limit, the divider's equation `.top.current == .vin.voltage / (.top.resistance + .bottom.resistance)`, `.after.voltage == .supply.voltage - .series.drop`, `.series.drop == .series.current * .series.resistance`, and every power rating that reads a current. They wait on one value, `series.current`. Nothing sets it. Its net `VIN` has two terms (`series.current` and `supply.current`), both unknown, so the solver can only equate them. Its net `AFTER` has three: `after.current` (a board's rail stands for the outside world and is not stated), `divider.vin.current` (the divider's port, which the divider's own balance sets from `top.current`) and `series.current`. A part's current is never set from a net of more than two terms (ADR 0026: the residual of several wide ranges says nothing about one part), so `series.current` has no source and `top.current` waits on it for good.

The pick is made without the check for the reason in the first section: a relation that is not evaluated cannot refuse a candidate. `solve` ends with the check in `deferred` and a note, and the divider is picked from what can be evaluated alone (330 ohm and 330 ohm, which draw 21 mA at 14 V against the 40 uA that the board asked for). `cycles::circles` found nothing because the dependency of `series.current` on `top.current` is the balance of `AFTER`, which the solver does not use; it is a circle only if that balance is counted.

### What was done

First, a root fix that is small and sound, for what can be settled. A term of a balance that is exactly zero (a stated `&= 0A`, or a value worked out to it) adds nothing, so it is not counted when the solver decides whether a net has more than two terms (`Model::draw_is_zero`, `solve_balance_but`). A net with a rail stated `.after.current &= 0A` has two live terms, `series.current` and `divider.vin.current`, and they are equal: the solver says so. Nothing else about the numbers changes (adding an exact zero is the identity), and a range such as `0A..1mA` is still a term. What is left in that board is a circle of equations, which the existing finding names and a voltage starts.

Second, the finding. `cycles::balance_circles` finds the groups of unset fields that would be a circle if the solver set a part's current from a complete balance of more than two terms, and keeps those where such a balance is part of the cycle (`Circle::through`). It is the same search as `circles` with those balances added as producers, so it needs an equation in the cycle too, and a net of unknown currents with no equation that feeds back (a fuse in front of a load whose current nothing states) is not found, as before. `open_circles` takes the circles first and these groups after them, and names a check only once. The checks are the same ones: a bound that reads a value of the group or a value worked out from one and is not evaluated. The rule, the severity (a `warning:` by default, an error with `--strict` or `guidelines = "error"`) and the condition that a bound must be waiting are unchanged. No note is added: the notes about the unknown current and the unevaluated relations already print.

The message, for the invented board above (the guide has the exact one):

```text
Behind: open circle guideline: these values depend on each other and none has a value: `after.current`, `after.voltage`, `divider.top.current`, `divider.vin.current`, `divider.vin.voltage`, `series.current`, `series.drop`, `supply.current`; so the checks `divider.top.current <= divider.max_draw`, ... are not evaluated, and the parts they would judge are chosen without them; net `AFTER` carries 3 currents (`after.current`, `divider.vin.current`, `series.current`), none of which has a value, and the solver works a part's current out only from a net of two currents (ADR 0026), so what the checks wait on is never set: state a range on `series.current` (the part's current: the most and the least that the net's loads draw through it), or on a voltage in the circle, for example `after.voltage`; if nothing else draws from the net, `after.current &= 0A` makes it a circle of equations, which a voltage starts; the checks are then made against what you state, and nothing compares it with the net's other currents
```

The checks are listed with the design's own first and the library's ratings after (a part's `drop * current <= max_power` would otherwise hide the draw limit behind "and N more"); the order of the rest is as before.

A range stated on the part's current, or a voltage, ends the finding and the limit is part of the pick (the same board no longer picks 330 ohm and 330 ohm). Tests: `crates/netsmith-solve/tests/two_unknowns.rs` and the new tests at the end of `crates/netsmith/tests/open_circle.rs`, with values that are not those of the repros: the finding on the shape and on a chain of a fuse and a diode, a start of either kind ending it, a range on another term not ending it, a design whose bound is absent (no finding), a net with unknown currents and no feedback (no finding), the zero term, and the pick that is made without the limit.

### Rejected

Setting a part's current from a balance of more than two terms when the others have ranges. It is the case ADR 0026 refused: the part's current becomes the residual of independent ranges, wider than any of them. Here `after.current` is not even stated.

Taking an unstated board rail's current to be zero. A board's rail stands for the outside world and the board may have a consumer that the design does not state; stating `0A` is the author's claim, and now it is honoured (above). Doing it silently would pass the draw check on a claim nobody made.

A finding for every deferred check that reads a current nothing states. A fuse in front of a load whose current is not yet stated would warn, and so would every resistor rating on a board in progress: ADR 0057 rejected reporting every unevaluated relation for the same reason. What distinguishes this case is that a stated range would also have to hold something that depends on it: the circle.

Counting those balances in `cycles::find`, so that the circle note and the solver's own start (ADR 0053) cover them. The note would change for designs that build, and step 4 of the start is proved for equations only. With the balance counted, an unset `after.current` makes the start fail for want of a value, which is safe, but the argument is not one this ADR should assume. The finding alone changes nothing the solver computes.

An error always: as above.

### Limits, left open

A range stated on the part's current or on a voltage is a claim that nothing here checks against the net. With a start on the voltage, `after.voltage == supply.voltage - series.drop` stays "not evaluated" (the drop waits on the part's current), so the start itself is not tested; with a start on the part's current every relation of the circle is evaluated, but the net's other currents are not compared with it, because one of them has no value. A claim that is too small passes the checks it makes. The message says so ("nothing compares it with the net's other currents"), and the voltage start is no worse than the one a board has been able to state since ADR 0053.

Only a complete balance is counted. A net with a pin whose draw the model cannot say (an IC's signal pin) is not, because nothing on it is determined by the others even in principle.

A group that a circle of equations already contains is found by `circles` and reported there; a larger group that merges it through a balance is reported for the checks that the first did not name. Where a design has both, the first finding is the one to follow.

The cost is a second search of the equations and balances, made only when a relation was left unevaluated.

## A part nothing can serve is named at once; parts that read the same stated value are searched apart

The joint search gave up after 250000 combinations without saying why when one part had no candidate whatever the others were, and the message of the closest candidate named another part. In an invented chain of ten dividers, 20 resistors tied by one total and a bound that no resistor on the last stage meets, the build took 71.8 s with the snapshot binary and 75.8 s with the tree before this change, and ended with `gave up` and a list of all 20 parts. It now ends in 0.4 s with `no resistor candidate satisfies t10: the closest of the 343 that fit, ... fails because t10.resistance must always be at least 20MΩ` (release builds; the board is generated, ten stages of two resistors each, and is not in the repository).

(a) Before the search of a group of more than one part, each part's candidates are tried alone. A part is the cause when every candidate that fits is refused by a relation whose value only this part's choice decides (the parts that feed it, as above, are this part alone) and no candidate passes. A candidate refused for a value that other parts decide, or by a conflict found while propagating, or a part with a bank count, is not counted: the search then runs as it did, and its messages are unchanged. Two messages in `crates/netsmith-solve/tests/solve.rs` (`a_regulator_without_headroom_fails_with_the_numbers` and `a_load_beyond_the_regulators_current_names_the_current_and_the_limit`) name the part now where they named the group.

(b) A part joined to a stated rail reads the rail's voltage as one value with every other part on it, and the groups were made from every value two parts share. A value that no choice of parts can change (a field with a value now, none of the fields joined to it worked out from anything, none a column) joins nothing, so two parts that read the same stated rail and nothing else in common are two groups. A failing group no longer undoes the picks of the other: `a_regulator_output_no_part_offers_is_blamed_on_the_regulator_not_the_capacitors` now has the input capacitor chosen. A group's picks keep the place they had in the listing.

Results on the existing tests are unchanged except for those two messages and the one pick above. All `examples/*.nsm` print the same with the tree before this change and after it (compared output of `netsmith build`).

Rejected for (a): trying each part alone with every other part's range open and calling any failure a cause. A relation that fails on the wide range of a part not yet chosen can hold for the part that is, so the design that builds would be refused. Rejected for (b): splitting by classes of joined values only. One equation between two parts joins their classes, and a power chain is held together by such equations.

### A bank, and what a derated capacitance reads

The same give-up came back on a real board in a group of seven parts: a switching IC with a catalog row, its frequency resistor, the stage's inductor, the stage's input bank and two more input capacitors, and a ferrite in front of the input. Nothing in the group could be chosen because no catalog row of the input bank was rated for the rail's peak (58 V on the board; 75 V against rows of at most 50 V in the invented repro below), and the search ended with `gave up after trying 250000 combinations` after 17 minutes, naming nothing.

Why (a) did not catch it: `hopeless` skipped every part with a bank count (`if job.count.is_some() { continue }`), and the stage's input capacitor is a bank. The skip was there because the bank's relations read the count, which the part's own choice sets, and nothing in (a) tried the counts. The rating check, `.input_cap.max_voltage >= .vin.peak`, does not read the count and is the same at every count.

Now `hopeless` tries a bank at every count its range holds (`counts_of`, as the search does) and a row is dead when no count is accepted and every refusal is by a relation whose value only this part decides. A refusal that reads nothing worked out from the count is the same at every count (as `count_free_failure` in `search`), so the other counts are not tried for it: the 50 V row in the repro costs one trial, not eight.

A second gap showed when the bank was tried: `Coupling::feeding` followed equations, connections and balances, but not that a derated column (a capacitor's `effective_capacitance`) is worked out from the capacitor's `.bias`. A check on `count * effective_capacitance` was therefore taken to depend on the bank alone, though the bias is the rail's voltage, which another part's pick (the ferrite's drop) narrows. Judged with the other parts open, such a check can fail on the wide range of the voltage and hold on the narrow one the filter's pick leaves, so a bank would have been blamed for what the filter decides (a test, `a_bank_refused_for_a_value_another_part_decides_is_not_blamed_alone`, failed without this edge). The bias is now one more thing a derated column is worked out from. This makes `feeding` larger for such relations only, so a bound that reads them waits for the parts that set the voltage (`try_candidate`) as it does for any other value worked out from a part still to be chosen.

Invented repro, not in the repository as a file (a project of one board, one invented IC catalog row, `FerriteBead`, three resistors, `BuckStage`, two input capacitors; the rail's peak is 75 V and the largest row rated for it is 50 V; the jlcpcb catalogs): release build of the tree before this change, 1 m 30 s to 1 m 42 s ending in `gave up ... for filter and reg.controller and reg.frequency_resistor and reg.stage.inductor and reg.stage.input_cap and reg.vin1_cap and reg.vin2_cap`; after it, 4.9 s (the whole build, of which the search is a small part; the rest is loading the standard library and the catalogs) ending in `no capacitor candidate satisfies reg.stage.input_cap: the closest of the 31 that fit, 1 x 10nF 10% 50V X7R 0402 C15195, fails because reg.stage.input_cap.max_voltage must always be at least reg.stage.vin.peak, but 50V is not always above 75V`. The tests with small invented catalogs are in `crates/netsmith-solve/tests/joint_bank.rs`; the first of them fails without the change (the message then names all seven parts together). All `examples/*.nsm` and the two example projects print the same before and after.

What is not done: a bank that no count can serve because of a value that other parts decide is still searched as before (that is the case above where the filter decides the voltage), so a design whose cause is of that kind can still give up. What was not checked: the real board itself (not available here).

## A search learns which picks a failure came from, and names them (2026-11-07)

A real board gave up at 250000 combinations in a group of many parts and the message named all of them and no fault. The search already went straight back to the nearest part a failure depends on (conflict-directed backjumping, above), but it found what a failure depends on from classes of values: the fields joined by any equation, connection or balance form one class, and a part depends on a failure when one of its columns is in a class the failure reads. One equation that mentions many parts (a total over all of them, a net that every part draws from) puts them all in one class, so every failure depended on every part, nothing was skipped, and the search was the plain one. The owner chose to learn from the failure which values it came from and stop earlier.

What was done (`crates/netsmith-solve/src/solve.rs`).

- **What a failure depends on is found by following the way values are worked out, not by classes.** `Coupling::fed_by` follows the same edges as `Coupling::feeding` (an equation's right side to its left, a connection both ways, the terms of a balance each from the others, a derated column from its bias; fields no pick can change are left out) from the fields a rejection was worked out from to the columns they come from. A rejection depends on the earlier picks that feed the fields of the relation or conflict that failed (`failure_reads`: for an equation also the fields of the other equations on the same value, which `verify` holds it against) and not on every relation judged before it: it is refused whatever those say. Which candidates fit depends on the picks that feed the part's own columns. The classes are used only where a rejection is compared with the count of a bank.
- **A failure keeps the parts it is made of.** `Failure.core`: the part, the earlier picks it depends on, the parts below it, and the parts still to be chosen that feed what the relation read (it is judged on the range that part has, so the failure is that part's too, and it is named though no choice of it is undone). A part that the search jumped over is not in it. `Failure.closest` is the rejection nearest to working among those the failure is made of.
- **The message names the core.** When the search of a group fails and the core is fewer parts than the group, the message names those and no others, with the closest choice for them and the relation that fails: `no combination of candidates satisfies `a` and `z` together, whatever the other parts of the group are: the closest, a = 800 1% and z = 800 1%, fails because (a.resistance + z.resistance) must always be at least 1MΩ, but ...`; for one part, `no `resistor` candidate satisfies `p` whatever the other parts of the group are: the closest, ...`. When the core is the whole group the message is the one it was (`no combination of candidates satisfies A and B and C together: the closest, ...`, closest over every rejection). `gave up after trying 250000 combinations of candidates for ...` is unchanged and is the safety net: it still triggers when a failure is made of every part.

Soundness. The search skips a part's other candidates, and the parts between, only when the failure does not depend on them, as before; what changed is how finely the dependence is found. The dependence a rejection has on an earlier pick is the one `Coupling::feeding` already stood on for deciding which bounds may be judged (a value is final when no part still to be chosen feeds it), so it is as sound as that. The gaps it does not close: a value that is worked out backwards, from a left side to an operand (propagation does not do so), and a value that depends on a pick through something that is not a field the solver follows (a hidden read). The test suite and the gate below did not find a pick that changed. The identity of every successful build is the same: the first solution in catalog order, which `conflict_core.rs` checks on a board where the pair's candidates are not the first.

Results. A real board whose largest group gave up and named no fault now ends with a message that names a few of its parts and the relation they cannot meet (the board is not described here). The invented board of `conflict_core.rs` (14 parts of 8 candidates, one sum over all of them, a bound on a pair that no two candidates meet): `gave up` after 250000 trials before, 904 trials after. Messages that changed: `backjump.rs` (two: the pair is named, not the whole chain), `joint_search.rs` (one: `u01` and `z`, not `u02`). Every example and example project prints the same.

Rejected. Recording a nogood for each failure and checking every choice against the list: the dependence above already says which picks to go back to, and a list of nogoods would add a check to every trial. Finding the smallest set again by trying subsets of the picks: a failure of a range judged with fewer picks chosen does not imply the failure with more, so the subset's failure would not be a proof. Naming every part that fed the failure even when no choice of it can matter (the old message): it hides the fault, which was the problem.

## Cost, limits, left open

The feeding sets are one traversal of the equations for each relation, once per solve. `try_candidate` compares every field's range before and after a candidate; no timing of it was taken, on the invented board or the real one. The hopeless scan costs one trial per part when its first candidate passes. The board that gave up on a pair of dividers whose tolerances cannot hold the window (two parts, a joint search of 2.9 million combinations) is not helped by this: it is a pair, not one part.

The inductor and its frequency resistor stay in separate groups with the order of path names deciding, which is the left-open item above. The real board's groups of 49 parts were not available here, so how many of them split by (b) is not known.

## A search that runs away is counted in work as well as trials, and the message says what to change (2026-11-07, provisional, issue #177)

A scratch build of a real 12 V automotive board, with a balancing resistance bounded by expressions over a cell's leakage current, ran over five minutes with no output. The search already had a limit (`MAX_TRIALS`, 250000 candidates tried) and a group that reached it ended with `gave up`, so the case that hung did not stop for a missing count: it was not near the limit. The limit counted trials, and a trial is not a fixed cost. Each one runs the propagation twice, and a pass of it reads every equation, relation and balance of the board to skip those whose fields did not change, so a trial costs what the board's size makes it cost. The invented board of `crates/netsmith-solve/tests/search_budget.rs` (eight parts in one sum that no choice reaches, 8 candidates each) shows it: 250000 trials took 1.8 s alone and 36 s with 1000 independent parts beside it (release build, one trial about 7 microseconds against 145). The real board's trials cost more again; ADR 0057 above measured 17 minutes to reach 250000 for a group of seven parts. The real board was not available here, so that it is this and not another cost that held the five minutes is the reading of the code and of the invented board, not a measurement of the real one.

What was done (`crates/netsmith-solve/src/solve.rs`, `catalog.rs`).

- **A second count, of work.** `Solver::work` adds the equations, relations and balances that each pass of `propagate` reads, one for each relation a trial is judged against, one for each candidate that a part's turn looks at, and one for each earlier failed choice that a candidate is compared with before it is tried. A search stops at `SearchBudget::trials` (250000, as before) or at `SearchBudget::work` (1000000000, about 20 seconds of a release build on the invented board), whichever it reaches first. Both are counts, not seconds, so the answer is the same on every machine and every run. The work of a board with a dozen relations is a few dozen units for a trial, so 250000 trials of it stay far from the work limit and end where they did.
- **The budget is an option of the catalogs**, `Catalogs::with_search_budget`, as the series and the guidelines are, so a test lowers it and reaches the limit with a small board. There is no manifest key and no flag: no program can state it.
- **The message names the parts, the count and what to change.** `gave up after trying N combinations of candidates for `a` and `b` ...; a bound reads values of all of them, so no part could be blamed alone. State a value for one of them, narrow a window that a bound on them reads, or split the group so that fewer parts share a bound`. The first twelve parts are named and the rest counted. When it was the work that ended the search, the message says so in brackets, and `N` is how many combinations had been tried by then. `Solution::work` carries the total, as `trials` does.

What does not change. A program that builds today builds as it did unless one search of it does more than 1000000000 units of work, which is tens of seconds of a release build at least; such a build ends with the message above instead. The meaning of a program, the picks and every other message are as they were. The old text, `narrow what they need or give them fewer choices`, is replaced.

Rejected. A time limit: the result would depend on the machine and its load, and a test of it would be flaky (a timer in the search was not added). A lower trial limit: a search of a small board may need many trials and finish. A limit over every group together: a board of many small groups does a large number of trials in all, none of them a runaway.

Left open. The limit is a constant of the solver, chosen from the invented board's speed, and the real board's trial cost was not measured; if a real search stops sooner than a person waits, the number comes down. The hopeless scan before a search (one trial per part and candidate) and the retry of linked groups after a failure (`search_again_together`) have a budget of their own each, as before, so a group that gives up and is searched again with another is given a second budget. Whether a failing group should say which part to state a value for is not done: the search does not know it.

## What to look at

- `try_candidate` in `crates/netsmith-solve/src/solve.rs`: the `changed` set and the condition `coupling.feeding[index].is_disjoint(later)`. This is the one place where a design that built could stop building.
