# Doc comments and relations are drawn as notes in the schematic

An engineer's hand-drawn schematic carries its reasons: a framed box with the calculation that gave a resistor its value, a line that says where a circuit was taken from, a warning that two connectors must be 22 mm apart. netsmith's source has the same writing (the `///` above a part, a module or an interface, and the `==` lines such as `.led.current == (.supply.voltage - .led.forward_voltage) / .r1.resistance`) and the schematic dropped it. It is now drawn as a **schematic note** (GLOSSARY). This is a drawing convention, not a change of the language: the checker's model already kept every doc comment, and each relation statement keeps its own text; no syntax or meaning changes.

**What becomes a note.** `Design` has a new field `notes: Vec<Note>` (`netsmith_core`; empty for a design that does not know them, so every `Design { .. }` elsewhere says `notes: Vec::new()`). A `Note` has the path of the instance it belongs to (board-relative, like `interfaces`), a kind (`Doc` or `Relation`), the text, where it is written (file and line), the paths of the parts it is about (`anchors`) and, for a relation, what the solver settled on for the field on its left (`led.current = 7.05mA..10.57mA`), when the model has it. Lowering builds them in source order from the checker's model (`Instance::docs`, `Instance::def_docs`, `Relation::written`), only these: the doc comments of the board and of each module that has a part, of each part declaration (a note about that part) and of each interface declaration with parts on its nets (about those parts, or the sheet's when more than three); and each `==` relation of a board or a module body, as written, about the parts whose fields it names. **Not drawn:** an ordinary `//` comment (the lexer drops it before the model); the docs of the standard library and the relations inside it or inside any part's own definition (the part's model, not the design); a relation of a physical file (it says where parts go on the board); the `//!` of a file; the docs of an interface or a module that nothing drawn is in (a manufacturing process); a relation `>=` or `<=`.

**Where a note goes.** After a sheet is drawn and every wire routed, in free room, so no wire is moved for a note: a note of a part stands beside the part, on a side no pin leaves by before one a pin leaves by, nearest first and sliding along the side; a relation of several parts stands in free room near the middle of the parts; a note of a board or a module stands in a strip at the top left, above the drawing and under the sheet's title. A note with no free place within 40 mm of its part (50 mm of the middle of its parts) goes to a margin, a column to its right (level with its part as far as the notes above allow) or a strip under the drawing, whichever leaves the sheet nearer its aim of 1.6 wide for each high. A sheet with more than eight notes puts all of them in one column at its right margin. A note is a rounded frame, 25 to about 90 mm wide, text wrapped at word boundaries by the page's letter widths, cut at 600 letters with an ellipsis (the whole text is the tooltip). The tie-breaks are fixed (distance, then position), so a drawing is the same every time, and a sheet with no notes is drawn byte for byte as it was.

**What `check` and the metrics do.** A note is no conductor: it is left out of the netlist the check rebuilds. Two rules are new: `note-overlap` (a note's frame lies on a symbol, text, wire, thick line of a bus, power symbol, flag, dot, X or another note) and `note-text-overflow` (a line of text, measured as it is drawn, escapes its frame). The metrics count `notes`, `note_area` and `note_overlaps` (target 0); their columns are printed only for a board that has a note.

**In the SVG.** `<g class="note note-doc|note-relation" data-note="<path>" data-n="<index>" data-ref="<reference of the first part>" data-at="<file>:<line>">`, a `note-frame` rect, a `note-heading` text and one selectable `note-text` text with a tspan for each line; a default look in the embedded style (written only into a drawing that has a note).

**Rejected.** Reconstructing a relation from the solver's expression tree (constants and paths lose the way the author wrote them); reading `//` comments for "TODO" or "NOTE:" lines (not source information the language states); a leader line from a note to its part (the owner wants none: nearness says it); notes as obstacles to the router before routing (it moves wires, and every existing drawing would change; room is found in what is drawn, and the margin takes the rest).

**Cost and left open.** A sheet with notes is bigger by the margin or the strip. A note beside a part may stand where a later change of the drawing would want room. Whether a relation of a library module (a `BuckStage` feedback relation) should be drawn, whether `>=`/`<=` relations should, and whether a module's doc comment should say which module it is when a small board draws modules as one sheet, are for the owner.

## Built in the polish

What was added after the first stage, with the default taken where the text above left a choice. The status above is unchanged.

- **Every part a note is about is in the drawing.** `<g class="note">` has `data-anchors="<reference> <reference> ..."`: the references of all the parts of the design's note (`Note::anchors`), in the order the note has them, each once, also those on another sheet; empty for the note of a sheet. `data-ref` stays the first of them that is on this sheet. The page lights a note with any of its parts and the parts with a note that has the focus from this list (`schematic/notes.ts`); it keeps reading a relation's words only for a drawing that has no `data-anchors`. A note-free drawing is unchanged to the byte.
- **A relation of rails only is about the parts on their nets.** `.cards.voltage == .supply.voltage * 0.9` names no part, so it stood in the sheet's strip. Its anchors are now the parts on the nets of the pins of each interface it names, as the doc comment of an interface has them: in natural path order, none when there are more than three (`MAX_DOC_ANCHORS`), and with the return net left out (a net that a return runs on and no rail draws from: ground has every part, so counting it would make every such relation the sheet's). A relation that names a part keeps the parts it names and takes none from a rail (`netsmith-lower`, `notes_of`). A relation of fields of a module's own (a ratio declared in the module) still names no part. Default taken: the doc comment of an interface already worked this way, and it needs no new syntax.
- **The ring of a note follows the zoom.** The ring was sized when the focus came and kept that size when the zoom changed under it (a ring 6 px clear at 240 % where it stood 2 px clear at 100 %). It is sized again when the zoom changes, and the frame keeps its resting width while the note has the focus (`--note-stroke`), so a hover or a lit note, whose frame is heavier, no longer changes the room the ring leaves. The recipe `schematic` measures the ring at two zoom levels.
- **Nets are not marked.** A rail's field names a net, and the page lights parts for a note; `data-anchors` stays parts only. A note lighting the nets it is about would be a new thing for the owner to decide (which net of a rail, and what a note of ground lights).

Not built, still open as in the section above: whether a relation of a library module is drawn; whether `>=` and `<=` relations are (they state a claim to verify, not what the circuit is, and the owner's question is open); and whether the note of a module says which module it is when a small board draws its modules as one picture. The last needs the drawing to know whether it holds one module or several, which no layout entry point passes down today, so it is a change of the layout's interface and not a polish; the proposal is a heading with the module's path on the note of a module that is not on a sheet of its own.
