# Long work can be cancelled, and says how far it has got

Meets tests 2 (a cancel is an unwind, not a `Result`, through the checker, the solver and the design stage) and 3 (a `Result` is the obvious way). The status line said "proposed (the mechanism, what a cancelled job leaves, the routes and the progress units are written down for review; the owner reviews the choice of unwinding over a `Result` through the solver, and that a cancelled analysis keeps the last reading)", so it stays `proposed`. The text before the cut is `git show 31d3394:docs/adr/0062-long-work-can-be-cancelled-and-says-how-far-it-has-got.md`. The mechanism, the numbers and the limits are the header of `crates/netsmith-core/src/job.rs`; what a cancelled job leaves is the header of `crates/netsmith/src/editor/analysis.rs`; the routes are `crates/netsmith-editor/src/server.rs`.

**Problem.** On a real 14-sheet board the first analysis takes 28 to 47 s in a debug build and every edit takes about as long. The page could say only `Solving Beta, 1 of 1`, and nothing could be stopped: the checker, the solver and the design stage are single long calls with no way out, the analysis holds the state's lock with the version and the stamps already advanced, a compare goes on after its page left, and `build` and `fab` could not stop (only the PCB router had a flag). The solve is more than 99 % of the time that is not drawing, and picking parts is all of it, so the checkpoints that matter are in the search.

**Decision.** `netsmith_core::job` has a `Cancel` token, `scope(&token, work)` that makes it the thread's token for the length of `work`, and `checkpoint()`, which ends the call if the thread's token is cancelled. It ends it by `std::panic::resume_unwind` with a `Cancelled` payload, which unwinds without running the panic hook; whoever started the call catches it with `job::catch`, which tells `Cancelled` from a panic. Checkpoints are placed once for each unit of work of about a millisecond or more. Each job has a token of its own and a job never stops another: the analysis of the live project, a compare, a build, a fab and an order. A cancelled job keeps nothing it was making. A cancelled analysis publishes nothing and the last reading stays, with `Analysis.cancelled` set until a file changes or `POST /api/analysis/run`. A cancelled first analysis leaves the server ready with an empty reading and one note, because a server that stayed pending would answer `503` for ever. A cancelled build or fab answers `499` and keeps whole boards or none, because every checkpoint is before the writing. A compare follows its page and is cancelled when its client leaves; a fab or a build is cancelled only by its button. The solver reports counts as it goes (parts prepared, fields scanned, parts chosen), never a percentage.

**Rejected.** A `Result` through the solver: it would change every signature in five crates and every test to carry one case that stops the call and drops everything it made, and the unwind does that correctly because the crates keep no state outside the call. A `&Cancel` parameter: `solve(board, catalogs)` is called from `fab`, `build`, the editor, the examples and a hundred tests, none of which has a token. A cancel that waits for the lock: the analysis holds it for its whole length. Publishing the analysis as it was when cancelled: a half-made snapshot has boards that are not drawn and problems that are not found. Marking the reading stale instead of keeping `cancelled`: stale is the word for a file that does not build, and a cancel is the person's choice. A background thread the cancel could abandon: a thread cannot be stopped from outside and runs to its end for nothing. Cancelling a fab when its page closes: it would turn a reload into the loss of a package that takes minutes.

**The cost.** The unwind needs `panic = "unwind"`, the default, and every crate it passes through must keep no state outside the call (no cache, no lock, no thread, no global); a new crate that does breaks it. A checkpoint is a thread-local read, and inside a scope one relaxed atomic load. A call is stopped at its next checkpoint, so the latency is the longest stretch between two: a trial of the search, and longer for a solver slower per trial than the board measured. Nothing inside one `try_candidate`, one sheet's wire routing or one placer move can be interrupted, and a cancel that arrives after the last checkpoint is too late. KiCad's own design rules check and the check of parts against LCSC cannot be cancelled. The router's `Stop` button stays another thing: it keeps what it routed.

**Left open.** For the owner, word for word: "the owner reviews the choice of unwinding over a `Result` through the solver, and that a cancelled analysis keeps the last reading". Not measured: the latency of the drawing of a sheet of a real board, which can be long between two checkpoints (the layout's inner routing has none).
