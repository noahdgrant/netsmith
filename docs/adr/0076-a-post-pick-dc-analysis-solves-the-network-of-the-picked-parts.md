# A post-pick DC analysis solves the network of the picked parts

## The problem

The owner asked whether netsmith could solve the circuits "using typical circuit analysis techniques". Today it is a constraint solver. It picks parts, propagates ranges through the equations that the library and the design write, and applies Kirchhoff's current law to add up the currents on a net (ADR 0007, ADR 0014, ADR 0036). It never builds the network of the picked parts and solves node voltages and branch currents together. A range per field and one equation per relation are enough for a divider written as an equation (`.top.current == .vin.voltage / (...)`). They are not enough for the circuit around it, and the cost shows on a real 12 V automotive board (about 60 parts, two buck stages), whose build prints 47 notes:

- 26 relations are `not evaluated`: 20 are the drop and the power of ten passive parts (`.drop == .current * .resistance`, `.drop * .current <= .max_power`), four are the current rating of a MOSFET or a buffer driving a pulled-down line, two read a USB pin nothing drives. The ten resistors are the pull-downs of the modem's lines, its GNSS divider, and the series resistors into an ADC and a PDM input. Each has no `current`, because the net it is on has two or more unknown currents and ADR 0026 refuses to subtract ranges (their residual says nothing about one part).
- 10 notes say a net's tracks are the process's least because the current of a part on it `is not known` (ADR 0036, amendment 3).
- 10 notes are logic levels: six from an I2C bus whose parts state no `voh` (ADR 0075 applies where a part states `vol`; whether it applies to these parts was not established), two from a microphone, two absolute maximums.
- 1 note says which generic was picked.

Two more things are written by hand. The sheet states `vout.voltage == vin.voltage - fuse.drop - diode.drop` for the protection chain, and the board states `reg5v3.vin.voltage &= 7V..16V` as a seed, because the regulator's input current depends on its input voltage, the drops of the fuse, the diode, the shunt and the bead depend on that current, and the input voltage depends on the drops: a circle of values. Without the seed the build fails (`reg5v3.stage.input_cap.bias is not known`). The seed is not a requirement. It hides a fact: the chain cannot give less than 8.05 V, so the window starts 1 V too low. GitHub issue #153 records both.

## Decision (recommended)

Add one pass, a DC operating-point analysis of the picked parts, between the pick and the final verification of the relations. Build a netlist from the parts that state a DC element (a resistor with its tolerance, a fuse, a diode with its forward drop, a rail with its range, a regulator that conserves power, a logic pin that is high, low or high impedance, a MOSFET as a switch). Cut it into islands at nodes whose voltage is held. For each island, solve every corner of the stated ranges and every state of the pins and switches as a modified nodal analysis, and keep the range each node voltage, branch current and power takes, with the state that made each end. Write the results back as narrowings of `.current`, `.drop` and `.voltage` where they are unstated, and as findings where a stated value or a rating is exceeded. The relations that were `deferred` are then evaluated, the tracks are sized from known currents, and the levels check reads node voltages.

It is additive for a design that already builds: a range that the pass narrows was wider, a relation it evaluates was a note. It does not replace the cheap bound of section 7, which should come first because it needs no language decision and is the only help when an island has a pin nothing models. It does not pick parts, and it does not model time.

## 1. Where it runs, what it consumes, what it produces

`solve_model` in `crates/netsmith-solve/src/solve.rs` propagates, seeds circles, picks, propagates, seeds again, checks the windows, then verifies every relation and lists the ones with an unknown as `deferred`. The pass goes after `check_windows` and before that loop, and is followed by one more `propagate`, so that the equations see what it narrowed. It runs on the solver's `Model` and not on the lowered `Design`, because the relations to feed live in the model's fields and lowering already reads the solved currents from there (`plan_tracks`, ADR 0036).

It consumes the nets of the checker (the pins that `~`, `~>` and the `bridge` of each part join), each picked part's values with their tolerances (a catalog row's `resistance: 10kohm +/- 1%`, a fuse's `resistance`, a bead's `dc_resistance: 0ohm..70mohm`), each rail's stated `voltage` range and any stated `current`, and the logic levels of each pin. It produces, for each net and each branch, the range of the voltage, the current and the power over every consistent combination, and for each end of each range the combination that produced it (`i(f1) at most 0.997 A: battery 9 V, diode 0.8 V, shunt 50.5 mohm, bead 70 mohm, 5.3 V stage at 5.5 V and 91.5 %, 3.8 V stage at 3.914 V and 80 %, modem 1.2 A`). A finding names those states, which is the "which line states produced them" of the request.

A combination whose states are not the circuit's (a diode blocking while its anode is at 9 V and its cathode at 0 V, a MOSFET off with its gate 3.2 V above its source) is refused and not counted. A combination in which a power-conserving element asks for more than the source can give through the resistance in between has no operating point; that is a finding (`the source cannot give 8.1 W through 0.15 ohm`), reported when the states are consistent and never as a side effect of a refused state.

## 2. The element models

Every element is a stamp of the modified nodal analysis: node voltages and one current for each element that holds a voltage between two nodes. Prototype: `crates/netsmith-dc/src/netlist.rs` and `op.rs`.

| Element | Model | Ranges and states tried |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resistor, fuse, shunt, bead | conductance `1/R` (a resistance under 1 mohm is taken as 1 mohm) | the two ends of the tolerance (`10kohm +/- 1%` is 9.9 kohm and 10.1 kohm; a bead's `0..70 mohm`) |
| Rail | ideal voltage source between `pos` and `neg`, and the stated `.current` as a current source on the net | the two ends of `.voltage`; a current limit is not modelled (a finding if the solution exceeds the stated `.max_current`, as today) |
| Regulator, switching | ideal output source `Vout`; input draws `Iq + Vout * Iout / (eff * Vin)` | the ends of `Vout` and of `eff`; the fixed point below |
| Regulator, linear | output source; input draws `Iout + Iq` | the ends of `Vout` |
| Series chain `~>` | nothing of its own: the chain is the netlist of the parts in it, in the order of their `bridge` | as the parts |
| Diode, Schottky | blocks, or holds a drop `Vf` between anode and cathode and carries a current of at least zero | blocking; conducting at each end of `Vf` (a catalog row's `0V..0.8V`) |
| Logic pin | high: its node joined to its own supply through `roh`; low: to its own ground through `rol`; high impedance: a leakage resistance `rz` to its supply or to its ground | the states the pin's kind allows (an output `H` and `L`, an input `Z`, a bidirectional pin all three, an open-drain pin `L` and `Z`), the ends of `roh` and `rol`, both leak directions |
| MOSFET | `Rds(on)` between drain and source, or open; the gate draws nothing | on and off; the pair is refused when the gate-source voltage is below the least threshold while on, or above the greatest while off; a voltage inside the threshold's range keeps both |
| Capacitor, TVS below its stand-off | open circuit | none |
| Load of constant power | draws `P / V` | the ends of `P` |

The fixed point. A regulator's input current depends on its input voltage, which depends on the drops its own current makes: `Iin = P / (eff * (Vsrc - R * Iin))`. The pass starts from `Iin = 0`, solves the linear network, computes each regulator's and constant-power load's current from the solution, and repeats until no current changes by more than 1e-10 of the largest (halving the step after 60 rounds). Starting from zero, the iterates rise monotonically to the smallest fixed point if there is one, and the voltage at the input falls to zero if there is none, which is the collapse: 12 V through 10 ohm gives at most `12^2 / (4 * 10)` = 3.6 W, and a regulator that asks for 5.6 W is reported. The real board's chain settles in at most nine rounds. A nonlinear solve by Newton would converge faster and is not needed: no island of this board needs more than nine.

A logic pin's high is the pin's own supply through `roh`, and not a voltage range of its own. The levels check of ADR 0046 uses the guaranteed ends (`voh` as the least of a high, `vol` as the greatest of a low), which is the right comparison for a reader and the wrong one for a current: a pull-down carries the most when the pin is as high as its supply can make it. A pin on a rail also follows the rail's range and cannot be above it. A leakage as a current source was tried and refused: 1 uA through a 1 Mohm pull-down is a volt, which the pin's clamps would not allow. A leakage as a resistance (`Ii` at `Vi` is `Vi / Ii`, 3.3 Mohm for 1 uA at 3.3 V) cannot do that.

## 3. Ranges: corners per island, not intervals

Interval arithmetic counts a value once for each time it is written. `10 * R2 / (R1 + R2)` with both resistors `1 kohm +/- 1 %` is `4.90 V..5.10 V` that way and `4.95 V..5.05 V` for the circuit; ADR 0026 is the same fault on currents. A corner solve gives each resistor one value in each solve and so counts nothing twice. For every combination of the ends of the ranges (times the states) the network is solved exactly, and the range of a quantity is the hull of the results. Two resistors of 1 % are four solves; the real board's whole power tree (13 elements, a battery from 9 V to 16 V, a diode, three regulators with ranges, three loads) is 12288 combinations, of which 8192 are consistent.

Islands keep the count small. A node held by an ideal source (a rail, a regulator's output) separates what is on its two sides as far as voltage goes, so each side is its own island and the current that one island takes from the node is a range that the other treats as a current source. The cost is the correlation between that current and the node's voltage, which makes the result wider and never narrower. The board's logic lines are a pin, a resistor or two and a rail: 24 to 256 combinations each. Four of the modem's islands (96, 24, 80 and 96 combinations) are four small solves, where one island of all four would be 17.7 million combinations and over the limit of section 6. A power tree cut at the 5.3 V stage's output takes 192 combinations for the part in front of it (section 6).

What a corner enumeration does not promise. Its range is the hull of its corners, which is the circuit's range only where the quantity is monotone in each value. A node voltage and a branch current of a network with one source are, and so is any quantity of a series chain; a ladder with two sources, or a power (`V^2 / R`), need not be. In the prototype's tests, `p(r1)` of `R1` from 500 to 1500 ohm over a fixed `R2` of 1000 ohm peaks inside the box (25 mW at `R1 = R2`, where the corners give 22.2 mW and 24 mW). The pass checks itself: `check_interior` draws random points inside the ranges and states and counts the results outside the hull; on every island of the real board it found none in about 150 to 300 solved points, which is evidence and no proof. For a power of a resistor with a tolerance of 1 % the error is second order (about `(tolerance / 2)^2`, 0.003 %), and the proposal is to take a stated margin of that size for the power of any element with a range wider than 5 %, and to name the quantity when the interior check fails. A sound enclosure for the cases that remain (interval Newton or affine arithmetic over the network) is possible and is not built.

## 4. How the result feeds back

- **The `not evaluated` checks.** A resistor's current and drop are narrowed to the solution, so `.drop == .current * .resistance` and `.drop * .current <= .max_power` are evaluated by the verification that follows, with the numbers: the 20 relations of the real board. A rating that the solution exceeds fails with the states that made it. The four current ratings of drivers need the circuit behind the pin (the modem's pull-up on a MOSFET's drain, a buffer's load), which no part states: they stay `not evaluated` and the note names the boundary (section 5).
- **`.current` of parts whose current is unknown.** The same narrowing. The rule of ADR 0026 (a part's current is set from a balance of two terms, never from a longer one) is unchanged for what the pass does not model, and is not needed for what it does: the currents of a modelled network are solved together, so nothing is subtracted.
- **Track widths (ADR 0036).** A carrier whose current is known no longer makes a net `not sized`; the ten notes of the real board go away. With the netlist the net's current can be what a corner really puts through it, the largest over the corners of the sum of the currents into the net. That removes the two pessimisms amendment 2 states (loads are not correlated; a regulator's ground counts the input and the load's return). It is the second step; the first keeps the sum of ADR 0036.
- **The open circle and the seed.** The pass solves the circle numerically, so no seed is needed for a check made after the picks. A pick that reads a value before the circle is solved still needs one: the real board's seed exists because the input capacitor's bias (its derating) is read when it is picked. Two ways to remove it are in the plan (phase 3): pick the series parts first, run the pass, then pick the parts that read its results; or run the pass once before the picks with the range every candidate of an unpicked part could have. Either makes the hand-written `vout.voltage == vin.voltage - fuse.drop - diode.drop` redundant, which is issue #153's option (a) with the pass as its engine: a `~>` over rails carries the source's voltage less the drops.
- **Levels (ADR 0046, 0064, 0075).** `net_range` takes the hull of the rails and drivers it can see. The pass gives the net's voltage over the states of its drivers. An open-drain line's high is then the node voltage with every driver high impedance (3.186 V to 3.399 V for a 4.7 kohm pull-up to 3.3 V +/- 3 % with three devices leaking 1 uA at 3.3 V; a test of the prototype), and its low is the node voltage with one driver low through its `rol`. ADR 0075's inference ("states `vol` and no `voh`") would become the part's state set, `L` and `Z`, if the owner adds a marker for it (ADR 0075, left open).
- **A stated value is a budget (ADR 0038).** A range stated on a current or a voltage is an allowance. The pass narrows an unstated field and checks a stated one: if the circuit needs more than the allowance the finding says what the circuit needs and which states give it, as ADR 0038 does for a complete balance.

## 5. What it replaces, what it adds, what it cannot do

It replaces, where an island is complete: the hand-written equations that restate a series chain or a divider (`.top.current == .vin.voltage / (...)`, `.vout.voltage == .vin.voltage - ...`), the seed, the `not evaluated` notes for passives, the `current ... is not known` notes, and the pessimism of two stated sums. It adds: node voltages (a divider's middle, a cell's share of a supercapacitor, a diode's drop at the current it carries, a regulator's input voltage), the state of a switch, the power of every element, a regulator's dissipation (`p(reg5v3)` 0.16 mW to 0.68 W on the real board), and a finding for a source that cannot give what its load asks.

What it cannot do, by design:

- It does not pick parts. A pick is made before it runs (ADR 0057: a relation that is not evaluated cannot refuse a candidate), so a resistor whose rating the pass finds too low is a finding that names the part, and the design narrows it; the search does not learn from it. Running the pass inside the search for each candidate multiplies its cost by the search's 250000 trials and is rejected.
- Transients are out of scope: inrush, a supercapacitor charging from empty (`V / 10 ohm` = 0.55 A in the real board's own words; the pass sees the capacitor as open), switching, ripple, AC, stability.
- A regulator's efficiency is one range, not a curve over its load and input, and an input below the part's minimum is a warning and not a shutdown.
- Temperature is a stated tolerance and nothing else: no hot resistance, no derating, no self-heating.
- A MOSFET's body diode and a diode's reverse leakage are not modelled, so a reversed battery through a P-channel switch is not seen.
- A pin that nothing models (an IC's signal pin with no drive model, an interface with no DC element) is a boundary: the island stops there, the currents that depend on it stay `not evaluated`, and the note names the pin and what is missing. Nothing is guessed. On the real board the modem's own pull-up on ON_OFF, the buffer inputs' load and the ADC's input current are such boundaries; the prototype's islands state them as assumptions, marked in the file.

## 6. Performance and determinism

The editor re-solves on every edit, and the build of the real board takes about 14 s with a debug binary and 1.25 s optimised (the comment in the root `Cargo.toml`). The budget proposed: the pass costs at most 5 % of the solver's time and 100 ms for the whole board in an optimised build, and a debug build of the crate is optimised like the solver's (`[profile.dev.package.netsmith-dc] opt-level = 2`). The way to meet it:

1. Islands cut at held nodes, so combinations add and do not multiply (above).
2. A cache per island keyed by its netlist and its boundary ranges: an edit that changes one resistor solves the island it is in, and the other islands' results are reused.
3. A limit of 2^22 combinations per island, with an error that says to cut at a node (`cut the island at a node whose voltage is held`), so that a design never waits unannounced.
4. For an island that is still too large, the extremes of a quantity need not be searched in the corners: the sign of its derivative with respect to each value (one extra solve per value, at the centre) says which end of each range gives its maximum, which is `n + 2` solves a quantity for a monotone one. Not built; it is the way past 2^n if a design needs it.

Measured on the prototype (a release build; the same code in a debug build is 5 to 15 times slower, 1 s for the unsplit tree): the five modem pull-downs and dividers, the battery divider, the series resistors and the supercapacitor's divider take 13 us to 380 us each, 0.74 ms for all of them. The whole power tree, unsplit, takes 66 ms (12288 combinations, 8192 consistent, at most nine rounds of the fixed point; 5 us a combination). Cut at the 5.3 V stage's output, the part in front is 192 combinations and 0.46 ms, 145 times faster, and its answer is 10 % wider (the fuse's current at most 1.098 A and not 0.997 A) because the load behind it is a range that no longer follows the output voltage.

The pass is deterministic. The elements are taken in the order the model lists them, the combinations in a fixed mixed-radix order, and nodes are numbered in the order they are first named, so no result depends on a hash order. The solver uses addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and comparison only, which IEEE 754 defines to the bit, with no transcendental function and no fused multiply-add, so the same design gives the same numbers on any target. The interior check uses a fixed seed and is for tests and for the debug build only.

## 7. Alternatives

**The cheap bound.** Bound an unknown current by Ohm's law over the voltages at the two ends, `|I| <= max |Va - Vb| / R`, and the power by `max (Va - Vb)^2 / R`, taking the ends from `net_range` (ADR 0046), which already gives a net's voltage over the rails, drivers and resistors it can see. It uses no new concept and no language, and it is sound. On the real board's pull-downs it gives what the pass gives, because the pin's supply is the whole drop: `3.333 V / 9.9 kohm` = 336.7 uA for the modem's gate pull-down, the same as the pass. It is looser where the node between two resistors has no voltage of its own: 168 uA for the GNSS divider's top resistor and 224 uA for its bottom one against 96 uA, 0.75 mW against 0.14 mW. And it can be wrong in a way that matters. `R16`, a 100 ohm series resistor into a PDM input, has `[0, 3.333] V` at both ends, so the bound is 33.7 mA and `3.333^2 / 99 ohm` = 112 mW against a rating of 62.5 mW: a failure for a part that carries the input's 1 uA, because the bound does not know that the pin draws nothing. It is the fault of ADR 0026 (the dependency of two ranges that are not independent). Used as a verdict it would refuse a good design. Used pass-only it is safe: a relation that the bound satisfies is evaluated and its note goes away; one it fails stays `not evaluated`, and says nothing. On the real board that closes the nine of the ten passives whose bound passes (the six pull-downs, the divider's two resistors, the ADC's series resistor), and does nothing for `R16`, the power tree, the fuse, the shunt and the bead (their ends span the whole supply: 16 V over 50 mohm is 323 A), the drop carried along a chain, or a diode's state.

The DC pass does not make it unnecessary. The bound is the fallback for an island with a pin nothing models, where the pass stops, and it is the only one of the two a pick can use, because it is a relation and not a post-pick pass. And it should come first: it waits on no language decision (the pass waits on question 1 below), it is one rule in `solve.rs` over code that exists, and it closes most of the passive notes of this board. Where an island is complete, the pass's answer is the same or tighter, and replaces the bound's.

**The pass inside the search.** Rejected for cost (section 5).

**A circuit simulator.** Run ngspice or write netsmith's own transient solver. Rejected: a dependency that does not run in the editor's loop, a model per part to write, and a transient answer to a question that the design states as a worst case.

**A sound enclosure by interval Newton or affine arithmetic.** It would remove the monotone assumption of section 3, at the price of a more difficult solver and wider answers where the network has loops. Not now; the interior check says when it is needed.

**Equations only (issue #153).** (a) `~>` over rails carries the source's voltage less the parts' drops as an equation, (b) circles start from a source's bounds. Each is a rule that does what the pass does for one shape. (a) needs the regulator's input current, which is the circle itself, and (b) leaves the seed to the solver's range search of ADR 0053. The pass is the engine that makes (a) exact, and the plan below builds (a) on it.

## 8. What the prototype shows

`crates/netsmith-dc` is about 1650 lines of source and 650 of tests, with no dependencies, and nothing in the workspace depends on it, so `netsmith build` is unchanged. Its 23 tests are hand calculations (a divider, in which the corners give `4.95 V..5.05 V` and interval arithmetic `4.90 V..5.10 V`; a pull-down in each state of a pin; a fuse and a diode in a chain, and the diode blocking a reversed supply; a regulator whose input current depends on its input voltage through a 0.5 ohm drop, `0.45 I^2 - 10.8 I + 5 = 0`, `I = 0.4723 A`, against `0.463 A` without the drop; two regulators in a row; a collapse; a MOSFET off for a low gate and on for a high one; an open-drain line; a supercapacitor's divider with cell leakage) and eight islands of the real board, each checked against a closed form worked out in the test. "Today" for the power tree was evaluated by hand from the board's own equations: `build` does not print solved values, and no current was read from the tool's model. The others are the notes the build printed.

Two things the prototype showed that were not expected. The cell voltage of the supercapacitor is a quantity a netlist gives and the equations did not: with the board's own 6 uA as the largest leakage and the worst case of 0 uA on the other cell, the balance resistors of 43 kohm leave one cell 0.16 V above half the rail at the top of the rail's range. Whether that is a defect is the board's author's to say; the 6 uA is the board's own assumption, and applying it to one cell only is the worst case. And the efficiency window of the 5.3 V stage (0.915 to 1.0, from the part's curve) is the widest term of the result: the upper end, 100 %, is not a physical efficiency, and a tighter window changes the chain's current more than any tolerance of a resistor does.

## 9. Phased plan

0. This ADR and the prototype. Nothing else changes.
1. **The cheap bound, pass-only.** One rule in the solver: a part with a `current` that nothing else gives is bounded by `max |Va - Vb| / R` from `net_range`; a relation that the bound satisfies is evaluated, one that it fails is left `not evaluated`; tracks are sized from the bound as a lower bound already is (ADR 0036, amendment 3). No syntax. Closes about nine of the ten passives on the real board. Needs a test with an invented board of each kind (a pull-down, a divider, a series resistor into an input, a part whose bound fails).
2. **The pass, behind a decision on question 1.** The model extraction (the netlist from the checker's nets and the parts' DC statements), the islands, the writes of narrowings, the findings with their states, the cache, and the tests of the prototype ported to the solver's own types. The `not evaluated` notes of passives go; the pass's islands replace the bound where complete. The guide gets a section ("DC analysis") and the glossary the terms _Operating point_, _Island_ and _Corner_ with their _Avoid_ lines.
3. **Seed removal and `~>` carrying voltage.** Pick the series parts, run the pass, then pick the parts that read its results (a capacitor's bias), or run it once before the picks on the hull of the unpicked parts' candidates. The board's seed and its hand-written drops go; issue #153's option (a) is a consequence.
4. **Levels and tracks from the netlist.** Node voltages in the levels check (the open-drain high as a node voltage; `roh`, `rol` and `Ii` as members of `Levels`, question 2); the track width from the corner-wise sum of currents into a net. Optional: the sensitivity corners (section 6, item 4) and the interval-Newton enclosure, if the interior check ever fails on a real design.

## 10. Questions for the owner (language-design choices)

**Decisions of the owner, 2026-11-07.** 1: (b), a `dc` statement in the part or the generic. 2: (b), the datasheet's own form (a level with its test current), from the beginning and not the members of (a); the exact syntax is written in phase 2 and shown to the owner first. 3: no feature for which states can happen together, and the limit is documented (the pass tries every combination of pin states, which is the worst case, and a design cannot say that two lines are never high together; `docs/LANGUAGE.md` says so when the pass is added, with issue #45). 4: (a), narrow where unstated and check where stated. 5: a stated `.current` on a boundary pin. 6: severities as recommended. 7: capacitors stay open at DC, no charging state now. The options and the reasons follow as they were proposed.


1. **How does a part say what its DC element is?** (a) A table in the solver keyed by the standard generics' names (`Resistor`, `Fuse`, `Schottky`, `FerriteBead`, `NMosfet`), no syntax. A part with another name does not take part, and the tool holds knowledge about library kinds that the library does not state. (b) A statement in the part or the generic, so that the library says what is always true of it:

   ```nsm-fragment
   generic Fuse { dc resistor .a .b .resistance }
   generic Schottky { dc diode .anode .cathode .forward_voltage }
   generic FerriteBead { dc resistor .a .b .dc_resistance }
   ```

   A part with a `bridge` and no `dc` statement is a boundary and is named. (c) The pass recognises `.drop == .current * R` with a `bridge`, and nothing is written. It needs no syntax, and a part whose relations take another shape is silently left out. Recommended: (b). It states in one place what each library part is for the pass, it keeps `docs/DESIGN-RULES.md`'s "a part states its own", and a part that has none is visible.
2. **How does a logic pin state its drive and its leakage?** The levels give `voh` and `vol` as guaranteed bounds and no current, so an output resistance cannot be derived. (a) Members `roh`, `rol` and `ii` on `Levels` (or `Logic`), optional (ADR 0074), that a part states from its datasheet. (b) The datasheet's test current beside the level (`.voh &= .vdd.voltage - 0.4V at 2mA`), from which `roh` is `(vdd - voh) / ioh`; this is how datasheets state it, and it needs syntax. (c) A fixed assumption in the tool (0 to 200 ohm, 1 uA); the prototype's islands do this and say so. `docs/DESIGN-RULES.md` makes an assumption the last resort. Recommended: (a) for the first step, since a member exists already, and (b) if the owner wants the datasheet's own form.
3. **Which states can happen together?** The pass tries every combination of states of the pins on an island, which is the worst case (issue #45 states the same about loads that do not run together). A design that knows two lines are never high together could say so; how is the owner's. Recommended: none until a real design needs it.
4. **Does the pass narrow fields or only report?** (a) Narrow where unstated, check where stated (as an equation does, and as ADR 0038 treats a budget). (b) Report only, and leave every field to the solver as today. With (b) the notes stay. Recommended: (a). And for the hand-written equations that restate what the netlist says: left as they are (they agree and pass), and the guide stops showing them once the `~>` chain carries voltage (phase 3).
5. **What does a boundary look like?** A pin with no DC element ends an island. May a board state what is behind it (a stated `.current` on the pin, as a rail has one), or only a part (question 1)? Recommended: a stated `.current`, the way a design states a load now.
6. **Severity.** A failed rating found by the pass is an error like any relation. A source that cannot give what its load asks, and an input below a regulator's minimum, are errors too. A node voltage above an absolute maximum is an error as ADR 0046 already says. A pass that has an island it cannot cut small enough is a note that names it. Recommended: as stated.
7. **Capacitors in the pass.** At DC a capacitor is open. A supercapacitor charging from empty is a different state (a source of 0 V behind the ESR), which the pass could try as a state of the capacitor. Out of scope here. Recommended: not now.

## Amendment 1 (provisional): the syntax of `dc` and of a level with its test current

Written for phase 2, stage 1, on the owner's decisions of section 10 (question 1, a `dc` statement in the part or the generic; question 2, a level with its test current) and with the owner's authority to fix the exact syntax within today's grammar. The owner confirms or changes it. It adds the syntax, its checks and the records the checker keeps. It changes no solver behaviour: nothing reads the records yet, so a build is the same with the statements as without.

**The grammar.**

```text
dc-statement = "dc" element operand* NEWLINE        operand = path (`.a`, `.vin.pos`)
dc resistor  <terminal> <terminal> <resistance>
dc diode     <anode> <cathode> <forward voltage>
dc nmos      <gate> <drain> <source> <gate threshold> <on resistance>
dc pmos      <gate> <drain> <source> <gate threshold> <on resistance>

statement    = path op expression [ "at" expression ]    op = `&=` | `?=` | `==` | `>=` | `<=`
```

`dc` is a keyword at the start of a statement when a word follows it, as `bridge` is, so a member called `dc` still works. `at` is read after the right side of a constraint; a member called `at` still works, because no value can be followed by a word.

**What a `dc` statement says.** A part is one element at DC, and says which, once, with the pins and the fields it is made of (ADR 0076 section 2 gives each model):

- `dc resistor .a .b .resistance`: a resistance between two pins, over the range of the field. Used for a resistor, a fuse and a ferrite bead (`.dc_resistance`).
- `dc diode .anode .cathode .forward_voltage`: blocks, or holds the forward voltage between anode and cathode and carries a current of at least zero.
- `dc nmos .gate .drain .source .gate_threshold .on_resistance` and `dc pmos ...` with the same operands: a MOSFET as a switch. The channel is `.on_resistance` between drain and source when the gate drive is above the threshold and open when it is below; the gate draws nothing. The two words differ in the polarity of the drive, and both are written with magnitudes (see `std::parts::PMosfet`).

The pins are the first operands and the values the last, in the order above, so a reader and the checker know which is which. A value operand is a field of the part with the quantity of its place: a resistance for a resistor, a voltage for a diode's drop and a threshold. A pin operand is a pin of the part, directly or in one of its interfaces. The two or three pins of an element are different. A part has one `dc` statement. When a part has a `bridge` too, the pins the element runs between (both of a resistor or a diode, the drain and the source of a transistor) are the two pins the `bridge` joins, in either order, because a `~>` chain passes through the element. Statement order does not matter: the check waits until the whole part is read.

**Where it is written and who has it.** In a `part`, or in a `generic`. A provider of a generic takes the generic's items as it takes its `bridge` (ADR 0068), so `generic Fuse { ... dc resistor .a .b .resistance }` gives every provider of `Fuse` the element on its own pins and fields, and a provider that states a second `dc` is refused with the place of the first. A module and a board have none: the network of a module is the elements of the parts inside it, found by walking the instances. A `dc` in a module, a board or a physical file is an error. A part with a `bridge` and no `dc` is a boundary (question 1), and the analysis names it; this stage does not name anything.

**The standard library.** `Resistor`, `Fuse`, `FerriteBead`, `Diode`, `Schottky`, `NMosfet` and `PMosfet` state their element, as the table of section 2 has them. The others are left out until each has its own decision: `Inductor` (the winding's `.dc_resistance` is a resistor, and it is not in the table), `Led` (a diode whose drop is `.forward_voltage`), `Switch` (a closed contact), `Zener` and `Tvs` (conducting only past a voltage that the part states in another form), `Capacitor` (open at DC, question 7: it needs no element), and the regulators, the level translator and the logic pins (they are not one of the four kinds: see "Left open").

**A level with its test current.** `.voh &= .vdd.voltage - 0.4V at 2mA` is the datasheet's `VOH = VDD - 0.4 V at IOH = 2 mA`. It means exactly what the statement means without `at`: the same narrowing or relation, the same levels check (ADR 0046), the same everything. `at` adds one fact, the current the level was measured at, which the checker records with the level. The analysis derives from it the pin's output resistance of question 2 (`roh` at most `(vdd - voh) / 2 mA`, `rol` at most `vol / 8 mA`, and at least zero); that derivation is stage 2's, and with no `at` the pin has none stated. Rules: `at` goes on `voh` or `vol` of a `Logic` or of a `Levels` (a `vih` and a `vil` are thresholds and have no load); the current is one positive number written in the statement (not a range, not an expression of other fields, since it is what the datasheet prints); a level is stated at one load, so a second `at` on the same level is refused with the place of the first (a datasheet that gives `VOH` at two currents is two statements of two different values, and a level holds one).

**Rejected.**

- *Words for each operand* (`dc resistor between .a .b value .resistance`). Longer, and the checker still has to know which operands each element takes. The position is fixed by the element, as in a `pins` row.
- *A block* (`dc { element: resistor, a: .a ... }`). A block for one line, and a second way to name a pin.
- *The element in the `bridge`* (`bridge .a ~> .b as resistor`), or inferred from the field names. A second spelling for `bridge`, and an inference the library cannot see, which question 1 refused as option (c).
- *One word for both MOSFETs with a polarity operand* (`dc mosfet n ...`). The polarity is the part's kind, as `NMosfet` and `PMosfet` are two generics; two words keep the statement as long as the others.
- *Members `roh`, `rol` and `ii` for the drive* (question 2, option (a)). The owner chose the datasheet's form.
- *The test current before the level* (`.voh at 2mA &= ...`) or in the unit (`0.4V@2mA`). The statement reads as the datasheet does, a value and then where it was measured, and the left side stays a plain field.

**What the checker records** (stage 2 reads these, in `crates/netsmith-check`): `Instance::dc: Option<DcElement>` (`src/model.rs`), with `DcKind`, the pins and the fields as `InstId`s and `FieldId`s in the order of `DcKind::pin_roles` and `DcKind::value_roles`, and the source place; `Model::dc_elements()` walks the parts that have one. `Model::level_tests() -> &[LevelTest]` and `Model::level_test(field)`: for a level field, the test current as a `Quantity`, the operator and the statement's place. Both are in the model of each board and of each system.

**Left open.** (1) The leakage of a pin in the datasheet's form, `Ii` at a test voltage `Vi`: there is no member to state it on (`Logic` and `Levels` have the four levels only), and adding one is a change to those interfaces that is the owner's. (2) The element kinds of the regulator (a power-conserving source, section 2), the logic pin and the constant-power load: the regulator's efficiency and quiescent current are fields of the part, and how a `dc` statement names an output, an input, a supply and a ground is a design of its own; this amendment gives no `dc regulator`. (3) Two elements in one part (a MOSFET's body diode, section 5 says it is not modelled). (4) Whether a part with a `bridge` and no `dc` should be an error at the checker or only a note of the analysis: it is the analysis's note here (question 1). (5) The count of the elements a part is, for a part that holds two (a dual diode, a resistor network): a `dc` statement for each, which needs a name for each, is not designed.

## Amendment 2 (provisional): the DC analysis of the resistive islands (phase 2, stage 2a)

Written with the code of `crates/netsmith-solve/src/dc/` and `src/solve/dcpass.rs`. It builds the pass of section 1 for the elements that need no further decision: resistors (a fuse and a ferrite bead are resistors), diodes, the board's own supplies and what a part draws. The owner's decisions of section 10 stand; the choices below are the engineer's, within them, and each is a line to change.

**Where it runs.** After `check_windows` and before the verification of the relations, followed by one propagation (section 1). The cheap bound of section 7 is unchanged and still runs after it: it gets what the pass leaves, which is every island that has something the pass does not model.

**What is a source, and what is not.** A `Rail` that the board itself declares (its parent is a `board`), whose `.voltage` a statement narrowed and has finite ends, is an ideal source between its `pos` and its `neg`: the `neg` net is held at zero and the `pos` net over the stated range. Not a source: a rail of a part (what the part draws), a rail of a module (a port, whose range may be a seed such as `reg5v3.vin.voltage &= 7V..16V` or a promise), and a board's rail whose voltage the solver worked out. The reason is the one of section 4: a range held on a node whose voltage a circuit sets would separate the voltage from the current that makes it, and every drop in the chain would be taken between two independent values. The seed of issue #153 stays a seed, and the pass solves the chain below it as an island of its own, with the board's supply as its source. Two rails whose nets are the same hold their hull; a net that is a ground and a positive, or a positive over two grounds, makes the island that touches it unsolved.

**What is a load.** A rail of a part, or a rail of the board that is not a source, with a `.current` that has a value and is never negative: a current source over that range from its `pos` to its `neg` (a rail's current is what is drawn at `pos`, ADR 0014). A rail that can give current (a negative end) is an output that holds its net, a regulator's, and is not modelled here: the net stays open. A part with a `bridge` and no `dc` element that the pass models (an LED, an inductor, a MOSFET) with a `.current` that has a value, stated or worked out: a current source over it between the pins of its `bridge`, which is question 5's stated current on a boundary. A pin that the checker says draws nothing (ADR 0027), except on a connector, and a pin with no connection, add nothing. A capacitor is open (question 7).

**Open nets.** Every part pin on a net that no source holds must be one of the above. Another (a pin that drives, a pin of a part with no `bridge` current, a connector, a port of the board that is not a rail with a value, a part whose element has no value yet) may set the net or draw from it, so the island it is in is not solved: nothing is guessed, and what depends on it stays as it was. No note names such a boundary in this stage (see "Left for the next stages").

**Corners and the limit.** Both ends of each range, a diode blocking and conducting at each end of its forward voltage, over the island's values: as in section 3, ported from the prototype. A diode's blocking state is refused when its anode is above its drop (a node a load draws from cannot be held by a blocking diode), and a conducting one when its current is negative; no combination is counted that is not the circuit's. The limit is 65536 combinations and a work of 2^28 (the combinations times the cube of the island's size), so that no island takes more than a tenth of a second optimised: an island of 15 free nodes with 16 resistors of 1 % is the largest. Over it, a note names the island's parts and says to cut it at a node a rail holds, and nothing is written for it. A matrix that cannot be solved (a loop of ideal sources) leaves the island alone, without a note. A conductance of a femtosiemens from each node to the ground is added only to a combination whose matrix is singular without it (a node behind a blocking diode); a network solved without it is not touched.

**What it writes.** For a modelled part, `.current` (taken from the first terminal of its `bridge` to the second) and `.drop` (the first relative to the second), each by the rule of section 4 and question 4: an unset field takes the range; a field a statement narrowed is a budget, and when the circuit needs more the error says `` `r.current` is stated A, but the DC analysis of the circuit around it finds B: at most X at the high end with u at 16V `` and leaves the field as stated; a field the solver worked out is narrowed to what both say, and when the two share nothing it is left alone (the relations that read it say so). The ranges are widened outward by a billionth and by a picoampere or a nanovolt, so that a quantity that is zero in the circuit (a capacitor in a chain) does not miss another range by a rounding; a diode's current is never widened below zero. The fields are marked as worked out, so an equation on one narrows it and a balance can work it again. The states of an error are those that matter: each value that can be put at its other end with the quantity unchanged is not named (a load's current is the load's, whatever the supply is), found by solving the combination again with each value changed. A rating that the pass makes fail is the relation's own failure, with `(the DC analysis puts `f.current` at R, its high end with S; its low end with T)` after it.

**What changes in existing designs.** A relation that waited for a current is judged, so a design that built with a note can now fail with an error: a resistor across a rail, a pick that took the first row of a catalog (ADR 0065) and carries more than its rating, a divider whose limit on its draw the arbitrary pick exceeds. These are errors by question 6, found where they were only notes, and one more reason for phase 3 (pick the series parts first, then the rest). The `not evaluated` notes of the checks the pass judges, the `incomplete balance` note of a current the pass sets, and the open circles that the numbers close, go. The tracks of a net whose carrier's current is now known are sized (ADR 0036). The guide's examples of unconstrained picks now put a 3.3 V supply across their invented resistors, because 5 V across 330 ohm is over the rating of the first row.

**Cost.** Measured on a release build, one run each: `examples/led.nsm` 17 us of 0.9 ms; the other examples (no island with a source and a part to write) 8 to 9 us of 0.9 to 1.2 ms; an invented board of 24 stages (a divider of two resistors of 1 %, an LED of 0.1 mA to 0.3 mA on its middle, all off one supply of 9 V to 16 V: 24 islands, 384 combinations, 96 fields written) 0.36 ms of 14 ms, 2.5 % of the solve; an invented chain of 16 resistors of 1 % off one supply, the largest island the limit allows (65536 combinations), 61 ms, and one of 15 resistors (32768) 28 ms. A board of the size of the real 12 V automotive one has about forty islands of the sizes of section 8; the sum was not measured, because that board is not a file of this repository.

**Left for the next stages.** Regulators and constant-power loads with their fixed point (stage 2b); the drive states and the leakage of logic pins, which read the test currents of amendment 1 (stage 3); a MOSFET as a switch; a source rail's own stated `.current` as a budget that the pass checks (a source that cannot give what its load asks, question 6) and its sum, which needs the loads of the whole net to be modelled; the node voltage above an absolute maximum; naming each boundary with its pin and what is missing (it adds a note to every board that has a resistor beside an unmodelled pin, and was left out of this stage to keep the notes that exist unchanged); `.bias` of a diode or a capacitor from the node voltages; the cache of section 6 (an edit that changes one resistor solves the island it is in, and the cost above does not need it yet); and the removal of the seed (phase 3).

## Amendment 3 (provisional): regulators in the DC analysis (phase 2, stage 2b, slice 1)

Written with the code of `crates/netsmith-solve/src/dc/` (`extract.rs`, `net.rs`, `mod.rs`) and `src/solve/dcpass.rs`. It builds the regulator of section 2 and the fixed point, on the owner's decisions of section 10 (question 4: narrow what is unstated and check what is stated; question 6: a source that cannot give what its load asks and an input below a regulator's minimum are errors). The syntax below is the engineer's, within amendment 1's grammar, and the owner confirms or changes it. Logic pins and MOSFETs are the next slice.

**The grammar.** Two more elements for `dc`, each a word as `nmos` and `pmos` are two words, with the same rule (pins or rails first, values last):

```text
dc linear_regulator    <input rail> <output rail>
dc switching_regulator <input rail> <output rail> <efficiency>
```

The first two operands are *rails* of the part (interfaces with a `pos`, a `neg` and a `voltage`, directly or inside an interface of the part: `.vin`, `.buck.sw`), where every other element takes pins: the checker needs the voltage and the current of each side, and a rail names the pin pair, the voltage and the current at once. The efficiency is a field of the part with no unit (a `Number`, a range such as `0.85..0.95`). A regulator has no `bridge` and the check of the `bridge` against the element does not apply. `LinearRegulator` states `dc linear_regulator .vin .vout`. A buck converter's IC is a part of the design's own and states `dc switching_regulator .vin .buck.sw .buck.efficiency`; the `Buck` interface cannot (a `dc` statement is in a part or a generic), so the IC states it, next to the relation it already writes for the input current, which stays.

**The model.** The output rail is an ideal source: its `pos` net is held over the range of the rail's `.voltage` (the range the solver has after the picks, the tolerance the design asked for) and its `neg` net at zero. The input draws, in the units of section 2: a linear regulator the current its output supplies; a switching one `Pout / (eff * Vin)`, with `Pout` the output's power, `Vout * Iout` at both ends of both ranges, and `eff` at both ends. A regulator whose two grounds are different nets, whose output net is its input net, whose output voltage is not above zero, whose efficiency is not above zero or whose output net another regulator holds too, is not modelled (nothing is guessed). The quiescent current of section 2 is left out: no std regulator states one, `LinearRegulator` ignores it in its own relation, and a `dc` operand for it would be one more syntax; adding an optional operand later changes nothing else.

A board `Rail` on a net that a regulator holds is not a second source: the regulator holds the net, and the rail's `.current` is what the regulator supplies (a load) and its `.voltage` a requirement the relations check.

**The order of the islands.** A regulator's output cuts the network, so the loads behind it are an island, or several, that have the output net as a held node. The pass solves them first and takes, for each held node, the current it gives into the island's elements (`Which::HeldDraw`), summed over every island on the net: that range is the regulator's `Iout`, and `Pout` is `Vout * Iout` (`lo * lo` to `hi * hi`: both are non-negative and the power is monotone in them). The island in front of the regulator, with the regulator's input as an element, is solved next, and so is a chain of regulators, the one nearest the loads first. The cost, as section 3 says, is the tie between a load's current and the output voltage, which each side loses (a result wider, never narrower); here the regulator's `Pout` takes the output voltage and the load current apart, and a load that follows the output voltage (a resistor) is taken over the whole range of the output. When a pin on the output net is not modelled, the load is the regulator's own `.vout.current` if it has a value that is never positive (stated, or worked out by the balances); when it has none the regulator is not modelled and the island in front of it is left alone, as before. A cycle in the order leaves the islands in it alone.

**The fixed point.** As section 2 and the prototype: for each corner, the sinks start at zero, the network is solved, each switching regulator's input current is set to `Pout / (eff * Vin)` at the voltage the solve gave, and the rounds go on until no current moves by more than `1e-10` of the largest (half steps after 60 rounds, 400 at most). From zero the iterates rise monotonically to the smallest fixed point, and the input voltage falls to nothing when there is none. An input under 1 mV is the *collapse*: it is reported when the states of the combination are the circuit's (the diodes' states are what the solution says) and is a refused state otherwise (a diode blocking in front of a regulator leaves it no input). The combinations that collapse are not counted in the ranges; if none holds, nothing is written. A matrix with a pivot below `1e-13` of its largest entry is singular (a cluster of nodes nothing holds, behind a blocking diode): the first solve took the rounding of such a matrix for a voltage of 1e13 V and let a blocking diode with a positive node voltage pass as consistent, so the threshold is relative.

**What it writes and says.** The regulator's `.vin.current` and `.vin.voltage` by the rules of amendment 2 (an unset field takes the range, a field a statement narrowed is a budget that is checked, a worked-out field is narrowed to what both say), and, in the island in front of it, the `.current` and `.drop` of every resistor and diode as before: the fuse, the diode, the shunt and the bead of a power tree take the range of the current that the regulators' inputs make. Two findings, both errors (question 6):

- *The source cannot give what the load asks*: `the source cannot give what ``reg`` asks (5W at its output, efficiency 90 %): its input falls to 0V and no operating point exists, with supply at 10V`. The states are those that vary in the first combination that collapsed; the output side's states are not named (the load behind the regulator is its range, not a state of this island).
- *An input below the regulator's minimum*: the relation the part states (`.vin.voltage >= .vout.voltage + .dropout` of `LinearRegulator`, `.vin.voltage >= .buck.min_input_voltage` of a stage) fails with the number the analysis wrote for `.vin.voltage`, and carries the states after it as any rating does. No second statement carries the minimum: the part states it once, as a relation. The consequence is that the finding exists when `.vin.voltage` has no stated value; a seed narrower than the circuit (issue #153's `&= 7V..16V`) is a stated budget, the check of amendment 2 compares the circuit with it (an error when the circuit goes outside it), and the relation then reads the seed, so a seed that is above the minimum hides a circuit that is not. Removing the seed is phase 3.

**The hand-written equalities and the seed (issue #153).** Nothing is removed in this slice. On an invented power tree (a battery of 9 V to 16 V, a fuse, a diode, a shunt, a bead, a 5 V stage with a 3.3 V linear regulator on its output, a 3.8 V stage and a load on the chain: `crates/netsmith-solve/tests/dc_regulators.rs`) the analysis gives the current of the chain, 105.3 mA to 507.3 mA, and the input of the stages, 8.124 V to 15.491 V, equal to a calculation of every corner made apart in the test to a part in a million. The same board written with `.buck5.vin.voltage == .supply.voltage - .fuse.drop - .diode.drop - .shunt.drop - .bead.drop` and the seed `.buck5.vin.voltage &= 7V..16V` builds with no message and the same numbers: the equation is evaluated on the drops the analysis wrote and the seed holds the solved range (the seed's lower end is 1.1 V below the least the chain can give). Removing both needs, beyond this slice: (1) the picks that read a value before the analysis (a capacitor's `bias`, read when the capacitor is picked, which is why the seed exists) to wait for it: pick the series parts, run the pass, then pick the parts that read its results, or run it once before the picks over the range every candidate could give; (2) `~>` over rails to carry the voltage (issue #153's option (a)), now an equation the pass makes redundant; (3) the guide to stop showing the equations.

**Left for the next stages.** The drive states and the leakage of logic pins; a MOSFET as a switch; a regulator's quiescent current (an optional operand or a member); a source rail's own stated `.current` as a budget; a node voltage above an absolute maximum; naming each boundary; `.bias` from node voltages; the cache; the seed's removal; the efficiency as a curve and an input below the minimum as a shutdown; two regulators in parallel on one net.

## Amendment 4 (provisional): logic pins that drive and MOSFETs as switches (phase 2, stage 2b, slice 2)

Written with the code of `crates/netsmith-solve/src/dc/` (`extract.rs`, `net.rs`) and `src/solve/dcpass.rs`. It builds the two rows of section 2 that were left: the logic pin and the MOSFET, on the owner's decisions of section 10 (question 2: the datasheet's own form, a level with its test current; question 3: no feature for which states can happen together, the limit is documented; question 5: a stated `.current` on a boundary pin). No syntax is added: `dc nmos`, `dc pmos` (amendment 1) and `at` (amendment 1) are what this slice reads. Everything under "Choices for the owner" is the engineer's conservative option within those decisions, and the owner confirms or changes it.

**The logic pin.** A pin is a driver of the analysis when all of these hold, and a boundary otherwise (the net it is on stays open, as before):

- its kind is `output`, `tri_state` or `bidirectional`, and the board has not stated its interface unused (ADR 0083);
- it is the `.line` of a `Logic`, or a pin of a bus that states `.levels`, and that interface states no `.swing` (a swing says the line goes to a supply that has no pin, so the reference rail is not what the pin drives to);
- the nets of its reference rail's `pos` and `neg` are held by a source (a board's supply or a regulator's output), the `neg` as the ground the `pos` is measured from;
- it states `voh` and `vol`, each with a value and each with the current it is measured at (`.voh &= .vdd.voltage - 0.4V at 2mA`);
- it is the only driver on its net.

The model is section 2's row. High: the line joined to the net of the supply through `roh`; low: to the net of the ground through `rol`; high impedance (`tri_state` and `bidirectional` only): nothing. An `output` is high or low, never high impedance. `roh` runs from 1 mohm to `(VDD - VOH) / IOH`, where the drop `VDD - VOH` is the constant when the level is written `.vdd.voltage - c` on a rail whose `pos` is the pin's supply net (exact: 0.4 V at 2 mA is 200 ohm), and the greatest supply less the least `voh` otherwise (never narrower, and wider where the supply has a range); `rol` runs from 1 mohm to `vol / IOL` at the greatest `vol`. Both ends are corners. The datasheet states the level at one load, so the resistance is the one at that load; a lighter load drops less, and the range down to 1 mohm covers it.

A pin's states are tried in every combination with every other element's, as question 3 decided: a board cannot say that two pins are never high together. The pass writes nothing for a pin (it has no field for the current it drives); what it draws from a regulator's output counts in the regulator's load (the held node's draw), and what its load carries (a pull-down's `.current` and `.drop`) is written as for any resistor. A net that has two pins that could drive it is not modelled at all (see the choices).

**The MOSFET.** A part with `dc nmos` or `dc pmos` and finite ranges for its threshold and its channel resistance is a switch of the analysis, when what sets its gate is known: the gate's net is held by a source, or has a resistor, a diode or a driving pin on it, and nothing on it is a pin that nothing models. It is off (no channel) or on (`.on_resistance` between drain and source, both ends of the range). The gate draws nothing. The drive is the gate above the source for an N-channel part and the source above the gate for a P-channel part (the values of the part are magnitudes). A state is refused as the circuit's when on with a drive below the least threshold or off with a drive above the greatest; inside the range both are kept. A gate that no conducting path joins to a held node in a combination (a tri-state pin that let go and no resistor on the net) floats: it has no voltage, and both states are kept. The switch writes `.current` (drain to source, or source to drain for a P-channel part, as its `bridge` runs) and `.drop` where the part has one, by the rules of amendment 2. A MOSFET whose gate is left to something unmodelled is a boundary as in the earlier stages: a current source over its `.current` when that has a value, so the island of its drain and source is solved as before.

**What changes in existing designs.** A board with a pin that states `voh` and `vol` at test currents (nothing in the standard library and no example did before this amendment, except `Sn74lvc1g04`, which now states its test current of 100 uA from its datasheet's table 5.5) now has the passives on the net of that pin solved: the pull-down on a modem's line gets its `.current` and `.drop`, and its relations are judged. A board with a MOSFET whose gate is held or driven gets the channel's current, and the load behind it is a state of the island (zero when the gate is low). One example of the guide changed, and the analysis was right to find it: the high-side `PMosfet` example joined its drain to the ground, so a switch that is on was a short across a 5 V supply through 70 mohm (75 A) with `.current &= 1A` stated. It now has a load on the drain and states `0A..1A`. No other example of the repository and no existing test changed.

**Cost.** An output pin is four states (high and low at each end of the resistance), a tri-state pin five, a MOSFET three. An island of a pin, a gate pull-down, a MOSFET and its load, off a supply with a range, is 48 to 60 combinations (a test of the solver).

**Choices for the owner.**

1. *Open drain and the pin that states one level.* A pin that states `vol` at a current and no `voh` is an open-drain pin when ADR 0075's inference applies, and a push-pull pin that left its high out when it does not. A marker for it is the open question of ADR 0075. Taken: such a pin is a boundary (its net stays open), which guesses nothing. Alternative: model it as low or high impedance with ADR 0075's inference; it closes the I2C buses of a real board's notes and wrongly calls a push-pull pin open-drain when a part omitted its high.
2. *Leakage.* The datasheet's `Ii` at a test voltage has no member on `Logic` or `Levels` (amendment 1, left open 1), so the high-impedance state is an open circuit: no leakage. A pull-down against a pin's leakage (1 Mohm and 1 uA is a volt) is therefore not seen. Alternative: members `ii` (leakage at a test voltage), or `at` on a third level; both change `Logic` and `Levels`.
3. *Several drivers on a net.* The states of two pins that can drive one net include both driving it apart, which is a fault and not a design; and which states can coexist (a bus has one driver at a time) is the thing question 3 declined to state. Taken: no pin of such a net is modelled. Alternative: try all combinations (a contention current through two output resistances ends every range of the net); or a statement that says one driver at a time on a tri-state net, which is the feature question 3 left out.
4. *A line with a `.swing`.* Left as a boundary: the supply it drives to has no pin. Alternative: read the high from the swing's top as a node of its own, with `roh` to it; that makes the swing a source, which section 4 refuses for a range that a circuit sets.
5. *The high from the datasheet's drop.* `roh` is taken at the test point (the drop that the level states over its current). Alternative: a table of the level over several currents (a datasheet gives `VOH` at 2 mA and at 8 mA), which `at` refuses today (a level holds one load).
6. *A floating gate.* A gate that no conducting path joins to a held node keeps both states of the switch. Alternative: take its voltage to be the ground's, as the numeric solve does when it adds its leakage conductance: it calls the switch off and hides a case a design must handle.
7. *`Sn74lvc1g04` states `at 100uA`* on its `voh` and `vol`, the figures it already states, because its datasheet gives them at that load (table 5.5). Nothing changes for a board that uses it as the guide does, since nothing on its output net is a load the analysis writes to.

**Left for the next stages.** A pin's leakage and the open-drain marker (choices 1 and 2); a pin on a line with a swing; a MOSFET's body diode and the reversed battery (section 5); a source rail's own stated `.current` as a budget; a node voltage above an absolute maximum; naming each boundary; the cache; the seed's removal and `~>` carrying voltage (phase 3, issue #153); levels and tracks from the netlist (phase 4).

## Amendment 5 (provisional): a chain carries the voltage, a part can give a supply, and a circle a pick waits on starts without a window (phase 3, first slice, issue #153)

Written with the code of `crates/netsmith-solve/src/dc/extract.rs` and `src/solve/dcpass.rs`, and `crates/netsmith-check/src/dc.rs`. It is the first slice of phase 3: the voltage carried along a `~>` chain (issue #153, point 1) and the start of a circle that a pick waits on (point 2) for a board whose network the analysis can solve before the picks. The owner's decisions of section 10 stand and the owner chose option (b) of the issue ("start from the interval a source states and evaluate the chain with the drops at their worst") with the engine of this ADR. Everything under "Choices for the owner" is the engineer's conservative option, and the owner confirms or changes it. What is left, and why the seed of the real board is still there, is under "What is not done".

**A rail carries the voltage of its node.** After an island is solved, each rail whose `pos` net is a free node of it and whose `neg` net is the island's ground is given the range of the node's voltage, by the rule of amendment 2: an unset `.voltage` takes the range, one the solver worked out is narrowed to what both say, and one that a statement narrowed (or a rail joined to it by `~`) is the design's and is left as it is. One class of rails joined by `~` is written once, and a regulator's input is the earlier write (its `.vin.voltage` is the drop across the input element). So the rail at the end of `vin.pos ~> .fuse ~> .diode ~> .vout.pos` has the supply's voltage less the two drops at the currents they carry, and the board no longer writes `.vout.voltage == .vin.voltage - .fuse.drop - .reverse_polarity_diode.drop`: that is issue #153's option (a), with the analysis as its engine, as section 4 said. A relation that reads the voltage is judged with it, and a failure says what the analysis put there and the states of each end (the guide's example).

A node is written only when a positive source reaches it through resistors, diodes, switches or driving pins (not through the ground, and not through a current source). A node that nothing supplies has only the loads on it to set its voltage, which the solution puts below the ground (a backup input tied to the ground through a resistor, loaded with 750 nA: -7.5 mV), and a relation such as `.vbackup.voltage >= 1.3V` would fail on a number that is not a voltage. The carried voltage is the peak's neighbour and not its equal: `.peak` is not a DC quantity and is still stated (a TVS's clamping voltage on the rail it clamps; ADR 0086 gives a fuse the rail's peak from the chain's start).

**A part can give a supply: `dc source <rail>`.** The real board's supply is not a `Rail` of the board but the supply rail of a connector part (`Obd2M.vbatt`), which the board gives a range (`.obd2.vbatt.voltage &= 9V..16V`). Amendment 2 took only a board's own rails for sources, so the whole power tree of that board had no source and was never solved (the analysis had one island there, a pull-down on the real-time clock). `dc source` is the part's statement that a rail is a supply it gives, in the grammar of amendment 3 (a rail operand first): the rail's `pos` net is held over the range of its `.voltage` that the design states (as for a board's rail: a range the solver worked out is not stated) and its `neg` net at zero. A part that does not state it is as before: its rail is what it draws. `Obd2M` and `UsbCPower` state it (`.vbatt`, `.vbus`). The checker has `DcKind::Source`; `dc source` takes one rail of the part.

**A regulator's other input pins.** The pins of a modelled regulator that are on the nets of its input (an IC with two `VIN` pins, an enable tied to the input) are its input's, and draw nothing more: without this a buck IC with `.vin2 ~ .vin.pos` left its input net open and the island unsolved.

**The start of a circle that a pick waits on.** A pick that reads a voltage nothing has given (a capacitor's bias across the input of a regulator behind a fuse; a divider's draw, `vin.voltage / (top + bottom)`, with the input behind a diode) cannot be judged, and the board states a window by hand. When a pick waits on such a voltage (a derated column's bias with no value, or a relation that reads a column of a part to be picked and the voltage of a rail with no value), the solver solves the network once before the picks. Each part still to be picked is taken at the hull of the values its candidates can have for its `dc` value (the rows that fit what the design says of the part: a fuse's resistance `0.35 ohm..2.7 ohm` over the fuses that fit), and the voltage of each rail and each regulator's input that results is written as the range the picks see. It is an enclosure: whatever the picks choose, the voltage ends inside it, and the pass after the picks narrows it to the circuit that was picked (the field is worked out, not stated). It writes voltages only: a current that a pick would then judge a rating against is the later pass's. A board with neither waiting pick is solved as before, so its picks are the same; a board with one now has the pick judged on the voltage, which can refuse a candidate that the old pick took and the pass after the picks refused (below). A stated window is used as stated and starts nothing.

On an invented board (`crates/netsmith-solve/tests/dc_chain.rs`, the guide's "A circle that a pick waits on") of a 4 V to 5.5 V supply, a link of 0.2 ohm, a 3.3 V converter at 90 % with a load of 1 A and a capacitor across its input picked for 4 uF at its bias, the build fails with `cin.bias is not known` and passes with the start, with the input at 3.807 V to 5.363 V (the roots of `0.9 I (v - 0.2 I) = 3.3` at both ends of the supply, to a part in a million).

**What changes in existing designs.** `Obd2M` and `UsbCPower` state a source, so a board that gives their rail a stated voltage now has the networks behind it solved where it had no source: that can judge a relation that was a note and write `.current` and `.drop` on its fuse, diode and shunt. No example of the repository and no example of the guide changed in what it builds; the one guide message that changed is the list of elements in the error of an unknown `dc` word. One test changed, and the analysis is right to change it: `open_circle::nothing_else_on_the_net_is_a_circle_the_dc_analysis_closes_and_judges_the_pick_by` (amendment 2) had a divider behind a series resistor with nothing else on the net, picked as 330 ohm and 330 ohm because its limit on the draw read a voltage nobody had given, and refused after the pick (`divider.top.current must always be at most divider.max_draw`, `14 V to 22 V over 660 ohm`). The start gives the voltage before the pick, so the divider is picked against its 40 uA as it is with a window the board writes (the test beside it), and the build passes: it is the test now called `..._the_dc_analysis_starts_and_the_limit_is_part_of_the_pick`. The real 12 V automotive board (read only) builds with its own parts as before, with 0 errors, 0 warnings and 0 notes under `--strict`, and its own 102 checks give 102 as expected: its supply is now a source, and its power tree stays open (see below), so nothing there is written. One finding was a bug of this slice and is fixed: a node nothing supplies was given a voltage below the ground and failed a part's own `>= 1.3V` (the real-time clock's backup input in the board's own test); the rule above is what the fix is.

**What is not done, and what the real board needs.** The seed of issue #153 (`.reg.vin.voltage &= 7V..16V`, and the divider's `.vin.voltage &= 8V..16V`) is not removed on the real board, and the issue is not closed. On a scratch copy of the real board, with `dc switching_regulator .vin .buck.sw .buck.efficiency` added to its two converter ICs (parts of the board's own that state no `dc` element, which this slice does not touch), the power tree is solved after the picks (one island of the fuse, the diode, the shunt, the bead, the regulator, the battery divider and the CAN supply, 2048 combinations, 13 rails on its nodes) and the strict build is clean with both seeds in place. With the divider's seed removed the open circle is closed (the analysis carries the voltage to the divider), and the divider's resistors, picked before the circuit was known, fail their 10 uA draw with `526.5uA..1.002mA`: the start did not run for them, because the island is open before the picks (the regulator's load is the inductor's current, which a pick sets). With the converter's seed removed the pick of its input capacitor still fails (`bias is not known`), for the same reason. Three things are needed, none of them built here: (1) the loads behind a regulator known before the picks, or an enclosure for them (the part's own `max_output_current` as a range, which is sound and wide); (2) the converter ICs of the project stating their element, and the `Inductor` stating `dc resistor .a .b .dc_resistance` so that a buck stage's output is the node past the inductor (without it the output net is a current source's: tried, and the node came out at +/-115 kV); (3) an owner decision about the output of a buck: the regulator's output is held at the node its part names (the switch node), so the rail past the inductor is a few millivolts below a window the board states, and that window is therefore not checked (below).

**Cost.** One more extraction of the network before the picks, for a board with a pick that waits on a voltage; nothing for any other. The rail writes add one island's node voltages to the results already computed.

**Choices for the owner.**

1. *`dc source <rail>` is a new word.* Taken: the part states that a rail is a supply, as it states that a rail is a regulator's input. Alternatives: infer a source from a stated `.voltage` on a rail of a connector (a guess: a part's `.vdd.voltage &= 1.7V..3.6V` is a window and not a supply); a marker on the board (`.obd2.vbatt &= source`), which makes every board say what the connector is; or a rail kind in the language (`source: Rail`), which changes `Rail` for every part.
2. *Which standard parts state it.* Taken: the two connectors that give a supply, `Obd2M` and `UsbCPower`. A battery or a barrel jack would, when the library has one.
3. *A voltage that a statement gave a rail is not checked against the circuit.* The question 4 of section 10 says check what is stated, and the pass does for a part's `.current` and `.drop` and for a regulator's input. For a rail past a regulator's output it gave a finding that is the model's and not the circuit's: the switch node is held at the stated output, so the rail past the inductor is 9 mV below the window `3.686V..3.914V` (a real converter regulates the node past the inductor), and the analysis said `reg3v8.vout.voltage` was stated 3.686 V to 3.914 V but 3.677 V at the least. Taken: the stated rail is left alone. Alternative: check it, and give the regulator a way to say which node it holds (the output rail of the stage and not the IC's switch node), which needs the `dc` statement to name a rail outside the part.
4. *The carried voltage and not the peak.* `~>` over rails carries the voltage and the drop; the peak is a transient and stays stated. Alternative: the chain's output rail takes the start's `.peak` as an upper bound (`.vout.peak <= .vin.peak`), the way ADR 0086 gives a part the peak, which would remove `.reg5v3.vin.peak == .monitor.vout.peak` from a board and keep `.vout.peak == .clamp.clamping_voltage`. It is a change of the checker for every chain and a new meaning of `<=` on a rail, so it is not built.
5. *The start runs only when a pick waits on a voltage.* Taken: a board with no such pick is solved as before; a board with one (a divider or a derated capacitor behind a series part, with no window written) is picked on the voltage, which is what the pick should have read, and one test of the repository changed for it (above). Alternatives: always run it (the picks of every board then see the hull, and more candidates are refused earlier, which changes picks), or pick the series parts first and run the pass between (the first option of section 4; the picks of the series parts are then made without their currents, which the later pass judges).
6. *The hull of the candidates.* A part still to be picked takes the hull of its `dc` value over the candidates that fit the design (`fits`, the cheap filter), and not over the candidates that survive every relation, which is the search's own work. The hull is wider than the final answer, and the start only ever holds a range that every choice ends inside.
7. *A regulator's other pins on its input nets are its input's.* Taken: no note for each, as the later "naming each boundary" would add (a note on every board with such a pin). Alternative: a regulator part states its extra input pins (`dc switching_regulator .vin .buck.sw .buck.efficiency` plus a list), which makes every IC with a second pin say so.
8. *The hand-written equalities of the chain.* `.vout.voltage == .vin.voltage - .fuse.drop - ...` stays valid (the analysis writes the field before the equation reads it and the two intersect) and the guide shows the chain without it. They are the board's to delete; std has none.

**Left for the next stages.** The removal of the seed on the real board (what is not done, above); an enclosure for the loads behind a regulator before the picks; `Inductor` as a DC resistor and the output of a buck stage; the peak along a chain (choice 4); the voltage above an absolute maximum from node voltages (phase 4); levels and tracks from the netlist (phase 4); a source rail's own `.current` as a budget; naming each boundary; the cache.

## Amendment 6 (provisional): the winding of an inductor, an enclosure of the load behind a regulator, and what the real board still needs (phase 3, second slice, issue #153)

Written with the code of `crates/netsmith-solve/src/dc/mod.rs`, `src/solve/dcpass.rs`, `std/parts/passive.nsm` and `std/parts/regulator.nsm`. It builds the three cheap items that amendment 5 listed under "What is not done", and measures what is left on the real 12 V automotive board (read only; a scratch copy was changed, and its diff is in the pull request). The owner's decisions of section 10 stand. Everything under "Choices for the owner" is the engineer's conservative option, and the owner confirms or changes it. The seed of issue #153 is still not removable on the real board, and the issue stays open: the reasons are measured below.

**An inductor is its winding at DC.** `Inductor` states `dc resistor .a .b .dc_resistance`, as `FerriteBead` does. The output net of a buck stage is then the switch node less what the load makes the winding drop, and the output side is an island of its own with the load (the divider, the rails, the probes on the net) and the switch node held by the regulator. Without it the inductor was a boundary current source and the node past it came out at +-115 kV (amendment 5). The pass writes the inductor's `.current` and `.drop` as for any resistor (a stated current is a budget, a worked-out one is narrowed), so the check `.inductor.current <= .saturation_current` is judged with the circuit's current as well as the stage's own relation. A part still to be picked takes the hull of its `.dc_resistance` over the candidates that fit, and a generic row (ADR 0047), whose value the design supplies, takes what the design says of it: an upper bound a relation gives (`.dc_resistance <= .max_inductor_dcr`) makes the row `0..bound`; no bound makes the part a boundary as before, because a value nothing bounds is no enclosure.

**An enclosure of the load behind a regulator.** A regulator whose output net has a pin the pass does not model (an ADC input, a cell's balance pin) has no sum of the loads behind it, and the pass fell back to the regulator's own `.current` when that field had a range that is never positive. Before the picks that field has no range (a one-sided relation gives an unset field no domain). The start now reads the relation itself: a relation `-<output current> <= <limit>` whose limit has a value is a limit on what the output supplies, and the load is the range from nothing to that limit. `LinearRegulator` states `-.vout.current <= .max_current` on its output rail, and `Buck` now states `-.sw.current <= .max_output_current` on the switch node (the stage already stated it on its own output rail, which is a different field), the datasheet's one figure in both. The enclosure is used only by the start before the picks; the pass after the picks takes the load from the islands behind the regulator, or from the field, as before. The start also works through the design's own relations once before the hulls (a bound on a winding's resistance, a limit on a current), as the propagation that follows it would, and discards what that finds: the propagation after it finds the same conflicts and says them.

On an invented buck stage (`crates/netsmith-solve/tests/dc_buck.rs`: a 9 V to 16 V supply, a bead, an IC that states `dc switching_regulator`, an inductor and a divider that are picks, an input capacitor picked at its bias) the build passes with no window on the input; the input is 8.988 V to 16 V, the node past the inductor is the output less the winding's drop at 0 A to 1 A, and the input current is `Pout / (eff * Vin)` at the worst corner, each to a part in a million. With no current stated on the output rail the load is enclosed by the IC's 3 A: the input is 8.927 V to 16 V, the root of `v = 9 - 0.1 * 5.562 / (0.85 v)`.

**The standard converter ICs.** `LinearRegulator` and the standard LT8641 (`Lt8641Buck`, merged while this was open) already state their element, `dc switching_regulator .vin .buck.sw .buck.efficiency` for the buck, next to the relation each writes for its input current, so nothing in std is left to add: `Buck` is an interface, and any other buck IC is a part of the design's own. The real board's two ICs are such parts and state the same line themselves (`dc` is in a part or a generic, ADR 0076 amendment 1).

**What changes in existing designs.** The test `dc_analysis::a_boundary_without_a_current_leaves_the_island_alone` used an inductor as its boundary and now uses an LED, since the inductor is no boundary. A board with a buck stage now has its output side solved, so a relation on the inductor's current or drop, and the stated `.current` of a part on the output net, are judged with the circuit's numbers (a budget the circuit exceeds is an error, by question 6). No example of the repository changed in what it builds; the guide's examples are in step.

**The real board, measured.** The scratch copy builds under `--strict` with 0 errors, 0 warnings and 0 notes, with the same parts picked (the `.kicad_sch` values and footprints are identical), and its 102 checks give 102 as expected, after these changes to it: the two ICs state `dc switching_regulator`; the board's `.reg5v3.vin.voltage == .monitor.vout.voltage - .input_filter.drop` and `.input_filter.current == .reg5v3.vin.current` are deleted, since the chain carries both. The equalities of the sheets (`.vout.voltage == .vin.voltage - .fuse.drop - ...` in the input protection, `.vout.voltage == .vin.voltage + .shunt.drop` in the input monitor, `.vout.voltage == .vin.voltage - .r.drop` in the supply clamp) can be deleted too, and the board builds clean with the same parts, but each of them is what lets the pick of a series part refuse a candidate on its drop: without them one negative test of the module (a battery that sags to 6 V) still fails to build, with a later message (`the transceiver's VSUP must always be at least 4.5 V, but 2.449 V to 15.2 V is not always above 4.5 V`, from the pass after the picks, in place of `no combination of candidates satisfies the clamp and its resistor together`), and the test expects the first, so they stay. (Deleting only one of two such equations and keeping its neighbour gives a false error, `must stay within 8.077V..15.2V, but ... works out to 8.077V..15.2V`, by the widening of the analysis's ranges: issue #253.) The backup charger's equality (`.vout.voltage == .vin.voltage - .diode.drop`) cannot be deleted: its node is on the 3.3 V net, where the LDO is a part that states a quiescent current and so no `dc` element, and the I2C lines and processor pins are unmodelled, so the island is open and the relation on the backup voltage is a note.

The two seeds stay, for these measured reasons, none of them a bug of this slice:

1. *The input of the 5 V stage.* With its seed removed the start does not run for the power tree: the island has the clamp's Zener, a boundary whose current is a window that reads a column of the part to be picked (`.clamp.current &= 0A .. .clamp.leakage_current`), so its net is open. Taking every column of every such part at its hull, and the relations once with them, closes the island, and the answer is the enclosure of every choice of every series part: a fuse from 15 mohm to 2.7 ohm, a shunt, a bead, the supply of the CAN transceiver behind a resistor from 25 mohm to 10 Mohm, the converter's load from 0 A to its 3.5 A. The input of the stage comes out 146 mV to 15.45 V (it collapses at the corner of the 2.7 ohm fuse), which fails `.vin.voltage >= 3V` of the IC, the stage's `>= .vout.voltage`, and every capacitor's bias; the supply of the transceiver comes out -1.364 kV to 15.45 V and fails its own `>= 4.5V`. A check cannot be judged on an enclosure over parts that are not picked and are not constrained by it, which is why the start writes only what the first slice's rule says (a rail that a pick waits on) and leaves the rest to the pass after the picks. That attempt is not built.
2. *The divider's draw.* The same island is open before the picks, so the divider's limit on its draw is judged on the hull of the divider's own catalog, as before. With the Zener's current closed the divider would be picked on the input's top end (15.45 V), which is right, but the island cannot be closed without (1).
3. *A regulator's quiescent current.* The board's LDO states a quiescent current of up to 140 uA in its own relation, and no `dc` element carries it (amendment 3: an optional operand later); stating `dc linear_regulator` for it would leave 140 uA out of the sum, so it is not stated.

The way out is the first alternative of section 4 and of choice 5 of amendment 5: pick the series parts, run the pass with them, then pick the parts that read its results, with each series pick judged against the currents of the pass over the candidates that are left, and not against the hull of the whole catalog. It is an order of the picks, not a hull, and it is phase 4's neighbour (issue #196); it is not built.

**Choices for the owner.**

1. *Which node a buck holds.* Taken: the regulator holds the node its part names (the IC's switch pin), and the winding is a resistor from it to the output net, so a rail past the inductor is the held node less the winding's drop, and a window the board states on that rail (`.vout.voltage &= 3.8V +/- 3%`) is the rail's own and is not checked against the circuit (amendment 5, choice 3). Alternatives: (a) the output of the stage is held, so the rail the board names is the node the window means, and the switch node is that plus the winding's drop: the `dc` statement of the IC has to name a rail outside the part, which `dc` cannot do (the rules of amendment 1), or `BuckStage` would state the element, and a module has no `dc`; (b) hold the switch node and check the stated window against the node past the winding, with the drop as the regulator's own tolerance: the real board's window is 3.686 V to 3.914 V and the node is 9 mV lower at the least, so the check fails a design that is right, and the check needs the window to say whether it is for the node or for the pin; (c) the pass writes a rail that a statement gave, narrowed to what both say: the circuit's range then sits inside the design's window and the window is never the circuit's, which hides a stage that cannot hold its window.
2. *`Buck` states `-.sw.current <= .max_output_current`.* It is true of every buck IC, is the limit the start encloses the load by, and adds one check on the switch node's current to every IC (a board that stated a load above the part's limit already failed on the stage's own output rail). Alternatives: an optional operand of `dc switching_regulator` for the limit (new syntax; every IC repeats the figure it already states), or each IC states the relation on its own sw rail (more to write, and a library that forgets it has no enclosure).
3. *`dc` in an interface.* A buck IC's part repeats `dc switching_regulator .vin .buck.sw .buck.efficiency`, one line in each of the real board's two ICs. If `Buck` could state it, every IC would have it, as `LinearRegulator` has its own; but `dc` is a statement of a part (ADR 0076, amendment 1, rule "a part has one `dc` statement"), the interface has no rail named `.vin`, and an interface that states an element changes what an interface is. Taken: the IC states it.
4. *The equalities of the sheets stay.* The board's `.vout.voltage == .vin.voltage - .fuse.drop - ...` is redundant in the result and not in the picks (above), so the issue's "hand-written drops go away" is true of the result and of the board's own wiring, and not yet of a module that a fail test holds to a pick-time message. Alternative: change the module tests' expected messages to the later one. That moves the finding from the pick to the pass after it, and a pick refused earlier says which parts to change.
5. *Generic rows in the hull.* A generic row takes `0..bound` from a relation that bounds the field above, and no enclosure from a field with no bound. Alternative: a generic row takes the catalog's largest real value as its bound; wider than the design asked, and a guess about a value the design supplies.
