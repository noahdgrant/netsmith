# A chain carries the reverse peak and the fault current to the parts in series

Extends ADR 0086 (a chain carries the peak of its rail) with the two values it left open: the voltage a rail reaches the wrong way round, and the current a fuse in front of a part lets through. Closes the rest of issue 175.

## The problem

A real 12 V automotive board's input protection is a fuse, a series reverse-polarity diode and a TVS diode. After ADR 0086 the fuse is rated against the peak of its rail with no line on the board, but the board still wrote two more lines for the diode, and one number for it:

- `.reverse_polarity_diode.max_forward_current >= .fuse.rated_current`: the diode carries what the fuse lets through before it opens, which is more than the load's draw that the chain's `.current` gives it.
- `.reverse_polarity_diode.max_reverse_voltage >= .reverse_peak`, with `.reverse_peak &= 100V` stated on the input protection as an assumption: the negative transient of the battery line. `Rail` had a positive `.peak` only, so the number lived in the board and the diode's rating was compared with it by hand.

The half of issue 175's first point that is the load's draw, the fuse's rating and the diode's forward current against the current the chain carries, was built before this ADR: `chain_currents` and the balance (ADR 0014) give every part of a chain the draw of what is behind it as `.current`, so `Fuse` states `.current <= .rated_current` and `Diode` and `Schottky` state `.current <= .max_forward_current`, and a test pins that (`a_fuse_is_checked_against_the_draw_of_its_load`). What the chain did not carry is the fault side: what can get through to a part when something behind it fails.

## Decision

Three field names that the checker looks for, as it looks for `supply_peak`. No syntax is added.

**The reverse peak.** `Rail` has `.reverse_peak: Voltage optional` (ADR 0074): the highest voltage the rail reaches the wrong way round, as a positive number, as a design states it for its input (`.obd2.vbatt.reverse_peak &= 100V`). A part that has a `bridge` and a voltage field named `.supply_reverse_peak`, in a `~>` chain, is given the `.reverse_peak` of the rail the chain starts on (the rail is chosen as in ADR 0086), as the relation `part.supply_reverse_peak == rail.reverse_peak`. Only the first part of the chain that has the field is given it: the part that meets the reversed supply is the one that blocks it or conducts it, and what is behind it is protected by it, so a TVS diode behind a reverse-polarity diode is not checked against a transient that the diode stops. `Diode` and `Schottky` have it and state `.supply_reverse_peak <= .max_reverse_voltage`. `Tvs` has it and states `-.supply_reverse_peak >= 0V`: a unidirectional TVS diode that is first in a chain from a rail that can be reversed conducts, and the finding says so with the numbers (`-t.supply_reverse_peak must always be at least 0V, but -100V is not always above 0V`).

Both members are `optional`. A rail that states no `.reverse_peak` leaves the diode's `.supply_reverse_peak` unstated, so the relation is skipped and nothing is listed, and a diode in series from such a rail is as it was before: its `.bias`, if the board states it, is checked against `.max_reverse_voltage`, and is listed as not evaluated if not. A board that states the reverse peak gets the check with no line of its own.

**The fault current.** A part with a `bridge` and a current field named `.limit_current` is one that limits what gets through: `Fuse` has it and states `.limit_current == .rated_current`. A part in the same chain that has a current field named `.supply_fault_current` is given the `.limit_current` of the nearest part in front of it that has one, as the relation `part.supply_fault_current == fuse.limit_current`. `Diode` and `Schottky` have it, `optional`, and state `.supply_fault_current <= .max_forward_current`. The nearest fuse is used and not the smallest: it is never less than the smallest, so it is an upper bound, and the check is conservative. A fuse behind the diode gives it nothing. The value needs no rail, so a chain that starts on a signal has it. A diode with no fuse in front of it has none, and the relation is skipped.

**The conservative readings.** The fuse's rating is the current it lets through, though a real fuse carries 1.35 to 2 times its rating for a time before it opens: that factor is the datasheet's, and the board's to state if it wants the diode rated for it (`.d.max_forward_current >= .fuse.rated_current * 1.5`, which still checks). The reverse peak is the rail's, the input side: the diode's cathode is on a rail that may hold a voltage of its own (a capacitor behind the diode charged to 12 V), so a diode can see the reverse peak plus that, which is not added (see "Left open").

## Choices for the owner

- **The rail's `reverse_peak` is `optional`, so a rail that states none checks nothing and lists nothing.** The alternative is a member that is not optional, so a diode in a chain from a rail that states no reverse peak is listed as not evaluated. It would say that the reverse-polarity check was not made, which is useful on an input rail, and it would list one more note for every diode or TVS diode in a chain from any rail, such as the TVS diode of a rail behind a diode, which has no reverse peak and needs none. The ADR keeps the boards that state nothing silent, as the issue asked.
- **The reverse peak goes to the first part that has the field.** The alternative is every part of the chain, as `.supply_peak` does. It is wrong for the reverse peak: a series diode blocks it, and a TVS diode behind a blocking diode would be found to conduct a transient it never sees. The cost is that a chain whose first part with the field is not the one that blocks (a diode that is not rated, in front of one that is) reports the first.
- **A rail behind a blocking diode says `.reverse_peak &= 0V` itself.** Nothing derives it. The reverse peak after a blocking part is a rule about what the part does (a diode blocks it, a resistor drops some of it), which is the DC analysis's, not the chain's. Not built.
- **The fault current from any `.rated_current`.** The checker could read a fuse's `.rated_current` by its name instead of asking for `.limit_current`. Rejected: a `Switch` and a `Resistor` have a `.rated_current` that limits nothing, and a part that "limits" should say so; a field name is the one thing the checker looks for, as in the other two.
- **The smallest `.limit_current` in front, not the nearest.** It needs an expression the relations do not have (`min`), and the nearest is an upper bound. The cost is a pessimistic check when a larger fuse sits between a small one and the diode.
- **The fuse's trip multiple.** `.limit_current == .rated_current * 1.35` or a datasheet figure as a catalog column: the real number a diode sees. It needs a column in the `fuse` catalog (`data/jlcpcb/README.md` asks for a source for each), and a fuse's rating at which it holds is a different figure from the one at which it opens. Not built; a board that wants it writes the line above.
- **The diode's power.** The issue asks for the forward current and the power. `Diode` has no power rating in the standard library (`.max_power` is a Zener's), so there is nothing to compare; the forward drop times the fault current is `.forward_voltage * .supply_fault_current`, one line once a catalog has the column. Not built.
- **A bidirectional TVS diode.** `TvsBidirectional` (ADR 0089) is not given `.supply_reverse_peak`. It clamps a reverse transient as it clamps a forward one, so `.supply_reverse_peak <= .standoff_voltage` would find every use of it, and what to check is the owner's to say (the next two choices).
- **A TVS diode conducts a reverse transient by design, and a reversed battery by accident.** The unidirectional `Tvs` check treats the rail's `.reverse_peak` as a voltage the diode must not be exposed to, which is the reversed battery of a miswired harness (a steady voltage that turns the diode into a short). A negative transient is clamped by the same diode at about a forward drop, which is its job, and is limited by what the source can supply. `Rail` has one number for both, as the board's line had, so a design that wants the transient and not the reversal states no `.reverse_peak` on that rail. The alternative is two members, a steady `.reverse_voltage` for the reversed supply and a transient `.reverse_peak`, and the TVS diode's check reads the first; it is a second member on every rail. Not built.
- **A std module of the input protection** (option C of issue 175). Not built, as in ADR 0086: a fixed topology a board may not have, and a board that builds the circuit by hand has the checks too.
- **A part of the chain says it with syntax** and **the rail by name**: as ADR 0086 listed them for `supply_peak`, and for the same reasons not taken.
- **A negative `.reverse_peak`.** The sign: `-100V` is what a datasheet says ("pulse 1 reaches -100 V"). A positive number is taken so that every rating reads `<=` in the same direction as `.peak`, and so a design cannot state a "reverse peak" that is on the positive side. The cost is that the number has the opposite sign to the voltage it is. The doc comment says so.

## Cost

A board with a fuse in front of a diode is now found if the diode's `.max_forward_current` is below the fuse's rating, which the load's draw alone passed (`a_diode_behind_a_fuse_...` in `crates/netsmith/tests/std_kinds.rs` had a board with a 1 A diode behind a 3 A fuse that was clean, and is not). The catalog picks a diode that can carry the fuse's rating, so a board with a diode left to the catalog gets a bigger part. `Rail` has one more member that a design may state, and every board has it. The three field names are a convention a newcomer finds only in the doc comments of `Fuse`, `Diode`, `Schottky`, `Tvs` and `Rail`, and in the guide; a project's own diode part copied from the standard one has to add them.

## Left open

- The reverse voltage a diode sees is the supply's reverse peak plus whatever voltage the rail behind it holds. A board whose load keeps a capacitor charged to the input's voltage needs `.d.max_reverse_voltage >= .supply.reverse_peak + .load.peak`, written by hand.
- The first part with the field is given the reverse peak even when it is not the one that blocks (above).
- `Switch`, `Inductor` and other parts are not given `.supply_fault_current`; the field is one line on each once the owner has taken the convention.
