# A MOSFET's on-resistance is checked against the gate drive it is guaranteed at

Refs issue 63 (model gaps of the standard kinds).

## The problem

On a real 12 V automotive board a modem's two open-drain drivers are N-channel MOSFETs picked from the standard catalog: a 2N7002, whose 7 ohm is the datasheet's maximum at a gate-source voltage of 5 V (JSCJ datasheet, page 2, electrical characteristics: RDS(on) 7 ohm at VGS = 5 V and ID = 50 mA). The board drives the gate from a 3.3 V logic rail, 3.234 V to 3.333 V. The datasheet states no resistance at that drive, and a MOSFET driven below the voltage its table names has a larger resistance than the table. `NMosfet` and `PMosfet` read `.on_resistance` in the DC analysis (ADR 0076, amendment 4, `dc nmos`) but no relation compared the drive with the voltage the figure holds at, so the figure was used at a drive it does not apply to and the board listed the fact only in its own "verify before ordering" notes. The gate-drive check that exists, `.gate_source_voltage >= .gate_threshold`, is satisfied at 3.234 V (the 2N7002's threshold is at most 2.5 V): the channel starts to conduct, and its resistance is not the one the catalog holds.

## Decision

No syntax is added. `NMosfet` and `PMosfet` (`std/parts/transistor.nsm`) get:

- a member `.on_resistance_vgs: Voltage`: the gate-source voltage the datasheet guarantees `.on_resistance` at, a magnitude for both kinds as `.gate_source_voltage` is;
- the relation `.gate_source_voltage >= .on_resistance_vgs`: the drive the design gives the gate must always be at least it. The drive is the member the part already has, which a design states (`.gate_source_voltage &= logic.voltage`) and the board's other relation already checks against the threshold. The message is the checker's usual one and names the member paths and both ranges: `q.gate_source_voltage must always be at least q.on_resistance_vgs, but 3.135V..3.465V is not always above 5V`, with the row named by the pick's own message (`the closest of the 2 that fit, 2N7002 60V 115mA N-MOSFET SOT-23 C8545, fails because ...`);
- the column `.on_resistance_vgs optional` in `catalog n_mosfet` and `catalog p_mosfet` (ADR 0078).

The standard rows state it from the datasheets, each row's `source` and `notes` naming the revision, the page and the table: 2N7002 5 V (7 ohm at VGS = 5 V, ID = 50 mA), AO3400A 2.5 V (48 mohm at VGS = 2.5 V, ID = 3 A; AOS Rev 3, December 2011, page 2). `p_mosfet.csv` has the column in its header and still no row.

**A row that states no voltage.** A file may leave the column out, and a row may leave the cell empty. Such a row is offered to a design that says nothing about the column, and the relation is not evaluated for it: the field has no value, so the check is listed as not evaluated like any check on an unstated value. That is a change in the solver (`crates/netsmith-solve/src/solve.rs`, `asked_by_design`): ADR 0078 said that "a relation that names" a column is the design asking about it, and a row with no value is then never offered. A relation that the part's own definition states (`Relation::own`, set where a statement is lowered inside a `part` or `generic`) is not the design asking: `.gate_source_voltage >= .on_resistance_vgs` is the part's check of a value its row may record. A statement of a board or a module that names the field, a narrowing of it (`.q.on_resistance_vgs <= 3V`), a connection or a balance still is, and then only rows that record a value are offered. A relation of the part's own definition that names an `optional` member (ADR 0074) still counts as the design asking: it is in the model only because the design stated that member, so a `Zener`'s shift still refuses a row that states no temperature coefficient. Nothing else changes; a project's part that marks a column optional that its own relation reads, without an optional member in the relation, has its rows without a value offered now, and not refused.

**What a design does.** A board states nothing new and the check runs for every row that states a voltage. A board that wants a particular part states a requirement on the member, as for any column: `.q.on_resistance_vgs <= 3V` offers only rows that record a test voltage at or below 3 V (the AO3400A), whatever the drive. `.q.gate_threshold <= 1V` already works.

## What changes in existing designs

A board that drives an N-channel MOSFET from the catalog with less than 5 V is no longer given the 2N7002, the first row. The pick moves to the AO3400A (guaranteed at 2.5 V, threshold 0.65 V to 1.45 V), which a 3.3 V logic rail meets: the real finding of the request, as a different bill of materials. The guide's `Switch` example and the picks test of `crates/netsmith/tests/std_generics.rs` (`switch/Switch`: 3 trials and the AO3400A, where it was 2 and the 2N7002) show it. A board whose gate drive is at least 5 V (the 2N7002's `.on_resistance_vgs`) keeps its pick. No example is refused. A project's own row (a file of `catalogs/`) has no column, so the check is not evaluated for it and the build lists one note for each such MOSFET: `not evaluated: modem.vref_switch.vref_switch.gate_source_voltage >= modem.vref_switch.vref_switch.on_resistance_vgs`. The real board of the request shows both: its two N-channel drivers (3.234 V to 3.333 V of drive) move from the 2N7002 to the AO3400A, both Basic, and its P-channel switch, a project row with no test voltage, gets the one note. Its owner clears the note by reading the part's datasheet and filling the cell of its own file (the AO3401A's table gives 85 mohm at VGS = 2.5 V, which its 3.23 V to 3.465 V drive meets). The build still has no error.

## Choices for the owner

- **A relation of the part, with the solver offering rows that have no value.** Taken (above). The alternative is `.on_resistance_vgs: Voltage optional` (ADR 0074): the check is then skipped for a design that does not state the member, which includes the real board, because "stated" is what the design writes and a value a catalog row gives is never a statement (ADR 0074). The check would be silent for every design that does not already ask, which is the case the request is about. The member non-optional with a non-optional column makes every project's own file refuse until it has the column. The cost of the choice taken: a change of the rule of ADR 0078 for a column that a part's own relation reads, and one more not-evaluated note for each MOSFET whose row has no voltage. The alternative that keeps such a board silent is a skip of the relation for a row without a value after the pick, which needs the solver to take a relation out of the model per candidate; the check would then not tell a person that it was not made, and a check that cannot be made is reported as not evaluated elsewhere (the levels check).
- **The drive is `.gate_source_voltage`.** Taken: the member the design already states and the gate-threshold relation reads. The alternative is the DC analysis's voltage of the gate net (ADR 0076, amendment 4), which needs the analysis to model the gate's driver and is silent when it does not (a board with a 3.3 V rail on a pin that the analysis cannot model). It would read a number the design did not have to write, and would replace the stated one by a derived one. Not built.
- **A new column and a new member, not a range.** The test condition is one voltage for the row's `on_resistance`, so it is a voltage and not a range. A datasheet that guarantees several resistances at several voltages (the AO3400A's 26.5, 32 and 48 mohm at 10, 4.5 and 2.5 V) has its largest resistance stated, as ADR 0025 asks, and its test voltage is that row's. A design that wants the lower resistance at a higher drive needs a column with a value for each voltage, or a catalog row for each; not built.
- **Magnitude and the name.** `.on_resistance_vgs` reads as the datasheet's `RDS(on)` at `VGS`, a magnitude for a P-channel part as `.gate_source_voltage` is. `.test_gate_voltage` and `.on_resistance_at` are the alternatives; the first names the test and not the quantity it conditions, and the second would be the first member whose name ends in a preposition.
- **The comparison is `>=` over the whole range.** The drive's least value must reach the test voltage: 5 V +/- 5 % does not meet a guarantee at 5 V, and a design that drives at the test voltage says so with a rail that has no tolerance, or a row's voltage lower. The alternative is the drive's typical or greatest value, which is not conservative.
- **The temperature.** The datasheet's figure is at 25 C and a hot part has more resistance; the number is the datasheet's maximum at that temperature and is not derated. Out of scope, as it was.

## Cost

One member, one relation and one optional column in each of two generics, a field on `Relation`, a second form of `asked` in the solver, and the 2N7002's and AO3400A's cells. A board that does not state `.gate_source_voltage` gets the same not-evaluated line it had for the threshold check. The DC analysis still uses `.on_resistance` as the catalog states it. A project's own MOSFET rows state no voltage until their owner reads it from the part's datasheet.

## Left open

- The DC analysis reads `.on_resistance` at any drive. A pick at a drive below the voltage is now refused before the analysis runs; a row with no voltage is still read at the figure it states.
- A part that provides `NMosfet` or `PMosfet` (ADR 0068) and states no `.on_resistance_vgs` has the check not evaluated.
- A project's own rows (the real board's `n_mosfet.csv` and `p_mosfet.csv`) can state the column once a datasheet is read; none does here.
