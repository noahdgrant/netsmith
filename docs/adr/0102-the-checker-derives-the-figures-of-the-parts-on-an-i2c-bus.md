# The checker derives the figures of the parts on an I2C bus

Meets the second test (the obvious reading of `I2cPullups` is that the board states its figures; the checker fills them from other parts by the nets) and the third (the alternative, the board writes them, was in use for three checks). The owner chose it on 2026-11-10 after the buck ripple (#327), the rise time (#332) and the sink current (#349) each had a board write the figures of its parts, because a board cannot reach a member of a part that sits inside a module. Extends ADR 0088 and ADR 0086 (the checker gives a member a value by what it sits next to).

A part states what its datasheet says (`docs/DESIGN-RULES.md`): `.i2c.capacitance`, `.i2c.sink_current` and `.i2c.levels.vol` of its `I2c`. The pull-up module `I2cPullups` needs them, and a board writing `.pullups.bus_capacitance &= .a.i2c.capacitance + .b.i2c.capacitance + (20pF..40pF)` repeats the list of parts of the bus, so a forgotten or a later part is the board's silent error. The checker now finds the parts: every `I2c` interface of a part that shares a net with the pull-up's `.bus.sda` or `.bus.scl`, in the groups `bus_groups` already forms from the nets (`crates/netsmith-check/src/busnets.rs`, `bus_beside`). It makes two kinds of relation, in `connect.rs` beside `derive_bias`, before the optional members are skipped:

- `bus_capacitance == the .capacitance of each interface + .trace_capacitance`. `.trace_capacitance` is a new optional member of `I2cPullups`: the board's own estimate of the traces, connector and cable (`.pullups.trace_capacitance &= 20pF..40pF`). It is the addend that says the board wants a sum, so a board that states none gets nothing derived: the pins alone would pass a bound the traces break.
- For each interface, `resistance >= (supply.voltage - levels.vol) / sink_current` of that part. One relation for each part, not one for "the least current": the least `IOL` and the greatest `VOL` can be two parts, so no single pair is the worst case, and `min(.f)` gives no value (ADR 0098). The solver judges the greatest of them. A failure names the part.

A part that states no figure drops its relation (ADR 0074 skips a relation that reads an unstated optional member), and the solver names it (`crates/netsmith-solve/src/busfigures.rs`) as a not-evaluated line: for the capacitance, when a bound on it or on the rise time waits; for the sink current, when another part on the bus states one. A bus where no part states a sink current has no bound and no line, as before.

A board that states a figure itself (`.bus_capacitance`, or `.sink_current` or `.low_level`) is judged on its figure and the derivation of that figure is not made for it. Both would say one fault twice, and an equation against a narrower range the board states does not narrow: it reads as a figure that cannot be proved. The board's figure is its own responsibility, as it was.

Rejected. The board writes the sum (the old shape): it names the parts of the bus again, and a part that states no figure is silent in a guard (#349). A language construct that sums or takes the least of a member over a bus: new syntax or a new relation kind, the owner's, and the per-part relations need none. A std module that holds the parts: it would own the bus, against "only a board names nets".

Cost. A newcomer will not find the rule by reading `I2cPullups`: its doc comment and the guide say it. The module is found by its name, `I2cPullups`, and its members' names, as ADR 0086 found `supply_peak`; a copy of the module under another name gets nothing. A board with parts that state `IOL` and a pull-up too strong for one now fails where it passed. A part on a peripheral function of a microcontroller (no `I2c` interface) is not in the sum, and a bus with one gets no sum.

Left open, for the owner. Whether the field-name convention is the form wanted (ADR 0086's question again). The same derivation for the other buses, and for a buck stage's ESR: `.output_esr == .output_cap.esr` is refused today, since a statement of an optional member in its own definition states it for every use (ADR 0074), and a catalog row's value is never a statement, so the ESR a row records would not reach the relation. The options are a rule that an optional member a catalog column provides is read by the relations that name it, a marker on the catalog column that means "read me", or exposing `output_cap` to a board. Whether a figure the board states should narrow the derived one (a `?=`-like default) and not replace it.
