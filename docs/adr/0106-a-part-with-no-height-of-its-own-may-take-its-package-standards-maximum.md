# A part with no height of its own may take its package standard's maximum, labelled as such

Refs issue #430, P9e. The owner decided on 2026-11-12 that a part with no stated and no catalog height may use the height its package's standard gives, with its own provenance, so that the checks and the reports can tell it from a stated one; the owner left the figure and the checks' wording to this record. It decides what ADR 0105 left open ("whether a package standard's nominal height may count as a height source") and replaces the three height sources of ADR 0105 with four. The README allows no partial supersession, so ADR 0105 is marked superseded by this one, and everything else ADR 0105 decides (what is drawn, read-only, one scene as glTF, the rasterizer, three.js in its own chunk) stands as written there. It meets test 1 (a word of the `.glb` extras and the `board3d` report that viewers and agents read), test 2 (a newcomer reads "nominal" as the middle of a tolerance, and expects the standard's figure to be a part's height for every check) and test 3 (it rejects the alternatives below, among them ADR 0094's rejection of a height from the package's name).

**Four sources, the strongest first.** `stated` (the source or the physical file states `.height`), `catalog` (the picked row's `height` cell, ADR 0094), `standard` (the _Standard height_ of the part's footprint) and `unknown`. A stronger source always wins and is never compared with a weaker one. The standard height is never written into the design's heights: the board keeps the part's own height and reads the standard's from the footprint only where there is none (`Placed::standard_height`, `crates/netsmith-pcb/src/standard_height.rs`). The word is `standard`, not `nominal`, because the figure is a maximum.

**The figure is the standard's maximum.** A height is an upper bound held against a limit (ADR 0094), so the figure is the greatest seated height (`A`) the standard allows a body in that package. The 3D view draws the part to the same figure, so the picture and the checks never show two heights for one part. No nominal is kept: none of the maker drawings read prints one for `A`. A footprint has a standard height only when a maker's drawing of that exact outline was read and cites the JEDEC registration (JEDEC's own documents need a login); each row of the table names the registration and the drawing, and the module's header records each document, revision, page and checksum, as `data/jlcpcb/README.md` records a catalog row's evidence. Chip resistors and capacitors have none: the EIA and IEC size codes fix length and width, and the thickness is each maker's. QFN, DFN and SON have none: their outlines come in several profiles and the footprint's name does not say which.

**How the checks treat it.** Where the standard's maximum fits a height limit, the part passes, and the pass is said to be the standard's: a `note:` in `fab`'s output and in `fab-notes.txt` (`Board::standard_passes`), and `standard_passes` in the `board3d` report. Where the maximum is over the limit, the part is not `too tall`: a part in that package may be lower, so whether it fits is unknown, and it is reported as a part of unknown height is (`height_unchecked`, a warning, an error when constraints are strict), with the standard and its figure in the message. The placer and the editor's drag do not read a standard height. _Height coverage_ counts a standard height as known and names it apart: `standard` in the `board3d` report, in the `.glb` root's `coverage` and in the coverage line of `fab-notes.txt`; the 3D view colours it in a colour of its own.

**Rejected.** The standard's nominal for the drawing and its maximum for the checks: two heights for one part, and the drawings print no nominal. A `too_tall` error when the maximum is over a limit: it would stop `fab` for a part that may fit, a refusal the owner has not asked for (the alternative is open below). Writing the standard's figure into the design's heights, as a catalog height is: every reader would see a stated height. Matching footprint names by pattern (`SOIC-*`): a row without a drawing of that outline. A standard height for chips from one maker's datasheet: that is a maker's figure, which belongs in a catalog row (ADR 0094).

**Cost.** A table of 19 footprints to keep, each with a drawing; a fourth colour in the 3D view's themes; a part in a package outside the table stays flat until its row or its design states a height. A board with a SOIC under a 1.5 mm limit now warns with the standard's 1.75 mm where it warned with no figure.

**Left open.** Whether a standard's maximum over a limit should be `too_tall` (an error) rather than unchecked; whether the placer should keep a part of standard height out of a limit its maximum is over; rows for the other generated families when their drawings are read.
