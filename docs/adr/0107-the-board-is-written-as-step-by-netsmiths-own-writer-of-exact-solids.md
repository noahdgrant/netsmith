# The board is written as STEP by netsmith's own writer of exact solids

Refs issue #430, P7. The owner decided on 2026-11-12 that a STEP export is wanted, first of the three STEP items, and left how it is written to this record. It decides the "STEP export" that ADR 0105 left open. It meets test 1 (a file format that mechanical CAD tools and scripts will read, with product names they will key on), test 2 (a newcomer reaches for a CAD kernel or KiCad's `pcb export step`) and test 3 (it rejects those, and a faceted file, below).

**The decision.** `netsmith board3d --step <file.step>` writes the board's _Scene_ (the same scene the `.glb` is written from) as ISO 10303-21, AP214 (`AUTOMOTIVE_DESIGN`, which FreeCAD, Fusion, SolidWorks and KiCad's own reader all open), by netsmith's own writer, `crates/netsmith-3d/src/step/`. Each solid is an exact boundary representation of the shapes netsmith makes: planar faces with polygon loops for the extrusions and boxes, cylinders for holes, round corners and pins, so a CAD tool measures a hole's diameter and snaps to its centre. Each edge is one curve used by its two faces, so a reader sews nothing.

**What is in it.** An assembly: the root product is the board's label, its children `board` (the board through its chosen thickness, with its rounded outline, cutouts, and every drilled hole, round or a slot) and one product per part with a body. A part's product has its path as its id and its designator as its name, so the CAD tool's tree shows `U1`, `J1`; its description holds the part's kind and its _Height provenance_. A part's solids are its _Package shape_ (named `body`, `metal` or `mark`, coloured as the 3D view's light palette), or its body box from its face to its far end; a stated pin protrusion is a box named `pins`. The copper, mask, silkscreen and height limits are left out: they are in the `.glb`, and a mechanical engineer fits a case to the board and the parts.

**No height is invented.** A part of unknown height has no solid. It is its body's outline as a closed polyline on its face, coloured as the 3D view's unknown parts, and its description says `height unknown: outline only`, so the engineer sees where something stands whose height nobody knows (ADR 0094, ADR 0106: the four sources stand as they are). Leaving it out altogether was rejected: an empty spot on the board reads as free space.

**Deterministic.** The same scene gives the same bytes: the header's time stamp is fixed at `1970-01-01T00:00:00`, the entities are numbered in the order they are made from the scene's fixed order, a point or a direction is written once, a real to ten places, and the circles come from the scene's numbers, not the platform's maths library. No KiCad, no system library and no new crate (ADR 0097).

**Measured.** STEP against `.glb`, from `board3d` with no routing: `led` 23 kB (137 kB), `four_layer` 88 kB (412 kB), `outline_and_pours` 112 kB (2.0 MB), `easyduino_esp32s3` 312 kB (11.6 MB), `rp2350_minimal` 372 kB (7.8 MB), `orbit` 776 kB (4.1 MB). OpenCascade 8.0 (the kernel FreeCAD and KiCad read STEP with, through its Python bindings `cadquery-ocp`) reads the file of every example `board3d` places: every solid valid with a positive volume, the bounding box the board's outline and thickness with its tallest part, the designators as names, the colours read back.

**Rejected.** A faceted file (each triangle of the meshes a face): the simplest writer, but a hole is a polygon a CAD tool cannot measure as a diameter, and it is larger: `rp2350_minimal`'s board and parts are 6,506 triangles, about 0.9 MB as a `FACETED_BREP` (some 140 bytes a triangle) and 3.3 MB as planar faces with edges (some 500 bytes), from the entity sizes of the exact file, against 372 kB exact. The crate `truck` (`truck-stepio`, Apache-2.0): pure Rust, but six crates and the `ruststep` parser for a writer, its header takes the time from the clock, and it writes its planes as B-spline surfaces. OpenCascade bindings (`opencascade-rs`, LGPL-2.1): a C++ build of OCCT with CMake, minutes of build and a C++ toolchain for every user. KiCad's `pcb export step`: ADR 0097, and its parts are KiCad's models, not the scene. AP242 in place of AP214: AP214 is what every reader above opens, and the file uses nothing AP242 adds.

**Cost.** A STEP writer and its B-rep builder are netsmith's own code (about 1,100 lines), with tests that check every shell is closed. A BGA's balls are the one shape that is not exact: an ellipsoid has no elementary STEP surface, so a ball is the polyhedron of the 3D view's ball (96 flat faces). The shapes are drawn as the 3D view draws them, so a body box that a mark sits level with ends 0.02 mm short of it (`TOP_GAP`), as in the `.glb`; the part's highest point is still its height.

**Left open.** Whether `fab` writes `<board>.step` by default beside the `.glb`, with an off switch as the `.glb` has (the owner's question; until answered, `fab` does not write it); the copper as solids, for a thermal or an RF model; user STEP models in place of a part's shape and the enclosure STEP with a fit check, each with a decision of its own (#430, P7).
