# A part may be drawn from a project's own STEP model, read by netsmith's own reader

Refs issue #430, P7. On 2026-11-12 the owner said user STEP models are wanted, after the STEP export (ADR 0107). This record decides what ADR 0105 left open: "user-supplied STEP models (local, never bundled)". How a part names its model and what the model's height means are the owner's to decide, so this record is `proposed`, and the owner's questions are on #430. It meets test 1 (a std interface and a file that projects will name), test 2 (a newcomer expects the model's box to give the part its height, and a CAD kernel to read it) and test 3 (it rejects those, below).

**What it does.** A project names a STEP file in its own tree for a part. `render`, the `.glb`, the editor's 3D view and the STEP export then draw that model in place of the part's _Package shape_. The model is placed at the footprint's origin, on the part's face, turned with the part and mirrored on the bottom face. An offset in mm and a rotation in degrees correct a model drawn in another frame. netsmith never bundles a model and never downloads one. The path is relative to the file that names it and must stay inside the project. The file's units are its own (`SI_UNIT(.MILLI.,.METRE.)`, or an inch `CONVERSION_BASED_UNIT`) and are read into mm.

**How a part names it (owner's choice, recommendation first).** (1) A built-in member of every part, as `.height` and `.pin_protrusion` are, of a std interface `StepModel`: `.usb.model.file &= "models/usb4105.step"`, `.usb.model.z &= -0.2mm`, `.usb.model.rz &= 90`. A project part states it once in its definition. A board or the physical file states it for one part. (2) Columns of a `[[part]]` row of `kicad.toml`, beside the footprint the model is aligned to: `model = "models/usb4105.step"`, `model_offset = "0 0 -0.2mm"`, `model_rotation = "0 0 90"`. (3) A `[[model]]` table in `netsmith.toml`, keyed by footprint id, with `file`, `offset` and `rotation`. (1) is recommended. It is the precedent of `.height` (one way to state a physical fact of a part, in the same places), and a contradiction between two statements is the usual error. (2) and (3) need no language change, but they tie a model to a footprint, and two makers' parts can share a footprint. The rotation is applied about X, then Y, then Z, before the offset, so that the numbers of a KiCad footprint's `(model ... (offset) (rotate))` line can be copied in. A test pins this against a model KiCad places.

**The model is a drawing, not a height (owner's choice).** Recommended: a part's height still comes from the four sources of ADR 0106. Where the model's top is above that height, by more than the reader's chord tolerance, that is a warning that names both figures and suggests `.height &= <top>`. A part of unknown height drawn from a model stays unknown to the checks. Rejected as a recommendation: a fifth provenance `model`, after `catalog` and before `standard`. A maker's license often forbids committing its model to a public repository. A clone or a CI job without the file would then check heights differently from the author's machine. A missing model is therefore a warning, and the part falls back to its generated shape.

**netsmith's own reader (recommended).** A Part 21 parser and the B-rep entities that ADR 0107's writer uses, plus cones, spheres, tori, ellipses and B-spline curves and surfaces (rational too). It is tessellated with the scene's own triangulator and trig (`crate::mesh`). The STEP export needs no tessellation: it copies the model's entities into its file, renumbered, under the part's product with an `ITEM_DEFINED_TRANSFORMATION`, so the export stays exact. It runs only when a project names a model. The editor gets the model's triangles in the `.glb` (`GET /api/pcb/3d`), so the page needs no parser and the 3D chunk's 250 kB budget is untouched. A face the reader cannot draw refuses the whole model, with a message that names the entity and its line. A model is never half drawn.

**Measured** on five KiCad library models (CC-BY-SA, fetched for a local test, not committed): three USB-C receptacles, a Micro-B and a SOIC-8. Of 2,347 faces, 1,847 are planes, 426 cylinders, 17 cones, 4 spheres and 4 tori. 34 are B-spline surfaces, in 2 of the 5 files. Four files have B-spline curves, and one has rational ones. `truck-stepio` 0.3 with `truck-meshalgo` 0.4 (Apache-2.0, last release 2024-09) read and tessellated four files in 18 to 150 ms. One face of the GCT USB4085 model was left with no triangles. The Molex Micro-B model had not finished after 100 s and had used 9.5 GB, so it was stopped. The order of its output changed from run to run until the shells were sorted by id. It brings about 100 crates (a C build of `lzma-sys`, `vtkio`, `nom` 3 with a future-incompatibility warning) and adds about 2 MB to a stripped binary. OpenCascade in WASM (`occt-import-js` 0.0.23, LGPL-2.1) read all five in 0.4 to 1.4 s, but its module is 7.6 MB, 3.1 MB gzipped: twelve times the chunk budget. It would also serve only the editor, not `render`, the `.glb` or the export. `ruststep` alone parses and does not tessellate.

**Rejected.** `truck`: it hung on a real file, and its order and maths are not ours to make deterministic. OpenCascade in the editor: its size, its license, and three of the four outputs are written by Rust (ADR 0105). OpenCascade bindings in Rust: ADR 0107's reasons. Reading the `(model ...)` line of a project's `.kicad_mod`: it covers only footprints the project draws, and a line may name a VRML file (netsmith's generated footprints name KiCad's `.wrl`).

**Deterministic.** The bytes of the `.glb` and the STEP export depend only on the source and the model's bytes. Entities are read in id order, the chord tolerance is fixed, and trig is the scene's own, rounded to a nanometre as the scene is. The model's SHA-256 is in the node's `extras` and in the part's product description, so a changed model shows in a diff.

**Cost.** A reader of some 3,000 lines, the B-spline surfaces the hardest part, with tests that read back what our writer writes and a corpus of model files that the license allows to be committed. The `.glb` grows by each model's triangles. A `.glb` or `.step` with a maker's model in it is the user's to share under that model's license. `fab-notes.txt` names the parts drawn from a model.

**Left open.** Pending the owner: the syntax, the height meaning, and the GLOSSARY term. Also open: a scale for a model drawn in the wrong unit; AP242's tessellated faces; the enclosure STEP (#84), which may use this reader.
