# A system's boards are placed against each other by their mates, in one scene

Refs issue #430, P7, the fourth item, approved by the owner on 2026-11-12. It decides what ADR 0105 left open, "boards joined by connectors as one 3D assembly (ADR 0040)", and the "stacking and `.mated_height`" and "mated-connector alignment across boards" that ADRs 0040 and 0048 left unbuilt. A std interface and what an error rule refuses were the owner's; on 2026-11-12 the owner chose the recommended option of each, and the outputs below with `fab` writing the system's files (#430). It meets test 1 (a std field, a report and a `.glb` that agents read), test 2 (a newcomer expects a default stack height, or the boards laid side by side) and test 3 (it rejects those, below). The word is _System scene_: _Assembly_ is `.assembly`, and _System_ avoids it.

**What it is.** `board3d --board System` builds each member's _Scene_ as it is today, in its own frame (`docs/BOARD3D.md`), and places it with one rigid motion. The base is the first member the system declares: the system scene is in its frame, so `--board System/member` of the base and the system agree in numbers. Each mate places the member it reaches from a placed one. Contact N meets contact N, so the motion is the turn about z, with a flip when both stand on the same face of their boards (both on top, or both on the bottom), that puts each pad centre of one connector on its partner's (a least-squares fit of the pad centres, which the footprints already hold). The distance between the two boards' faces is the _Mated height_. Only straight-through connectors on parallel boards are placed: every std header and socket is one, and nothing else has a `.mating` (ADR 0040).

**Where the mated height comes from (the owner chose A).** (A) Each connector states its _Seat_, a std field `.mating.seat`: how far above its face its partner rests (a header's plastic top, a socket's body top). It is a catalog column with a `source`, as `pin_current` is, and a range, so the mated height is the two seats' sum with both tolerances: the std 2.54 mm rows give 2.50 +/- 0.20 mm (header) and 8.50 +/- 0.15 mm (socket), from the drawings `data/jlcpcb/README.md` already cites, so 10.65 to 11.35 mm. It is a fact of one part, true whatever it mates with. (B) A stack height stated for a mate in a physical file of the system (ADR 0048 left one open): every system writes a number the parts already know, and the two can disagree. Recommended later, as a requirement checked against (A), with standoffs, when a board needs it. (C) A table of connector pairs, for mezzanine families whose maker lists a stack per plug and receptacle, where seats do not add: when the first such part is added. Rejected: the header generator's plastic figure per pitch (it is a drawing figure, and #486 says a maker's part can differ); the socket's catalog `height` (a maximum, not a seat). The scene places at the least mated height, the worst case for the checks below, and the report gives both ends.

**What cannot be placed is shown apart, never guessed (the owner chose A).** (A) A member no mate places (a seat unset, a connector with no footprint or no layout yet, a join by `~` alone, a cable) is drawn beside the base along +x, 10 mm past its bounds, in its own frame, labelled `apart: <reason>`, and nothing across boards is checked for it. A finding `system_apart` says so, a warning, an error with strict constraints, as `height_unchecked` is. (B) Refuse the system scene: nothing to look at, for a missing number on one part. Rejected: a default mated height, or one from the part's height (ADR 0094, ADR 0106).

**Two mates of one pair of boards** (a card on two headers, a loop of three boards) over-determine the motion. The first placed in source order decides; each other mate's pads must land on their partners within 0.1 mm (a std doc comment), else `mate_misaligned` names the mate, the contact and the distance. A pinout that only fits mirrored is the same finding.

**What is checked across boards (the owner chose what is an error).** On what the height checks measure: _Body_ boxes to their ADR 0106 height, pin protrusions, and each board's solid; a part's drawn `.model` is not read (ADR 0108). The two connectors of a mate are exempt against each other, since one goes into the other. Findings, each with the parts, the members and the numbers:
- `system_collision`: a body or a board overlaps another member's body or board by more than 0.01 mm;
- `system_clearance`: a gap under a minimum (a std field; 0.5 mm proposed, as ADR 0109's);
- `mate_misaligned`, above;
- `system_unchecked`: a part of unknown height on a face that faces another member, or a standard height that collides (a part in that package may be lower).

Chosen: `system_collision` of a stated or catalog height or of a board, and `mate_misaligned`, are errors under the `constraints` key, as `too_tall` is; `fab` of a system refuses on them and `--allow-drc-violations` never waives them. The rest are warnings; `system_unchecked` is an error with strict constraints. Alternatives: every finding a warning for a first release; every finding an error with strict constraints.

**Geometry stays exact.** With parallel boards every body, board and limit is a prism: a polygon in plan and a z interval. A turn about z and a flip keep that, so a collision is a z overlap and a polygon intersection (`mesh::boolean`), with no triangles; the cost is the pairs of bodies of facing boards, some hundreds.

**Outputs.** `board3d --board System --json` gives `members: [{member, motion, placed | apart}]` (the motion a 4x4 matrix and its turn, flip and offset), each member's report as `--board System/member` gives it, `mates: [{a, b, contacts, mated_height: {min, max}, seats, residual}]` and the findings above. The `.glb` root is `system`, with one node per member carrying its matrix, and under it that board's subtree as it is today; ids gain the member (`led/J1`). `render --board System` draws and frames the whole; `--section` cuts in the system frame. The STEP export is one product for the system, a product per member (ADR 0107's assembly) and an `ITEM_DEFINED_TRANSFORMATION` each, ADR 0108's path. The editor's 3D view lists `System` above its members in the board selector; it toggles and isolates a member and explodes along the stack. A part picked in it selects that member's PCB and schematic, which stay one board each. A member alone is still `--board System/member`, unchanged. `fab` of a system writes `<out>/<System>/<System>.glb` and `.step` with the switches of each board's.

**Not in this step**, each placed apart until it is modelled: right-angle connectors and boards at an angle (a mating axis on the footprint, then a 3D overlap test of oriented boxes); edge connectors and a card in a slot (ADR 0040's `card_thickness`); cables and wire to board (ADR 0040: not modelled; recommended never drawn as a path, which nobody stated); a case around a system (ADR 0109 left it open: the system frame is the base's, so the base's case can check the whole later).

**Cost.** A `SystemScene` in `netsmith-pcb` beside `scene3d.rs`: the members' scenes, the motions and the mates, about 600 lines with the fit and the checks; `Mesh::transform` already moves meshes, so `render` and the rasterizer take the members' meshes moved. The `.glb` writer gains a root and one node per member; the STEP writer a level of products. A seat column for the 8 std header rows, from drawings already read. The editor's viewer reads node matrices (three.js does) and prefixed ids. Placing a system places every member, as `fab` already does.

**Left open.** A system's physical file (a stated stack, standoffs, the base); pair tables; the mating axis; the clearance and alignment figures, in the std doc comments; the GLOSSARY entries (_System scene_, _Seat_, _Mated height_), with the code.
