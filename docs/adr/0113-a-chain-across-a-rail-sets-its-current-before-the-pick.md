# A chain across a rail sets its current before the pick

A `~>` chain passed one current (ADR 0014), but nothing said what that current was until the DC analysis solved the picked parts (ADR 0076). So `.supply.pos ~> .r1 ~> .led ~> .supply.neg` with `.led.current &= 5mA..15mA` on a 3.3 V rail picked a 10 ohm resistor, the first row, about 150 mA, with only the unconstrained-pick warning (issue #621). Every LED in the examples carried Ohm's law by hand: `.led.current == (.supply.voltage - .led.forward_voltage) / .r1.resistance`.

**The decision.** The checker writes Kirchhoff's voltage law along a chain as a relation, before anything is picked: `I == (V - Vf1 - Vf2 ...) / (R1 + R2 ...)`. `I` is the through-current of a part whose current the design narrowed (an LED's window), so a failure names the field the design wrote, or else of the first part that has one (the others are equal to it, ADR 0014), `V` the rail's `.voltage`, each `Vf` a forward voltage and each `R` a resistance. The solver then treats it as any equation of the design, so the LED's window picks the resistor (120 ohm here) and the pick is no longer unconstrained. The relation is written where all of these hold: the chain starts on a rail's `pos` (the rail `derive_chain_values` already finds for the peak, ADR 0086), ends on a net with the same rail's `neg`, has no other pin on a net between its parts (a rail or a port there counts, which ADR 0014's equality of currents does not count), has at least one resistor, and each part is a `dc resistor` or a `dc diode` whose pins run the way the chain does (`connect.rs`, `chain_voltage_law`). A relation the design writes about the same current stays allowed and must agree: it stays the equation that narrows the current, and the chain's is checked against it. Counted as a second equation, the chain's would make the current a value two equations state (ADR 0065, 9.1), and a window that reaches the LED through a member (`.led.current &= .current`) would be lost (`solve.rs`, `sole_equations`).

**What counts as the part's DC element** (the owner left it to the agent): the part's own `dc` statement, the one the DC analysis after the pick solves (ADR 0076, amendment 1). Only `dc resistor` and `dc diode` are used, because they are the elements with a fixed drop along a series current. A `dc nmos` or `dc pmos` is a switch whose state depends on its gate, and a regulator or source is not a two-terminal part in a chain. The model is the analysis's, not a second one: the relation reads the same fields (`.resistance`, `.dc_resistance`, `.forward_voltage`) the `dc` statement names, and the analysis after the pick solves the same chain with the rest of the network. `Led` now states `dc diode .anode .cathode .forward_voltage`; it was a boundary of the analysis "until its own decision" (ADR 0076, the standard library), and this is it.

**A diode that blocks.** The relation is that of every diode conducting. Where the rail can fall below the sum of the forward voltages, the right side goes negative, where the analysis would say zero. For an LED that is refused by its own `.current >= 0mA`, the same refusal the hand-written equation gave: an indicator that may stay dark is a fault, and the solver picks another LED. For a standard `Diode` the negative end is only a wider range, which the analysis narrows after the pick.

**A part that is not one of the two** (the owner, 2026-11-13): the chain leaves the current free, as before. The checker records the first such part on the chain (`Chain::stopped_by`), and the existing unconstrained-pick finding of a part in that chain names it ("the `~>` chain it is in sets no current, because `lamp` (Lamp) in it is not a `dc resistor` or a `dc diode` the way the chain runs"). No new finding is made: a chain whose parts are all limited otherwise says nothing. When the rail has no voltage the relation waits; its "not evaluated" note is left out when a check of a part that reads the chain's current waits too and says so.

**Rejected.** *A refusal* of a chain with a part the law cannot read: it would refuse boards that build and are right (a lamp of a project's own with no `dc`, a MOSFET that switches an LED). *Running the DC analysis inside the search* for every candidate: ADR 0076 rejected it for its cost, and the relation is exact for the one shape it covers. *A table of std parts the law knows*: the `dc` statement already says what a part is at DC, and a project's own part joins by stating one. *Interval arithmetic's widening*: each field appears once in the relation, so the bound is the circuit's own.

**Cost.** A chain across a rail through resistors only (`.supply.pos ~> .pull ~> .supply.neg`) now has its current from the start, which only settles values the analysis gave later. Adding the `dc diode` to `Led` brings every LED into the analysis after the pick, so a window stated on an LED's current is now a budget the circuit must fit (ADR 0038): a design that states a resistor too small for its LED's window is now an error, where it was not checked. In the examples, `enclosure_sensor` moved its three green LEDs from a row of 2.6 V to 3.4 V, which could not light on its 3.3 V rail, to one of 1.9 V to 2.4 V, and lost twelve notes; the others' picks are unchanged.

**Left open.** A chain that starts on a logic pin (the status indicator) has no rail voltage and gets no relation; reading the pin's levels as the source is not planned until a board needs it. A tapped chain (a divider with a load), a chain between two different rails, and a Zener or TVS in reverse breakdown are not read either. A `Switch` is still a boundary.
