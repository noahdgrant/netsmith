# A chain from a logic pin or between two rails sets its current before the pick

ADR 0113 wrote Kirchhoff's voltage law only for a `~>` chain from a rail's `pos` back to the same rail's `neg`. The most common LED on a real board is driven by a GPIO (`.mcu.gpio.line ~> .r1 ~> .led ~> .supply.neg`), and that chain got no relation: its resistor was an unconstrained pick, and the DC analysis after the pick refused the LED's window outright, because with the pin low the LED is dark and carries 0 A (issue #655). The issue and the owner's decisions of 2026-11-13 settle what is read (the pin's levels and its drive limit, and two rails) and that a negative corner is refused. The rules below that they do not settle were decided by the agent, each by keeping one way to write a thing and ADR 0113's rules, and accepted by the orchestrating agent under the owner's delegation while the owner was away (2026-11-13). It meets two tests: it surprises a newcomer (the pin's source is a range, and its limit is the level's test current) and it rejects obvious alternatives (below).

**The relation.** `I == (Vstart - Vend - Vf1 ...) / (R1 + ...)`, over the same parts as ADR 0113 (each a `dc resistor` or `dc diode` the way the chain runs, no tap, at least one resistor). The start is a rail's `pos` or a logic pin that drives it high; the end is the start's ground, another rail's `pos` on that ground, or a logic pin that sinks it low (`connect.rs`, `chain_voltage_law`). The relation note (#653) shows it as a board would write it, `.led.current == ((.mcu.gpio.voh .. .mcu.gpio.reference.voltage) - .led.forward_voltage) / .r1.resistance`.

**A pin's level is a range, not the level alone.** A pin that drives high is at least its `voh` (at its test current) and at most its reference rail's voltage (at no load), so the source is `.voh .. .reference.voltage`, ADR 0066's range with values at its ends; a pin that sinks is `0V .. .vol`. With `voh` alone the relation would be narrower than the circuit: it picked a resistor whose current the DC analysis then found above the window at the supply's top. The range is the analysis's own model (ADR 0076, amendment 4: the supply through an output resistance from zero to the level's drop over its test current), so the pick and the analysis agree. That resistance now runs down to zero as a branch, as a resistor's does (amendment 16), where it stopped at 1 mohm, so a pin can be exactly at its supply, as the relation says.

**The pin's limit is its level's test current.** `Logic` states no `ioh` or `iol`. What it states is the load each level is measured at, `.voh &= .vdd.voltage - 0.4V at 8mA`: the datasheet's `IOH` and `IOL` are those test conditions, and the level is promised only up to them. So the checker writes `I <= 8mA` for the pin at each end of the chain, pointed at the level's statement, and a window above it is refused there. A level with no test current leaves the chain's current free (`Chain::unloaded_level`), and the unconstrained-pick finding names it, as ADR 0113 names a part with no `dc`. A pin qualifies when it is the `.line` of a part's `Logic` (output, tri-state or bidirectional; open drain only to sink) and alone on its net with the chain, so all it gives is the chain's current.

**The window is judged with the pin driving the chain.** A GPIO LED is dark when the pin is low, by design, not a fault. The checker records the driving pins (`Chain::pins`), and the DC analysis judges a stated window of a part in the chain over the combinations with each such pin in its driving state (`dcpass.rs`, `driven_runs`); what it writes for an unstated field is still over every state.

**The second rail must hold its voltage.** A rail at the end counts when a part holds it: a regulator's output or a part's `dc source` rail (`connect.rs`, `holds_voltage`). A rail whose voltage is what the chain leaves it (a module's output behind a fuse) is a load, and a relation there named a current the circuit cannot have. A board's own rail does not count, though the DC analysis takes one with a stated voltage as a source: at the end of a chain it is as often a load with the window of voltage it takes (`.supply.pos ~> .r1 ~> .load.pos` with `.load.voltage &= 5V..16V`), and the relation turned such boards, which built, into errors.

**Rejected.** *New `ioh`/`iol` members of `Logic*`: a second figure beside the test current that says the same thing, and the absolute maximum (25 mA on many MCUs) is not the load the level holds at. *`voh` alone as the source*: narrower than the circuit, as above. *A window that holds in every state of the pin*: no GPIO LED could have one. *Any rail at the end, or a board's rail with a stated voltage*: refused boards whose chains feed a load.

**Cost.** A level with a test current now limits every chain from its pin, so a board whose window is above its pin's test current is an error where it was not checked; the fix is the datasheet's level at a higher load. The analysis of a driving pin moves by at most its current times 1 mohm. No example's pick or design file changes.

**Left open.** A pin of a bus's `Levels`, a pin that shares its net with another load, a chain whose end rail is a module's port or a board's rail that stands for a supply, a tapped chain, and a Zener or TVS in reverse breakdown.
