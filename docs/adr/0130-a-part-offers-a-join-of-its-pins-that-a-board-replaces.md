# A part offers a join of its pins that a board replaces, written `?~`

Issue #738 (epic #638): `Lt8641`'s `support` section tied `BIAS` to ground (`.bias ~ .vin.neg`), the datasheet's option when no supply is available, and a board could not change it. The datasheet says "for output voltages of 3.3V to 25V this pin should be tied to VOUT" (Pin Functions, p.10), and the part cannot reach the output that `Buck` makes. ADR 0120 lets a board join a pin of the IC it named, but `.reg.ic.bias ~ .reg.vout.pos` then put the output and the ground on one net, and ADR 0116 lets a board narrow a value, not a join. The owner was away; the orchestrating agent chose the form below, and this ADR is `proposed` until the owner reads it. It meets two of the README's tests: it is hard to reverse (std and boards would be written with it) and it rejects obvious alternatives.

**Decision.** `.a ?~ .b` in a part's own definition, or in its `support` section, joins pin `.a` to pin `.b` as `~` does, unless something else joins `.a`. It is the join counterpart of `?=` (ADR 0051): an offer a board accepts by saying nothing and replaces by joining the pin. The checker reads the statement with the part and applies it after every other join of the board has been read (`apply_default_joins` in `crates/netsmith-check/src/connect.rs`): a default holds when the first pin is alone in its group, so any `~` or `~>` of that pin, from the board or from a module, removes it, and the order of statements never matters (DESIGN-RULES: statement order). `Lt8641` says `.bias ?~ .vin.neg` and states `BIAS`'s 25 V in its pin row (`abs_max 25V`, ADR 0124), where `.bias_voltage &= 0V` was: the solver now judges the net the pin ends up on, and a pin tied only inside its part is skipped as every such pin is (ADR 0095).

**Scope.** Exactly two pins, `.a ?~ .b`; not in a chain, not between interfaces or parts, not in a board, module, generic or system (they say `~`). A default join is a statement of a part's definition and nothing a board can write, since a board's own joins already are the final word. Two defaults on one pin both apply; a join of the pin removes both.

**Rejected.** *The IC states `BIAS` as a member that chooses its source (`ground` or `output`), and `Buck` joins it.* `BuckIc` would grow a member every IC must carry (ADR 0120 turned down optional members on the bound), and "output" is not a thing the part can name. *Two providers in std, one with `BIAS` on ground and one with it left open.* A copy of the part with one line fewer, which is the duplication the issue complains about, and a board's choice of IC would become a choice of wiring. *Leave it and let a board write its own part.* The copy drifts, as the issue says. *Let a board's `~` replace any `~` of the part.* A board could rewire a part's application circuit, which ADR 0116 and ADR 0120 refuse; only a join the part marks as a default gives way. *A named default (`.bias ?~ .vin.neg` read as "the net `GND`")*: a part names no net (ADR 0005).

**Cost.** One more operator, in the lexer, parser, printer and the editor's highlighter. A part now has joins that depend on its use, so a reader of a part must look for a `?~` before saying where a pin is; the net lists and the schematic show the result as for any join. `Lt8641` no longer states `.bias_voltage` and `.bias_abs_max`, which nothing else read; a board that wrote them (none does) breaks at that line. `BIAS` on the output is judged only at 25 V, as the part states no figure for the current `BIAS` draws from the output.

**Left open.** Whether a board should be able to leave a defaulted pin unjoined altogether (it can only replace the join). Whether another IC's choice (an enable divider, a mode pin) should use `?~` where it exists; none does yet. `SYNC/MODE` needed nothing: the part leaves it open, and a board that grounds it joins it and states the quiescent figure (ADR 0096).
