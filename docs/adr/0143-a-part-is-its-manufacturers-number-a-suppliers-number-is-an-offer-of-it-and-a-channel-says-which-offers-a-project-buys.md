# A part is its manufacturer's number, a supplier's number is an offer of it, and a channel says which offers a project buys

Meets tests 1 (the catalog files, the pick lock and the `.lcsc` narrowing of a source are formats and syntax that projects write) and 3 (a column for each supplier is the obvious alternative). The owner agreed the design in conversation on 2026-11-20 (the hand pick by `.mpn` only, the offers in a file for each supplier, and this ADR first); it stays `proposed` until the owner has read the text. Issue #1462 and the issues it lists carry the stages, and `docs/MIGRATING.md` the break when it lands.

**The problem.** A catalog row names its part by an LCSC number and carries JLCPCB's library class, `.lcsc &=` is a narrowing in the language (ADR 0122), the pick lock records the row, and the rule that resolves a generic is "Basic, then Preferred, then Extended" in code. That is one supplier in the identity of every part. A board that is bought from Digi-Key and sent to the board house (a consigned part, ADR 0084), or soldered by hand, has no place in it, and each supplier added the old way would add a column, a field of `PartKey` and a rule to the language and the solver. ADR 0047 started to take the supplier's word out of the row (price, stock, date); this takes out the rest.

**Decision.**

1. A part is identified by its `manufacturer` and `mpn`, written as the datasheet spells them. A catalog row says what the part is: those two, its values, its package and its `source`. It holds no supplier's number, no supplier's class and no price or stock. The same manufacturer and `mpn` twice is an error, as the same `lcsc` or `mpn` is now (ADR 0022).
2. What a supplier says about a part that a person can record is an **offer**: a row of `offers/<supplier>.csv` with `manufacturer`, `mpn`, `sku`, `class`, `source`, `checked` and `notes`. The file of a supplier holds its numbers, once: JLCPCB's parts library uses LCSC's numbers, so there is no `jlcpcb.csv`, and the `class` of the `lcsc` file is the sorting JLCPCB gives the part (Basic, Extended, Preferred), empty when its library does not list it (owner, 2026-11-20). The standard files are `data/offers/<supplier>.csv`, embedded in the binary; a project's own are beside its catalogs and add rows after the standard ones. The sourcing rule of the catalogs holds: an offer has a `source`, no number comes from memory, and the loader refuses an offer with none. A part with no offer from a supplier cannot be bought there, and a message says so by name.
3. A **quote**, the price ladder and the stock, is still a supply provider's answer, cached (ADR 0047, unchanged), and is asked for by an offer's `sku`. A new supplier is a new offers file and a new provider. The row format, `PartKey` and the language do not change.
4. A **supply channel** says where a project buys and how a part is priced: `jlcpcb-assembly`, `lcsc-retail`, `digikey-retail` and the like. A channel reads the file of its supplier: `jlcpcb-assembly` takes only the `lcsc` offers with a `class`, `lcsc-retail` all of them. The `[supply]` table of the manifest lists the channels in order, and a part may name its own. The first channel with an offer for a part buys it. A channel has its own rule for choosing among the rows that fit: JLCPCB's Basic, Preferred, Extended moves from the code into that channel, and another channel goes by stock, then price. The channel is not `.assembly` (who places the part, ADRs 0024 and 0084); the two are independent.
5. A hand pick in a source is by `.mpn`, with `.manufacturer` when two parts share a number. `.lcsc &= "C307331"` is an error whose message gives the `.mpn` narrowing to write, as ADR 0122 did for the picks file; a supplier number maps to one `mpn`, so the migration is mechanical.
6. The pick lock (`version = 2`) records the `manufacturer` and `mpn` of each part, and for each channel of the project the `sku` it resolved to.

This replaces the parts of ADRs 0022, 0047, 0100 and 0122 that make `lcsc` the identity of a part. They stay accepted for the rest, and the file order of a catalog is still the solver's policy.

**Rejected.** A column for each supplier on the row (`digikey`, `mouser`): every supplier would be a format change and a field of the language, and the number of one supplier would stay the identity. A provider that finds a supplier's number from the `mpn` at the time of the lookup and records nothing: a build could not be reproduced offline, a lock could not hold a number, and a packaging (reel or cut tape, two numbers of one part) could not be pinned. One `offers.csv` for all suppliers: the evidence and the review of each supplier would mix, and a project's private supplier would edit a shared file. `.lcsc` kept as a field that reads through the offers: it keeps a supplier's name in the language.

**The cost.** A break (`!`). Every `.lcsc &=` in a source, the `lcsc` and `library` columns of the standard and the projects' catalogs, every committed lock and design file, and the editor's and the bill of materials' use of `lcsc` change. A command moves a project's columns into an offers file, and `docs/MIGRATING.md` says how. The `catalog refresh` check becomes a check of the offers of a supplier that has a record to read.

**Open.** A supplier that spells a manufacturer another way (the offer's `manufacturer` must equal the row's: whether a table of aliases is needed). Whether the two packagings of a part are two offers, and how a pick names one. The order of one order across channels, and the bill of materials each channel's uploader reads (issues for each). Currency is not converted, and a real provider is built only after a person has read the supplier's terms of use (issue #10, ADR 0047).
