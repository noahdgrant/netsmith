## The standard library

The library is `.nsm` source in `std/`. It states only what is always true of a part. Import a definition by its full path, for example `import std::parts::passive::Resistor`.

Parts are numbered by their letters in the order of their paths in the board (a module's parts by their full paths, with the board's own; see Designators), and every kind states its own letters in its definition (`.designator &= "R"` in `Resistor`): `R` resistor, `C` capacitor, `L` inductor, `FB` ferrite bead, `D` LED, diode, Schottky, Zener and TVS, `Q` MOSFET, `Y` crystal, `F` fuse, `RT` thermistor (IEEE 315), `SW` switch, `J` header, socket, coax, USB-C and programming pads, `TP` test point, `H` mounting hole and plated hole, `FID` fiducial, `LBL` label, `U` linear regulator and load switch (ADR 0046; see Designators).

### Parts

`Resistor`, `Capacitor`, `CapacitorBank`, `Supercap`, `Cell`, `Led`, `Inductor`, `FerriteBead`, `Crystal`, `Fuse`, `NtcThermistor`, `Diode`, `Schottky`, `Zener`, `Tvs`, `TvsBidirectional`, `NMosfet`, `PMosfet`, `LinearRegulator` and `LevelTranslator` are written `generic`, not `part` (ADR 0068, stage 2): each is its own first provider, so a design uses it as it always did, and a project can add a provider of it (see _The standard passives are generics_). A row that says "A generic" is a generic with no pads, which only a provider stands for (`Connector`, the USB-C receptacles, `BuckIc`, `LoadSwitch`, `Charger`, `TwoLayer`). Every other definition in this table is a plain `part` or a `module`.

| `std::parts::passive::CapacitorBank` | Identical capacitors in parallel, whose number the solver chooses or a module passes in (ADR 0123): the fields of `Capacitor` for one capacitor, and `.count`, a whole number (unset: the solver chooses from 1 to 8; a range limits it), `.total_capacitance` and `.total_effective_capacitance`, the count times the marked and the bias-derated value. ### Interfaces

| Definition | What it is |
|---|---|
| `std::sources::Rail` | A DC rail: pins `pos` and `neg`, a `.voltage`, a `.current` (the draw), a `.peak` (the highest voltage, transients included), an optional `.reverse_peak` (the highest the wrong way round, as a positive number, ADR 0091), the optional `.peak_current` (the most its own load draws at an instant, which the DC analysis does not read) and `.max_peak_current` (the most its source gives at an instant, shared by `~`), with `.peak_current <= .max_peak_current`, the optional `.burst` and `.duty` of a load that draws its peak in bursts (how long one lasts and the fraction of the time it bursts, its own and not shared by `~`; the source gives the peak and the other loads at once, and the capacitance across the rail holds what it cannot, issue #1279), with `max(.current) >= max(.peak_current) * max(.duty)`, the optional `.voltage_floor` (the least voltage the rail may fall to while its regulator is in dropout, below `min(.voltage)`, shared by `~`: the dropout check and the loads' supply ranges are judged from it, issue #1301), the optional `.charge_time_limit` (the longest a charge of the storage on the rail may take, a field of the connection between a charger and a cell, shared by `~`, read by the charge-time check of issue #1264), a `.min_track` (the narrowest track of its nets), a `.min_clearance` (the least space between its nets' copper and other copper), and `.isolated` (`"none"`, or what keeps its ground apart from the board's other grounds: an isolated ground, see Systems and ports). |
| `std::sources::Cell` | A rechargeable cell (issue #1262): the rail `terminals` (pins `+` and `-`), which it gives (`dc source`), and from its row `.chemistry` (a `Chemistry`), `.capacity` (a charge, in coulombs: 1 mAh is 3.6 C), `.internal_resistance`, `.charge_temperature` and `.discharge_temperature` (the ranges it may be charged and discharged in), `.float_voltage` (the range it is charged to) and `.max_charge_current`. A `Charger` joined to it is checked against it. Catalog `cell`, which has no row yet. See A rechargeable cell and its charger. |
| `std::sources::Reverse`, `std::sources::ReversePath` | What a part between two rails does with current from its output back to its input (issue #1258), the type of the `.reverse` of `LinearRegulator`, `BuckIc` and `LoadSwitch`: `.path` (`ReversePath::BodyDiode`, the default, or `ReversePath::Blocking`), `.forward_voltage` (the body diode's drop, offered at `0.4V..1.2V`, wide on purpose) and the optional `.max_current` (the most it passes backwards). A board's `scenario` reads it. Chapter _Standard parts_, "Reverse current and backup sources". |
| `std::sources::TrackImpedance`, `std::sources::PairImpedance` | The impedance a design states for tracks (issue #1007): `TrackImpedance` has the pin `.line` and `.impedance` (one net, single-ended); `PairImpedance` has the pins `.p` and `.n`, `.impedance` (the differential impedance), `.spacing` (the space between the two tracks, edge to edge) and `.skew` (the most the two lengths may differ by, offered at `0mm..0.1mm`, issue #1234). | `std::sources::DiffPair` | Two nets that are a diff pair with no impedance stated (issue #1193): the pins `.p` and `.n`, joined with `~` to the two nets, and `.skew`, the most their lengths may differ by (offered at `0mm..0.1mm`, issue #1234). Chapter _The shape of a board_, "Diff pairs". |
| `std::sources::LengthGroup` | Nets matched in length, a parallel bus or a byte lane (issues #1234, #1252): `.lines`, a list of pins (`[Pin]`) each joined with `~` to a net of the group, as many as the indices the board joins, and `.tolerance`, the most the longest and the shortest net may differ by (the top of its range; no default). The router lengthens the shorter nets towards the longest, and `board nets` and `board drc` list the group's spread. Chapter _The shape of a board_, "Diff pairs". |
| `std::checks::HoldUp` | That a storage element holds a rail up after its supply goes (issue #1255): the pins `.storage` and `.rail`, joined with `~` to the storage's positive terminal and the rail's `pos`, and the load's `.average_power`, `.peak_current` and `.duration`. The solver walks the chain between them through the parts' `dc` statements (diode drops, resistances, a linear regulator's dropout, a switching regulator's least input and efficiency), adds the peak across the storage's ESR, and compares the energy the storage gives down to that voltage with what the load takes, at the parts' figures and at each named corner. Chapter _Load current_, "Holding a rail up". |
| `std::parts::regulator::BuckIc` | A generic, what a buck converter IC says about itself: its supply `vin`, the switching node `sw` (a rail the IC supplies), the feedback pin `fb`, `vref`, `fsw`, the input range, `max_output_current`, `efficiency`, `min_inductance`, the optional `min_inductor_saturation`, `max_inductor_dcr` and `high_side_resistance` (the high-side switch's on-resistance, for an IC that runs at 100 % duty cycle in dropout, issue #1259), and the capacitance asked for on each side.
