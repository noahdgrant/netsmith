# The plan for cleaning up the remaining ADRs

A working document. The cleanup batches delete it when the last one lands. It does not edit any ADR, the guide, std or code. It was written on `main` at `f93a003`, after the pilot (#311) cut ADR 0076 and ADRs 0085 to 0096.

The rules are in `docs/adr/README.md`. In this file the three tests are **T1** (hard to reverse), **T2** (would surprise a newcomer) and **T3** (rejects an obvious alternative). Classification decides no open choice: the language is the owner's (`AGENTS.md`).

## What the count is

`docs/adr/` has 97 numbered ADRs. The owner counted "about 85" that break a rule. Today:

| Group | ADRs | What breaks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Done by the pilot or already short | 0076, 0085 to 0097 (14) | nothing |
| On `docs/adr/.oversize` | 51 | size, the status word, or both |
| Pass `scripts/adr-check` | 32 | nothing the script sees; 17 of them still have notes after the status word (see the last section) |

So 51 need a cut or a fold, and 17 more need one line trimmed. The "about 85" figure is not what the repository holds; 51 is, by `grep -vc '^#' docs/adr/.oversize`. The 51 are classified below.

## How this was read, and what was not

I read each of the 51 for its status line in full, its headings, its opening paragraphs, and its "decision", "left open", "not decided here", "choices for the owner", "questions" or "what to look at" sections, and I grepped the rest. The six largest (0045, 0064, 0065, 0068, 0030, 0040; 76 to 93 KB or 30 to 40 KB) were read by headings and by their decision and open-question sections, not line by line. A cleanup batch must read its ADRs whole before it cuts one; this plan says where to look, not what every line holds.

Every file named below exists (checked with `test -e`). Every guide section named exists as a heading in `docs/LANGUAGE.md` (checked with grep). "Cites" is the number of files in `crates`, `std` and `scripts` that match `ADR ?00NN`, as `grep -rlE "ADR ?00NN" crates std scripts | wc -l` counts them. The guide and `GLOSSARY.md` cite ADRs too, and a stub keeps every number, so a high count asks for care in the stub's pointer and not for a keep.

I did not run any build or test. "Where it lives" is by reading: I checked that the guide section or doc comment is about the same rule, not that it carries every sentence of the ADR. Each batch confirms that before it folds.

Of the 51, **24** rows say **owner to read**: they carry a language-design choice or a status the owner has not confirmed (the full question is in the last column of the row, and 11 of the 24 are asked again in the list at the end). A fold or a keep marked so is a classification only: the owner decides the choice, and a fold must carry the choice word for word into the doc comment or the issue, as the pilot did.
