# Power Manifold charger module

A port of the open **Power Manifold charger module** (a USB-C Power Delivery "charger blade") to
netsmith, the fourth showcase board of epic #308 (after the RP2350A Minimal, ORBIT and the
Easyduino ESP32-S3), and the one that stresses the solver's DC analysis hardest: a buck-boost
converter with a feedback network and an inductor, a linear regulator, comparator thresholds and
a USB-C Power Delivery front end. **This is the first stage: the project, the licence, the
author's files and the survey.** `src/main.nsm` is a placeholder that builds, not the charger's
circuit; the parts, the board, its proof and the physical file are the stages that follow
(issue #417).

## Source

- The design: <https://github.com/mikesmitty/power-manifold>, folder `hardware/charger-module/`,
  read at commit `dd24000a9ce0cac567da674c473ded4cff34bdbc` (committed 2026-11-06). KiCad 10
  files: the board has file format version 20260206 and the schematic 20260306
  (`generator_version "10.0"`); both carry the title block date 2026-09-02 and revision
  **v0.16.2** (`reference/CHANGELOG.md` is the module's own changelog, whose latest entry is
  0.16.2, 2026-10-14).
- What it is: a charger blade of a six-port managed USB-PD power supply (the repository's
  `docs/src/content/docs/developers/architecture.md`). It plugs into a backplane through a PCIe
  x1 card-edge connector and is fed 24 V from the slot (`VIN`, behind an 8 A fuse) and 5 V for its
  logic. An STM32G071C8T6 runs ST's USB-PD stack over a TPS55288 buck-boost converter (feedback
  from the MCU's DAC, a 5.6 uH inductor) and a TCPP02-M18 port protector (CC overvoltage and ESD
  protection, VCONN switch, the VBUS switch's gate driver, over-current trip); a TLV7022 dual
  comparator opens the port's VBUS switch (two BSC059N04LS6 in series) above a DAC-set or fixed
  voltage whatever the firmware does. The blade is read and configured over I2C (address 0x3A)
  through the card edge. A LP2985-3.3 makes the MCU's 3.3 V from the slot's 5 V. One schematic
  sheet (`charger-module.kicad_sch`, no hierarchy: its thirteen groups are text boxes), **89 parts
  and a 90th footprint that is only a marker** (`HOT_SURFACE_SMALL`, no pad), four copper layers
  (F.Cu, In1.Cu, In2.Cu, B.Cu), 92.8 x 24 mm body plus the card-edge tab.
- Licence: hardware **CERN-OHL-P-2.0** (permissive). The repository's `README.md` says
  "Copyright 2024-2026 Michael Smith" and lists "Hardware designs in `hardware/`: CERN-OHL-P-2.0
  (`LICENSE-hardware`)"; the firmware is MIT and the documentation CC BY 4.0. `LICENSE` here is
  `LICENSE-hardware` byte for byte, and stays with every copy of anything in this folder that
  comes from their design (`reference/`). The licence asks for the Notices to be kept (section
  3.1) and, for a modified copy that is conveyed, a Notice that it was modified, with the date
  and a short description (3.3 b): **modification notice, 2026-11-11: netsmith's files in this
  folder are not Covered Source copied from the design; `reference/placements.toml` is data
  derived from the board file (positions, pads and nets written out as a table), and the port
  that follows is written anew in netsmith's language.**
- **Third-party material in the board file.** The author's `hardware/THIRD-PARTY.md` says that
  the footprints of `libraries/Local Library.pretty/` that are copies of KiCad's library
  (`C_0402_1005Metric`, `R_0402_1005Metric`, `BUS_PCIexpress_x1`, `SOT-23-6`, `L_Sunlord_MWSA0603S`

## Goal

1. **The same nets.** The schematic of the port and the charger module's design are the same
   circuit: a test proves it by comparing the wiring alone, as the earlier ports' do.
2. **A matching PCB where it matters.** A board made by `netsmith fab` is interchangeable with
   theirs in these ways, and the parity checker (with tolerances) fails when one is off:
   - the outline of the card: a 92.8 x 24 mm body and the PCIe x1 card-edge tab with its key
     slot, 101.2 x 24 mm in all (the board file draws the body with four lines and the tab inside
     the footprint J3);
   - the card-edge connector (J3, fingers on the back face, every finger on its signal), the
     USB-C receptacle (J4) and the Tag-Connect programming pads (J2).

   The other parts may be anywhere, and the routing will differ; the checker reports them.

## What is here

  placeholder for now) and the board's pick lock (`netsmith lock --update` refreshes it; CI
  checks it).
- `LICENSE`: the CERN-OHL-P-2.0 text, as the author ships it.
- `reference/charger-module.kicad_pcb`, `charger-module.kicad_sch`, `CHANGELOG.md`: their board
  and schematic files and the module's changelog, unchanged (1.6 MB, 450 kB, 12 kB).
- `reference/must_match.toml`: which footprints a copy must reproduce (J2, J3 and J4).
- `reference/placements.toml`: the facts the checker needs, read from the board file: the

  `cargo run -p netsmith --example reference_table -- --must-match reference/must_match.toml reference/charger-module.kicad_pcb > reference/placements.toml`
  (run from this folder); a test checks that the file is what the program prints.

## Run it

From this folder:

```sh
netsmith build    # the schematic, in out/ (a placeholder until the board stage)
```

The checks are in `crates/netsmith/tests/power_manifold.rs`.

## Survey

Read from the board file (`reference/charger-module.kicad_pcb`: every footprint's value, `LCSC`
property, footprint name and place; every pad's net). The schematic is one sheet with no
sub-sheets. Reference designators are theirs.

44 distinct LCSC numbers on 91 footprints. 14 are in the standard catalogs already (the common
resistors and ceramic capacitors and the green LED); 30 are not and each needs a recorded source
before it enters a catalog (`data/jlcpcb/README.md`).

### The nets (85, 26 of them one-pin)

The supplies: `GND` (92 pads), `+5V` (the slot's logic supply, to U2, U5, the connector and the
LED), `+3.3V` (U2's output: the MCU, the I2C pull-ups, the comparator and the thermistor
dividers), `/VIN_PREFUSE` (the slot's A2 to A7) behind the 8 A fuse F1 to `/VIN`, then L1/C-filter
to `/EMI_VIN` (the converter's input), `/SW1` and `/SW2` (the converter's switch nodes, L2
between), `/VOUT_CONV` (its output, behind the shunt R28 to `/VOUT`), `/VBUS_SRC` and `/VBUSf`
(the two series MOSFETs of the port switch, and the shunt R27) and `/VBUS` (the receptacle).
The signals: I2C (`/SCL`, `/SDA` from the slot and `/USB_SCL`, `/USB_SDA` to the converter and
the protector), `/EN`, `/~{ALERT}`, `/~{RST}`, `/SWDIO`, `/SWCLK`, `/USART_TX`, `/USART_RX`, the
CC lines (`/CC1`, `/CC2` to U5, `/UCPD_CC1`, `/UCPD_CC2` to the MCU), `/TCPP_EN`, `/~{FLG}`,
`/CONVERTER_FLT`, the analog sense lines (`/VBUS_SENSE`, `/VOUT_SENSE`, `/IANA`, `/NTC_CONV`,
`/NTC_PLUG`) and the comparator's references (`/OVP_REF`, `/OVP_CEIL`). The generated names are
`Net-(U3-...)` for the converter's feedback, compensation, frequency and current-limit pins.
The pins left open (26 nets of one pad): 18 of the MCU's, the converter's CDC, four of USBLC6's
(I/O1 pin 6, I/O2 pins 3 and 4 and VBUS pin 5), the receptacle's SBU1 and SBU2 and the programming
connector's pin 6.

### What the language has and lacks (to be settled in the next stages)

- **Has:** resistor, capacitor, LED, inductor, ferrite bead, fuse, Schottky, TVS, N-channel
  MOSFET, linear regulator, the standard catalogs for 0402 to 1210 passives, a USB-C sink
  (`UsbCSink`) with a receptacle provider, a programming pads connector (`ProgrammingPads6`, six pads
  in a footprint, the generic form of a Tag-Connect cable's), a test point.
- **Lacks:** a thermistor kind, a buck-boost or a boost converter interface (the standard
  `Buck` is the only converter), a comparator, a USB-PD controller or port protector, a
  microcontroller with a pin table of this size, a PCIe card-edge connector, an electrolytic
  capacitor row with the ESR column the converter's output capacitor needs.
- **Risks, from the research of the issue:** the 80-part guide size; custom footprints (the
  GT-USB-7004 receptacle, VQFN-HR-26, QFN-18 with a 2.2 mm pad, the PCIe fingers); a long thin
  outline; a board designed for a 24 V backplane (its input is 24 V and the converter's output can
  reach 21 V).

Gaps found while porting are recorded in the stage that finds them.
