# Power Manifold charger module

A port of the open **Power Manifold charger module** (a USB-C Power Delivery "charger blade") to
netsmith, the fourth showcase board of epic #308 (after the RP2350A Minimal, ORBIT and the
Easyduino ESP32-S3), and the one that stresses the solver's DC analysis hardest: a buck-boost
converter with a feedback network and an inductor, a linear regulator, comparator thresholds and
a USB-C Power Delivery front end. **The schematic is done**: `src/main.nsm` is the board
`PowerManifoldCharger`, the same circuit as the design's, with its nets proved equal by a test
(below). **The physical file and the parity gate are done too**: `netsmith fab` writes a four-layer board
with the original's card-edge connector, USB-C receptacle and programming pads where theirs are
(see "The PCB" below), and the outline is the one known difference.

## Source

- The design: <https://github.com/mikesmitty/power-manifold>, folder `hardware/charger-module/`,
  read at commit `dd24000a9ce0cac567da674c473ded4cff34bdbc` (committed 2026-11-06). KiCad 10
  files: the board has file format version 20260206 and the schematic 20260306
  (`generator_version "10.0"`); both carry the title block date 2026-09-02 and revision
  **v0.16.2** (`reference/CHANGELOG.md` is the module's own changelog, whose latest entry is
  0.16.2, 2026-10-14).
- What it is: a charger blade of a six-port managed USB-PD power supply (the repository's
  `docs/src/content/docs/developers/architecture.md`). It plugs into a backplane through a PCIe
  x1 card-edge connector and is fed 24 V from the slot (`VIN`, behind an 8 A fuse) and 5 V for its
  logic. An STM32G071C8T6 runs ST's USB-PD stack over a TPS55288 buck-boost converter (feedback
  from the MCU's DAC, a 5.6 uH inductor) and a TCPP02-M18 port protector (CC overvoltage and ESD
  protection, VCONN switch, the VBUS switch's gate driver, over-current trip); a TLV7022 dual
  comparator opens the port's VBUS switch (two BSC059N04LS6 in series) above a DAC-set or fixed
  voltage whatever the firmware does. The blade is read and configured over I2C (address 0x3A)
  through the card edge. A LP2985-3.3 makes the MCU's 3.3 V from the slot's 5 V. One schematic
  sheet (`charger-module.kicad_sch`, no hierarchy: its thirteen groups are text boxes), **89 parts
  and a 90th footprint that is only a marker** (`HOT_SURFACE_SMALL`, no pad), four copper layers
  (F.Cu, In1.Cu, In2.Cu, B.Cu), 92.8 x 24 mm body plus the card-edge tab.

## Goal

1. **The same nets.** The schematic of the port and the charger module's design are the same
   circuit: a test proves it by comparing the wiring alone, as the earlier ports' do.
2. **A matching PCB where it matters.** A board made by `netsmith fab` is interchangeable with
   theirs in these ways, and the parity checker (with tolerances) fails when one is off:
   - the card-edge connector (J3: 36 fingers, every finger on its signal, pad for pad), the
     USB-C receptacle (J4) and the Tag-Connect programming pads (J2): the same positions,
     rotations, pads and holes, with each pad on the same net;
   - the size of the card: 101.2 x 24 mm (a 92.8 x 24 mm body and the PCIe x1 tab).

   **Not matched, and the one known failure of the gate:** the outline's shape. The board file
   draws the body with four lines and the tab and its key slot (a 1.9 mm slot with a round end,
   chamfered tab corners) with 11 lines and an arc inside the footprint J3: 14 lines and one
   arc. The language's `Outline` is a rounded rectangle and has no notch, so the port's is a
   rectangle of the same size (see "The PCB"). The other parts may be anywhere, and the routing
   will differ; the checker reports them.

- `reference/parts.toml`: the parts file of the port, the original's designator of each of the

  (run from this folder); a test checks that the file is what the program prints.

## Run it

From this folder:

```sh
netsmith build    # the schematic, in out/ (no KiCad install needed)
```

The checks are in `crates/netsmith/tests/power_manifold.rs` (the board and the table),
`power_manifold_ics.rs` and `power_manifold_parts.rs` (the parts).

## The PCB

`src/main.physical.nsm` is the physical file: the outline and the place, rotation and face of the
three parts that must match, each number read from `reference/placements.toml` and written from
the board's own corner (the upper-left corner of the outline's bounding box: x 54.8, y 26 in
their file). There is no layout file: the placer's first answer (seed 0) places the other 86 parts
and routes about 240 of the 257 connections in the gate's 20 s (the gate allows an INCOMPLETE board: the
parts' places do not depend on the router). The board is dense (89 parts on 101.2 x 24 mm); seeds 3 to 14, which once stopped with the
courtyards of the receptacle (`usb`) and the capacitor `vbus_2u2` 0.005 mm in each other (the
placer measured the receptacle, pinned at x 97.405 mm, on its 0.05 mm grid), place too (#457). `netsmith fab` writes a four-layer JLCPCB
board (`.top.net`, `.bottom.net` and `.inner1.net` ground, `.inner2.net` +5 V).
**The router does not finish this board**: with its default effort (4 tries, 5 min in a debug
build) 4 of the 256 connections are left, so `fab` exits 1 with `INCOMPLETE`, writes the board
and the Gerbers marked incomplete, and says they are not ready to order. This is a finding about
the router (89 parts on a board 24 mm high, and the placer's layout is the only one it gets), and `scripts/check-no-kicad.sh` lists the example in `no_fab` for
it; the gate runs `fab` with a 20 s timeout and allows `INCOMPLETE`.

- **The outline** is a 101.2 x 24 mm rectangle (`.outline: Outline` in `case`). Their file
  draws it with four lines on the board for the body (x 63.2 to 156, y 26 to 50, the left side
  only between y 46.3 and 50) and 11 lines and an arc in the footprint J3 for the tab (x 54.8 to
  63.2) with a keying slot 1.9 mm wide and 7 mm long that opens to the card's end. **The port draws
  none of the notch or the chamfers**: that is a gap of the language (no outline but a rounded
  rectangle) and the gate's two known failures, `outline corner radius` (0 against their arc's
  0.95 mm) and `outline elements` (4 lines against 14 lines and an arc). A board made from this
  file has a card tab that is square at its end and has no key slot: **do not order it for a
  backplane without cutting the slot**. The outline's size, 101.2 x 24 mm, matches.
- **J3, the edge connector.** The 36 fingers are `connect` pads (an edge-connector pad: copper
  on one face; KiCad's `connect`): side A is on the front face and side B on the back in the
  original, which draws the footprint on the back face (`B.Cu`). `fab` writes a back-face
  footprint with every x negated, so the project's footprint
  (`libs/power_manifold_must_match.pretty/PCIe_x1_Edge_Fingers`) is drawn as seen from the back
  (x negated), and equals the reference when placed on the bottom face, rotated 90 degrees: the
  origin is 3.45 mm from the card's end and 19.65 mm from its top edge. Its courtyard (the pads
  and 0.1 mm, on both faces) is what the physical file measures, so `.slot.place.y` is the
  body's centre, 9.5 mm from the origin along the card. The footprint draws no `Edge.Cuts`.
- **J4, the receptacle,** on the right edge, rotated 90 degrees, top face; its pegs are the
  gate's two 0.6 mm holes, which the receptacle's footprint draws. Its D+ and D- are joined to
  each other and to U6's pin 1 in the design (a dedicated charging port): the net `/USB_DATA` is
  one net of five pads, and the gate checks every pad's net.
- **J2, the programming pads,** a project footprint of the Tag-Connect TC2030-IDC-NL
  (`TC2030-NL-connect` of `ProgrammingPads6`): the standard footprint's pads and three 0.99 mm
  holes, with the pad type `connect` that the original's has (the parity rule compares a pad's
  type; the standard one's pads are `smd`).

### Run the parity check

```sh
netsmith fab --locked          # from this folder; writes fab/PowerManifoldCharger/
netsmith parity fab/PowerManifoldCharger/PowerManifoldCharger.kicad_pcb \
    --table reference/placements.toml --parts reference/parts.toml \
    --svg reference/parity-overlay.svg
```

The gate is `crates/netsmith/tests/power_manifold_parity.rs` (`NETSMITH_NO_KICAD=1`,
`fab --locked --route-timeout 20`, every must-match item `ok` but the two known failures of the
outline, which it names, and the committed overlay equal to the one the board gives).

**Result: `must-match: 144 ok, 2 failed`** (the two are the outline's corner radius and
elements). Per item: the outline's size 1 ok, the five holes (J2's three and J4's two, 0.00 mm
off), J2 (position, rotation, layer, 6 numbered pads and 3 holes, and each pad's net), J3 (the
position, rotation and layer and each of the 36 fingers' geometry and net) and J4 (position,
rotation, layer, 16 contacts, 4 shield legs and 2 pegs, and each pad's net). Every difference
is 0.000 mm. **Reported, not gated:** every other part's place and turn.

## Same nets

`crates/netsmith/tests/power_manifold.rs`, module `board` (no KiCad needed; the ERC test skips
where there is no `kicad-cli`). The reference's connectivity is read from the committed
`reference/charger-module.kicad_pcb` with `Netlist::from_kicad_pcb` (netsmith's own reader), and
the board's from the built design. `PARTS` pairs the 89 parts by member path with the design's
designator (the members are named for what the part does: `.mcu` is U1, `.converter` U3,
`.protector` U5, `.q_port_a` Q3, `.sda_pullup` R3 ...).

- `every_pad_has_the_same_pads_on_its_net_as_in_the_charger_board`: for every pad of every part the
  set of the other pads on its net is the same on both sides. Passes: 85 nets less the 26 of one
  pad. Every pin is joined with `~` to a **named pin** (`.mcu.pb6 ~ ...`), never to a request, and
  every two-pin part has its pad 1 where the design has it (`.a` is pad 1: no pair is exchanged).
  The control `the_comparison_sees_a_pad_on_the_wrong_net` puts U1 pad 13 on the net of pad 14 and
  requires the comparison to name `U1.13`.
- `netsmith_compare_says_the_nets_are_the_same`: `netsmith-netlist::compare` with every value and
  footprint blanked and the designators paired by the table: `match`.
- `the_resistors_and_capacitors_have_the_charger_boards_values_and_packages`: all 27 resistors and
  38 capacitors have the value and the package of the reference's footprint.
- `the_parts_have_the_lcsc_numbers_of_the_charger_boards_footprints`: 84 parts carry an `LCSC`
  property in the board file; every one is picked as that number, 19 ceramics of them through the
  picks file (below).
- `the_open_pins_are_the_ones_the_reference_leaves_open`: the pads on the board's `unconnected-(...)`
  nets (26) are the pads that no net of the design names.
- `the_optimizer_cannot_move_a_pin` (no request, no pin file entry) and
  `the_electrical_rules_check_of_kicad_passes_on_the_schematic` (KiCad's ERC: no finding;
  skipped where `kicad-cli` is not found).

### Differences from the reference (all of them)

- **U6's pins 1 and 6.** The device joins pads 1 and 6 (I/O1) inside, and the design uses pad 1
  and leaves pad 6 open, so they are two pins of the part here (`.io1` and `.io1_b`) and pad 6 is
  one of the 26 open pads. The part is UMW's USBLC6-2SC6 (what LCSC and JLCPCB sell as C2687116),
  not ST's.
- **`/USB_DATA` is one net of five pads**: J4's D+ (A6, B6) and D- (A7, B7) and U6 pin 1 are
  joined, as the design has it (a dedicated charging port shorts D+ and D-), with `.dp` and `.dm`
  joined on the board. The receptacle's part provides `UsbCSourceReceptacle` (and `Connector`):
  this port is a source that the board's own converter drives, so VBUS is what the device on the
  cable draws (`.vbus.current >= 0A`), never a second source on the net. The design states no
  limit for the port's current, so that bound is one of the notes below.
- **Part numbers.** The design states no tolerance or voltage, and the solver picks the first
  catalog row that fits the value, the package and the rail. For a 100 nF 0402 it would pick
  C1525 (16 V, Basic) where the board has C307331 (50 V), and for 1 uF, 4.7 uF, 2.2 uF and 10 uF
  parts the board's number is another row too. `src/main.picks.toml` holds those 19 ceramics to
  the board's numbers (#462), and the solver judges each by the design as it judges any row. The two Sunlord
  inductors, the shunts and every other part have the board's number. U2 is C89826, which is the
  LP2985**A**-33DBVR (1 % grade), where the board's value field says "LP2985-3.3"; C46550469
  (C39) is an 8 x 9 mm part on the 8 x 10 mm footprint the board draws.
- **Net names.** The design's nets are named as KiCad names them (`/SW1`, `/VBUS_GATE`); the port
  names the rails and generates the others from the pin they join (`net-boot1_cap.a`). The tests
  pair pads, never names.
- **Pours.** The board states the ground pour on both outer layers and the first inner layer and
  the 5 V pour on the second inner layer; the design has a 3.3 V pour on its second inner layer
  too (a layer here has one pour net), and pours of `/VIN`, `/SW1`, `/VOUT_CONV` and others on
  the outer layers, which the port does not state.
- **The marker.** `HOT_SURFACE_SMALL` (a graphic on the back face, `REF**`, no pad) is not a part.
- **What the design has that this does not:** the 3D models and every drawing of its silkscreen.

## What the solver checks

- **Checked:** the microcontroller's supply window (1.7 V to 3.6 V) and the comparator's (1.6 V to
  6.5 V) against the 3.3 V rail the LP2985 makes, the protector's (2.7 V to 5.5 V) against the slot's
  5 V rail, the LP2985's input (from the slot's 5 V) and the tolerance of every rail the board states.
  The 24 V input is stated as 20.4 V to 28.8 V (24 V, +20 %/-15 %: the design's architecture notes),
  and the converter's output as the 5 V to 21 V of a USB-PD contract, an **ideal source of the
  board's own statement**, since no part models the converter.
- **Not evaluated (74 notes):** `usb.vbus.current >= 0A`, since the port's current is not stated; the TPS55288 is a part with no `dc` statement and the language has
  no buck-boost generic (only `Buck`), so its input window (`converter.vin.voltage >= 2.7V`), the
  current of the input, the output and the shunts, the fuse, the inductors and the capacitors
  that hang on those rails are listed as not evaluated; the MOSFETs' gate drive (`q_*.gate_source_voltage
  >= q_*.gate_threshold`: the gates are driven by pins that state no level); the LP2985's dropout and
  power (nothing states what the 3.3 V rail draws: the microcontroller states no current); the
  divider resistors; and every capacitor's bias on the rails behind the converter. The test
  `the_power_path_is_checked_as_far_as_the_parts_state_and_the_rest_is_listed` pins a few of
  them: when a part states what is missing, it fails and this paragraph is to be rewritten.
- **Gaps this port found (for the owner):**
  - there is no generic for a buck-boost (boost) converter or for a USB-C port protection IC, so
    the board's most interesting circuit (the feedback and compensation network, the inductor
    and the output capacitors of the TPS55288) is wired and proved equal but **not analysed**;
  - there is no NTC thermistor model (the dividers' currents are not seen), and the `Fuse` and
    `Capacitor` generics have no column for the fuse's I2t or the electrolytic's ESR;
  - a pin's own recommended window (EN, CC1c, VBAT) can only be stated through a `Rail`.

### The nets (85, 26 of them one-pin)

The supplies: `GND` (92 pads), `+5V` (the slot's logic supply, to U2, U5, the connector and the
LED), `+3.3V` (U2's output: the MCU, the I2C pull-ups, the comparator and the thermistor
dividers), `/VIN_PREFUSE` (the slot's A2 to A7) behind the 8 A fuse F1 to `/VIN`, then L1/C-filter
to `/EMI_VIN` (the converter's input), `/SW1` and `/SW2` (the converter's switch nodes, L2
between), `/VOUT_CONV` (its output, behind the shunt R28 to `/VOUT`), `/VBUS_SRC` and `/VBUSf`
(the two series MOSFETs of the port switch, and the shunt R27) and `/VBUS` (the receptacle).
The signals: I2C (`/SCL`, `/SDA` from the slot and `/USB_SCL`, `/USB_SDA` to the converter and
the protector), `/EN`, `/~{ALERT}`, `/~{RST}`, `/SWDIO`, `/SWCLK`, `/USART_TX`, `/USART_RX`, the
CC lines (`/CC1`, `/CC2` to U5, `/UCPD_CC1`, `/UCPD_CC2` to the MCU), `/TCPP_EN`, `/~{FLG}`,
`/CONVERTER_FLT`, the analog sense lines (`/VBUS_SENSE`, `/VOUT_SENSE`, `/IANA`, `/NTC_CONV`,
`/NTC_PLUG`) and the comparator's references (`/OVP_REF`, `/OVP_CEIL`). The generated names are
`Net-(U3-...)` for the converter's feedback, compensation, frequency and current-limit pins.
The pins left open (26 nets of one pad): 18 of the MCU's, the converter's CDC, four of USBLC6's
(I/O1 pin 6, I/O2 pins 3 and 4 and VBUS pin 5), the receptacle's SBU1 and SBU2 and the programming
connector's pin 6.

### What the language has and lacks (to be settled in the next stages)

- **Has:** resistor, capacitor, LED, inductor, ferrite bead, fuse, Schottky, TVS, N-channel
  MOSFET, linear regulator, the standard catalogs for 0402 to 1210 passives, a USB-C sink
  (`UsbCSink`) with a receptacle provider, a programming pads connector (`ProgrammingPads6`, six pads
  in a footprint, the generic form of a Tag-Connect cable's), a test point.
- **Lacks:** a thermistor kind, a buck-boost or a boost converter interface (the standard
  `Buck` is the only converter), a comparator, a USB-PD controller or port protector, a
  microcontroller with a pin table of this size, a PCIe card-edge connector, an electrolytic
  capacitor row with the ESR column the converter's output capacitor needs.
- **Risks, from the research of the issue:** the 80-part guide size; custom footprints (the
  GT-USB-7004 receptacle, VQFN-HR-26, QFN-18 with a 2.2 mm pad, the PCIe fingers); a long thin
  outline; a board designed for a 24 V backplane (its input is 24 V and the converter's output can
  reach 21 V).

Gaps found while porting are recorded in the stage that finds them.
